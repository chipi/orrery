The sphere in front of you is the Moon. Every yellow marker is a place a spacecraft has been. Drag to rotate. Zoom in to read the labels. Some of those markers are old. Luna 9, in 1966, was the first soft landing on another world. Six Apollo crewed landings, between 1969 and 1972, sit in a cluster on the visible near side. Tranquillity Base. Ocean of Storms. Fra Mauro. Hadley Rille. Descartes. Taurus-Littrow. Some markers are new. Chang'e 4 landed on the lunar far side in 2019 — the first soft landing there in human history, and the first to require a relay satellite, Queqiao, to get its signal home. Chandrayaan-3 placed the Vikram lander near the south pole in 2023 — the first soft landing in that part of the Moon, where craters in permanent shadow may hold water ice. Click any marker. The right panel opens with the mission's record — operating agency, lander type, crew if any, scientific instruments, surface duration, gallery of images. Most of the Apollo missions carry the lunar module model — descent stage on the surface, ascent stage either present or already gone, depending on the mission. The robotic missions carry their own per-mission three-D models — Luna 9's petal-capsule, Lunokhod 1's bathtub-on-wheels, Yutu-2's solar-paneled silhouette. Look at the cluster around the equator. That's where the Apollo program landed because the trajectories were cheapest there — direct ascents from Cape Kennedy, free-return geometry, navigation simplest near zero degrees latitude. Look at the south pole. That's where Artemis is going next. Different geometry, different fuel cost, different scientific motivation — water ice, permanently shadowed craters, the long-term lunar base argument. Use the filter chips at the top to show only crewed landings, only lander-rovers, only sample-return missions, only failures. Cross-check the missions you remember against the missions you didn't know about. Twelve people have walked on this surface. Eleven robotic landers have made it down softly since the last Apollo. At this moment, three rovers — Yutu-2, the Chandrayaan-3 Pragyan rover, and another mission whose name will appear here once it lands — are or recently were operating on the surface in front of you.
