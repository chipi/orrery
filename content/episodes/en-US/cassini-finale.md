<speak>
  Cassini launched in October 1997. <break time="500ms"/>
  It took almost seven years to reach Saturn — flybys of Venus, Venus again,
  Earth, and Jupiter built up the energy needed for the outer-system trip.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  It arrived in July 2004.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Cassini spent the next thirteen years in orbit around Saturn. <break time="500ms"/>
  It dropped the Huygens probe onto Titan in January 2005 — the first soft
  landing on a moon of another planet.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  It discovered that Enceladus, Saturn's small icy moon, shoots geysers of
  water vapor from its south pole. <break time="500ms"/>
  Underneath that ice is a liquid ocean. <break time="500ms"/>
  Possibly with life-supporting chemistry.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  By 2017, Cassini was running low on fuel. <break time="500ms"/>
  And the mission team faced a problem: <break time="400ms"/>
  if they let the spacecraft drift, it might eventually crash into Enceladus
  or Titan — and contaminate worlds that, by then, were leading candidates
  for finding life beyond Earth.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  So they planned the Grand Finale. <break time="500ms"/>
  Twenty-two orbits, each one threading the gap between Saturn's cloud tops
  and the inner edge of its rings — a corridor about two thousand four
  hundred kilometers wide.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  No spacecraft had ever flown through it. <break time="400ms"/>
  Each orbit took the spacecraft closer to the planet's surface than the last.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  On September 15th, 2017, Cassini fired its thrusters one final time to
  hold its antenna toward Earth, and dove into Saturn's upper atmosphere.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  It transmitted live data — atmospheric composition, magnetic field, radio
  signatures of dust — until the radio link broke.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Then it disintegrated.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  No spacecraft has been at Saturn since.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The next planned arrival, Dragonfly to Titan, will launch in 2028.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Cassini was twenty years between its launch and its end.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Most of what you see on Saturn in this orrery — the rings, the moons, the
  planet's interior structure — is what Cassini learned.
</speak>
