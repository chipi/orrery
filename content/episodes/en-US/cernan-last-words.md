<speak>
  Look at this map of the Moon. <break time="500ms"/>
  Every yellow marker is a place humans have been, or robots from Earth have been.
  <break time="600ms"/>

  Twelve of those markers were people.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Tranquillity Base. <break time="200ms"/>
  Ocean of Storms. <break time="200ms"/>
  Fra Mauro. <break time="200ms"/>
  Hadley Rille. <break time="200ms"/>
  Descartes Highlands. <break time="200ms"/>
  Taurus-Littrow.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  Six Apollo landing sites, between July 1969 and December 1972.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Three and a half years from first footstep to last footstep.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Gene Cernan was the commander of Apollo 17. <break time="500ms"/>
  He was the eleventh person to walk on the Moon, and the second-to-last back into the lunar module.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The very last person was Cernan himself — <break time="300ms"/> he was the commander,
  so he climbed up the ladder behind his crewmate, took one last look at the surface,
  <break time="500ms"/> and then he said this:

  <break time="1000ms"/>

  <prosody rate="82%" pitch="-1st">
    "As we leave the Moon at Taurus-Littrow, <break time="400ms"/>
    we leave as we came, <break time="300ms"/> and, God willing, as we shall return —
    <break time="400ms"/> with peace, and hope for all mankind."
    <break time="700ms"/>
    "Godspeed the crew of Apollo Seventeen."
  </prosody>

  <break time="1200ms"/>

  That was December 14th, 1972.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  Gene Cernan died in 2017, having lived almost his entire adult life as
  <emphasis level="strong">the most recent person to set foot on another world</emphasis>.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  As of the moment you are listening to this, <break time="300ms"/> more than fifty years have passed.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  In that half century, three Mars rovers have been built and sent.
  <break time="400ms"/> Two space stations have been continuously occupied.
  <break time="400ms"/> One probe has gone <emphasis level="moderate">interstellar</emphasis>.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  And nobody has been back to the Moon.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Artemis II is in flight planning. <break time="400ms"/>
  If it flies on schedule, Cernan's title — last person on the Moon — will pass to someone else.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  Until that happens, every time you look at this map, twelve markers represent a closed chapter.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  A chapter we are still trying to open again.
</speak>
