<speak>
  Curiosity drives at a top speed of about <emphasis level="moderate">four centimeters per second</emphasis>.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  A baby crawls faster.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  In a single sol — a Martian day — Curiosity covers an average of about
  <emphasis level="moderate">thirty meters</emphasis>.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Some days, much less. <break time="300ms"/>
  Some days, none — <break time="400ms"/> it stops, studies a rock with its laser, drills a hole,
  scans the dust.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  On bad days, the rover trips a fault, halts itself, waits to be told what went wrong.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Now do the arithmetic.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Curiosity landed in August 2012. <break time="500ms"/>
  As of now, it has been on Mars for <emphasis level="moderate">over four thousand sols</emphasis>.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  In that time, it has driven about <emphasis level="moderate">thirty-five kilometers</emphasis>.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  <emphasis level="strong">Thirty-five kilometers.</emphasis>
  <break time="500ms"/>
  A morning's bike ride.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  Stretched across the surface of another planet over twelve years.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Alone.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Every single sol of those twelve years, Curiosity has woken up at Martian sunrise.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Checked its temperature. <break time="300ms"/>
  Checked its battery. <break time="300ms"/>
  Listened for instructions that took fourteen minutes to arrive.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Then executed them. <break time="500ms"/>
  Then slept.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  There has been no day since August 6th, 2012, on which Curiosity did not exist alone on Mars.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  No relief shift. <break time="200ms"/>
  No vacation. <break time="200ms"/>
  No partner — <break time="400ms"/> until Perseverance arrived in 2021, more than three thousand
  kilometers away, on the other side of the planet.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  The car-sized robot you see on this map has been driving slower than a crawling child for longer
  than a child takes to grow into an adolescent.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  Every photograph it sends back is from a place no other being has ever stood.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Every datapoint it returns is one humans on Earth could not gather without it.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  This is what persistence looks like at four centimeters per second.
</speak>
