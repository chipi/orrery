<speak>
  The Moon does something strange. <break time="500ms"/>
  It rotates on its axis at exactly the same rate that it orbits the Earth.
  <break time="500ms"/>

  That means one side of the Moon always faces us. <break time="400ms"/>
  The other side — the far side — is permanently turned away.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  No human eye saw the far side of the Moon until October 7th, 1959.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Luna 3, a Soviet probe, returned the first photographs.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  They were grainy. <break time="200ms"/> Low contrast. <break time="500ms"/>
  And they showed something nobody had predicted: <break time="400ms"/>
  the far side looks <emphasis level="moderate">different</emphasis>.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Fewer of the dark plains — the maria — that dominate the near side.
  <break time="400ms"/> More cratered. <break time="300ms"/> Older. <break time="300ms"/> Rougher.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  No one has been there. <break time="500ms"/>
  Twelve people have stood on the Moon. <break time="400ms"/>
  All twelve stood on the near side.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  You can rotate this map and see the far side, but until the late 2010s, almost no spacecraft had
  even landed there.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Then, in January 2019, <emphasis level="strong">Chang'e 4</emphasis> made the first soft landing
  on the far side, inside the Von Kármán crater.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  To do this, China first had to launch a separate spacecraft —
  <emphasis level="moderate">Queqiao</emphasis>, the magpie bridge — into a halo orbit around the
  Earth-Moon L2 Lagrange point beyond the Moon.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Queqiao is the relay. <break time="400ms"/>
  Without it, a lander on the far side has no way to talk to Earth. <break time="500ms"/>
  The Moon is in the way.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  The Yutu-2 rover has been driving inside Von Kármán crater since that landing.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  It moves slowly. <break time="300ms"/>
  Sleeps through the fourteen-day lunar night. <break time="300ms"/>
  Wakes to study the regolith. <break time="300ms"/>
  Reports home through the magpie bridge.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Most of the far side is still untouched.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  When the next crewed mission goes to the Moon, it will probably land on the near side — Artemis
  has chosen the south pole.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Sometime after that, somebody will set foot on the far side for the first time.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Imagine the photograph they'll send back. <break time="500ms"/>
  Look at this map and pick the spot.
</speak>
