<speak>
  This grid is the Spaceflight Fleet. <break time="500ms"/>
  One hundred thirty-seven hardware entries — every rocket, every crewed
  spacecraft, every rover, every space station, every observatory, every
  cargo vehicle ever flown or seriously planned.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  The catalogue is organized into nine categories along the left.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Launchers. <break time="200ms"/>
  Crewed spacecraft. <break time="200ms"/>
  Cargo spacecraft. <break time="200ms"/>
  Stations. <break time="200ms"/>
  Rovers. <break time="200ms"/>
  Landers. <break time="200ms"/>
  Orbiters. <break time="200ms"/>
  Observatories. <break time="200ms"/>
  Concept vehicles.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  Click any category to filter the grid.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Try crewed spacecraft. <break time="500ms"/>
  You'll see Vostok at the top — Yuri Gagarin's vehicle, the first time a
  human flew into space.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Then Voskhod. <break time="300ms"/> Mercury. <break time="200ms"/>
  Gemini. <break time="200ms"/> Apollo CSM. <break time="200ms"/>
  Soyuz, in its many generations — Seven K, T, TM, TMA, MS.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Then Shuttle Orbiter, Shenzhou, Crew Dragon, Starliner, Orion, Gaganyaan.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  Six decades of human spaceflight engineering, lined up across one row.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Each Soyuz generation distinguishable from the last. <break time="500ms"/>
  Click any card to read what changed between generations.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Click Saturn V. <break time="500ms"/>
  The right panel opens with the spacecraft's anatomy — every stage, every
  engine, drawn as a hand-authored SVG diagram.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The flights tab lists thirteen Apollo missions Saturn V launched, with
  every crew name, every payload, every mission's outcome.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The links tab points to engineering reports, oral histories, and the
  Marshall Space Flight Center archive.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Try filtering by status. <break time="500ms"/>
  Failed. <break time="400ms"/>
  You'll see N-1. <break time="200ms"/>
  Schiaparelli. <break time="200ms"/>
  Beresheet. <break time="200ms"/>
  Hakuto-R. <break time="200ms"/>
  Vikram from Chandrayaan-2. <break time="200ms"/>
  Mars 2. <break time="200ms"/>
  Hitomi.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Failed missions are paired in this catalogue with their successors —
  the spacecraft that made it work.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Try the epoch timeline at the top. <break time="500ms"/>
  Drag the time chips to filter by era — Sputnik era, Mercury-Gemini-Vostok
  era, Apollo era, Shuttle era, ISS era, commercial era.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Compare what existed in 1965 to what existed in 1995 to what exists today.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Notice the cross-references. <break time="500ms"/>
  When you click a launcher, the panel lists every mission it ever flew.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  When you click a mission on the Missions screen, the panel links back to
  the launcher that put it up.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Hardware and missions reference each other in both directions —
  bidirectionally validated at build time.
