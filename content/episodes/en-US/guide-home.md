<speak>
  Welcome. <break time="500ms"/>
  An <emphasis level="moderate">orrery</emphasis> is a mechanical model of the
  solar system — brass, wood, and clockwork — built in the eighteenth century
  to show how the planets move around the Sun.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  This Orrery is the digital version of that idea. <break time="500ms"/>
  Real planetary positions, real orbital math, real missions — drawn on a
  screen instead of cast in metal.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Look at the cards on this page. <break time="400ms"/>
  Each one takes you to a different part of the story.
  <break time="700ms"/>

  <emphasis level="strong">Explore</emphasis> opens the whole solar system —
  eight planets in their orbits, real velocities, real distances.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  <emphasis level="strong">Earth</emphasis>, <emphasis level="strong">Moon</emphasis>,
  and <emphasis level="strong">Mars</emphasis> are the places we know best —
  each gets its own map with every spacecraft humanity has ever sent there.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  <emphasis level="strong">ISS</emphasis> and <emphasis level="strong">Tiangong</emphasis>
  are the two space stations currently being lived in — pickable, rotatable,
  module by module.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  <emphasis level="strong">Missions</emphasis> is the catalogue of forty-two flown
  and planned missions, sortable and filterable.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  <emphasis level="strong">Fleet</emphasis> shows the hardware itself — every rocket,
  every rover, every spacecraft, lined up by builder and era.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  <emphasis level="strong">Science</emphasis> is the in-app encyclopedia — every
  number you see anywhere else, with the physics that produced it.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  <emphasis level="strong">Plan</emphasis> and <emphasis level="strong">Fly</emphasis>
  let you design a transfer trajectory — Earth to Mars, for instance — and watch
  it play out in heliocentric scale.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Nothing on this site is made up. <break time="400ms"/>
  Every mission has a launch date, a crew list, a destination, and a citation.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Every image comes from a named agency with a named license.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Every link to read further points to a primary source, not an aggregator.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  This isn't a textbook. <break time="300ms"/> It's a working model of where
  humans have been in space — and what we know now because someone went there.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Take any card. <break time="400ms"/> Take the tour. <break time="500ms"/>
  When you find something that doesn't make sense, the tiny <emphasis level="moderate">question-mark
  chips</emphasis> open the Science encyclopedia at the right page.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  When you find something beautiful, look for the share button — every view in
  Orrery has a URL you can send to someone.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Welcome to the orrery. <break time="500ms"/> Pick a card.
</speak>
