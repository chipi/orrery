<speak>
  This is Mars. <break time="500ms"/>
  Every marker on the surface is the resting place of a spacecraft humans
  built on Earth and sent here.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Mariner 4 flew by in 1965. <break time="400ms"/>
  It took the first close-up photographs of any other planet.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Mars 2, a Soviet lander, crashed here in 1971. <break time="400ms"/>
  Mars 3 made the first soft landing — but transmitted for only twenty seconds.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Viking 1 and Viking 2, in 1976, took the first photographs from the surface.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Click Pathfinder's marker, in Ares Vallis. <break time="500ms"/>
  Pathfinder landed in 1997. <break time="400ms"/>
  It carried Sojourner — about the size of a microwave oven — the first wheels
  to ever drive on another planet.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Sojourner drove about a hundred meters in total.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Then the lander died, and Sojourner with it.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Click Curiosity, in Gale Crater. <break time="500ms"/>
  Curiosity landed in 2012. <break time="400ms"/>
  It is the size of a small car. <break time="400ms"/>
  It has driven over thirty kilometers across the floor of Gale Crater and up
  the lower slopes of Mount Sharp — and as of this moment, it is still moving.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Click Perseverance, in Jezero Crater. <break time="500ms"/>
  Perseverance is Curiosity's cousin — same chassis design, different
  instruments, different mission. <break time="400ms"/>
  It carries the first <emphasis level="moderate">helicopter</emphasis> ever
  flown on another planet, Ingenuity. <break time="500ms"/>
  Ingenuity made seventy-two flights before it broke a rotor blade in early
  2024.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Look at the orbiters around Mars. <break time="500ms"/>
  Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter. <break time="200ms"/>
  Mars Express. <break time="200ms"/>
  MAVEN. <break time="200ms"/>
  Mars Odyssey. <break time="200ms"/>
  ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter. <break time="200ms"/>
  Mangalyaan from India. <break time="200ms"/>
  Hope from the United Arab Emirates. <break time="200ms"/>
  Tianwen-1 from China.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  Eleven orbiters work, or recently worked, in the Martian sky. <break time="500ms"/>
  They photograph the surface, sniff the atmosphere, relay signals from the
  rovers, watch the dust storms.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Click any surface marker and the right panel opens with the mission's
  history, science return, and gallery. <break time="500ms"/>
  Where a rover has driven, the traverse is drawn on the map as a thin line.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Zoom in to Curiosity in Gale Crater to see its full route since 2012 —
  every sol's drive recorded as a faint trace across the plain.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Twenty surface missions have arrived here over sixty years.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Most of them are still here — silent, but there.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Look at the map. <break time="400ms"/>
  Read the dates. <break time="500ms"/>
  Notice how Mars has slowly stopped being a place humans only fly past.
</speak>
