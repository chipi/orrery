<speak>
  The grid in front of you is the Mission Catalogue. <break time="500ms"/>
  Forty-two missions, from the first robotic probes of the 1960s to the
  crewed flights planned for next decade.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  Every card has a launch date, a destination, an operating agency, a status,
  and a one-sentence description.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Use the filters at the top. <break time="400ms"/>
  Filter by <emphasis level="moderate">status</emphasis> — flown, active,
  planned, concept. <break time="400ms"/>
  Filter by <emphasis level="moderate">destination</emphasis> — Moon, Mars,
  Venus, outer planets, deep space.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Filter by <emphasis level="moderate">agency</emphasis> — NASA, ESA, JAXA,
  ISRO, CNSA, Roscosmos, SpaceX, SpaceIL.

  <break time="700ms"/>

  Try this combination. <break time="500ms"/>
  Filter status equals failed. <break time="500ms"/>
  You'll see Mars 2. <break time="200ms"/>
  Schiaparelli. <break time="200ms"/>
  Beresheet. <break time="200ms"/>
  Hakuto-R. <break time="200ms"/>
  Vikram from Chandrayaan-2. <break time="200ms"/>
  Hitomi.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  This is the redemption-arc layer of spaceflight. <break time="500ms"/>
  Every one of these missions is paired in the catalogue with its successor.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Vikram CY-2 failed in 2019. <break time="400ms"/> Chandrayaan-3 landed Vikram's
  twin at the lunar south pole in 2023.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Beresheet hit the surface in 2019. <break time="400ms"/> Beresheet 2 is in
  development. <break time="500ms"/>
  Apollo 1 killed three astronauts on the launch pad in 1967. <break time="400ms"/>
  Apollo 11 walked on the Moon two and a half years later.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Click any mission card. <break time="500ms"/>
  The right panel opens with the full record — launch vehicle, payload mass,
  crew or instruments, key events, gallery of images, links to read further.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Where the mission has a destination on a planetary surface, the
  <emphasis level="moderate">explore button</emphasis> jumps you to that surface
  with the landing site already selected.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Look at the timeline. <break time="500ms"/>
  Notice the gaps. <break time="500ms"/>
  Between Apollo 17 in 1972 and Luna 24 in 1976, the Moon was visited four times.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Then nothing landed there for nearly thirty years.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Then Chang'e 1 in 2007 reopened the visit list, and the line is now thicker
  than it has ever been.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Forty-two missions is not the whole story. <break time="400ms"/>
  Every entry on this page enables the next entry — the capability ladder.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Filter, click, read. <break time="500ms"/>
  See where you stand on the ladder.
</speak>
