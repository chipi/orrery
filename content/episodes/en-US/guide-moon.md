<speak>
  The sphere in front of you is the Moon. <break time="500ms"/>
  Every yellow marker is a place a spacecraft has been.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  Drag to rotate. <break time="300ms"/> Zoom in to read the labels.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Some of those markers are old. <break time="500ms"/>
  Luna 9, in 1966, was the first soft landing on another world.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Six Apollo crewed landings, between 1969 and 1972, sit in a cluster on
  the visible near side.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Tranquillity Base. <break time="200ms"/>
  Ocean of Storms. <break time="200ms"/>
  Fra Mauro. <break time="200ms"/>
  Hadley Rille. <break time="200ms"/>
  Descartes. <break time="200ms"/>
  Taurus-Littrow.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Some markers are new. <break time="500ms"/>
  Chang'e 4 landed on the lunar far side in 2019 — the first soft landing
  there in human history, and the first to require a relay satellite,
  Queqiao, to get its signal home.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Chandrayaan-3 placed the Vikram lander near the south pole in 2023 — the
  first soft landing in that part of the Moon, where craters in permanent
  shadow may hold water ice.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Click any marker. <break time="500ms"/>
  The right panel opens with the mission's record — operating agency, lander
  type, crew if any, scientific instruments, surface duration, gallery of
  images. <break time="500ms"/>
  Most of the Apollo missions carry the lunar module model — descent stage on
  the surface, ascent stage either present or already gone, depending on the
  mission. <break time="500ms"/>
  The robotic missions carry their own per-mission three-D models — Luna 9's
  petal-capsule, Lunokhod 1's bathtub-on-wheels, Yutu-2's solar-paneled
  silhouette.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Look at the cluster around the equator. <break time="500ms"/>
  That's where the Apollo program landed because the trajectories were
  cheapest there — direct ascents from Cape Kennedy, free-return geometry,
  navigation simplest near zero degrees latitude.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Look at the south pole. <break time="400ms"/>
  That's where Artemis is going next. <break time="500ms"/>
  Different geometry, different fuel cost, different scientific motivation —
  water ice, permanently shadowed craters, the long-term lunar base argument.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Use the filter chips at the top to show only crewed landings, only
  lander-rovers, only sample-return missions, only failures.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Cross-check the missions you remember against the missions you didn't know
  about.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Twelve people have walked on this surface. <break time="500ms"/>
  Eleven robotic landers have made it down softly since the last Apollo.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  At this moment, three rovers — Yutu-2, the Chandrayaan-3 Pragyan rover, and
  another mission whose name will appear here once it lands — are or recently
  were operating on the surface in front of you. <break time="500ms"/>
  Look at the map. <break time="400ms"/>
  See how recent most of the activity is.
</speak>
