<speak>
  The T-shape in front of you is Tiangong — in Chinese, 天宫,
  <emphasis level="moderate">Heavenly Palace</emphasis>.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  It is the second continuously-crewed space station ever flown.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  It is operated by the China Manned Space Engineering Office, and it has
  been continuously occupied since June 2022.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Drag to rotate. <break time="400ms"/>
  You'll see three modules. <break time="500ms"/>
  The core of the T is <emphasis level="moderate">Tianhe</emphasis> — meaning
  Harmony of the Heavens — launched in April 2021 on a Long March 5B rocket.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Tianhe holds the crew quarters, the life-support systems, the control
  cabin, and the docking ports.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Click Wentian — the arm to one side. <break time="500ms"/>
  Wentian — Quest for the Heavens — is the first experimental laboratory,
  launched in July 2022. <break time="400ms"/>
  It houses life-science experiments and the airlock used for spacewalks.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Click Mengtian — the arm to the other side. <break time="500ms"/>
  Mengtian — Dreaming of the Heavens — is the second laboratory, launched in
  October 2022. <break time="400ms"/>
  It carries physics experiments and a cold-atom clock for fundamental
  research.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Look at the solar arrays. <break time="500ms"/>
  Four large panels, two at each lab module's far end. <break time="400ms"/>
  Together they produce about <emphasis level="moderate">one hundred
  kilowatts</emphasis> of electrical power.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Look at the docking ports on the front of Tianhe. <break time="500ms"/>
  Crews arrive on Shenzhou spacecraft — the Chinese crewed vehicle, derived
  in part from Soyuz but with a domestically developed re-entry capsule.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Cargo arrives on Tianzhou ships — the Chinese resupply vehicles.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Tiangong was assembled in seventeen months. <break time="500ms"/>
  By comparison, the International Space Station was assembled over twelve
  years. <break time="400ms"/>
  Tiangong is smaller — about a sixth the mass of the ISS — but it was built
  on a much tighter timeline by a single agency.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Click any module. <break time="500ms"/>
  The right panel opens with its builder, launch vehicle, launch date, and
  pressurized volume.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Three modules. <break time="400ms"/>
  One agency. <break time="400ms"/>
  Continuously crewed for four years.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Most people who ask "is there anyone else in space" don't yet know to
  include the answer: yes, also here.
</speak>
