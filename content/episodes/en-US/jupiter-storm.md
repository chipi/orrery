<speak>
  Click Jupiter. <break time="500ms"/>
  Find the Great Red Spot — the oval, roughly the size of Earth, in the
  southern hemisphere.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Now stop and consider what you're looking at. <break time="500ms"/>
  That is a single storm system. <break time="500ms"/>
  It has been there, continuously, for at least three hundred and fifty
  years.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Astronomers first noted it through telescopes in 1665. <break time="400ms"/>
  It was a storm then. <break time="400ms"/> It is a storm now. <break time="500ms"/>
  Whatever combination of fluid dynamics, atmospheric chemistry, and Jupiter's
  rapid rotation sustains it has been keeping it spinning continuously since
  before the discovery of Newton's laws of motion.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Jupiter rotates once every nine hours and fifty-five minutes. <break time="500ms"/>
  Faster than any other planet in the Solar System. <break time="400ms"/>
  That rotation drives winds in the Great Red Spot that reach
  <emphasis level="moderate">four hundred and thirty kilometers per hour</emphasis>
  at its outer rim.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Triple the strongest Earth hurricane.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  The spot has been shrinking. <break time="400ms"/>
  In 1879 it was forty thousand kilometers across.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Today it is around fifteen thousand. <break time="500ms"/>
  Whether it eventually closes — or whether something keeps reseeding it
  out of the bands of upwelling gas around it — is one of the open questions
  Juno was sent to answer.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Juno arrived in 2016. <break time="400ms"/>
  Its orbit takes it over Jupiter's poles — a polar orbit, a frame no
  previous Jupiter mission used — so it can map the planet's magnetic
  field, atmospheric chemistry, and the interior structure below the cloud
  tops.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Photographs of the Great Red Spot from Juno are some of the most detailed
  ever taken from any spacecraft.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Earth's worst hurricane lasts a few weeks.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Jupiter has had the same hurricane for three and a half centuries.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Look at it. <break time="500ms"/>
  Then notice the scale bar. <break time="500ms"/>
  Then breathe.
</speak>
