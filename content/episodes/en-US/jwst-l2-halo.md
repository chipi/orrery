<speak>
  Look at the diagram. <break time="500ms"/>
  At the top, far above Earth's orbit, is the
  <emphasis level="moderate">James Webb Space Telescope</emphasis>.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Not in orbit around Earth. <break time="400ms"/>
  Around a point in space that doesn't exist in any material sense.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The second <emphasis level="moderate">Sun-Earth Lagrange point</emphasis> —
  L2.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Imagine a line from the Sun, through Earth, extending outward.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  One and a half million kilometers past Earth, in that exact direction,
  the combined gravity of the Sun and the Earth, plus the centripetal force
  of orbiting the Sun at Earth's pace, all balance out.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  A spacecraft placed there moves at exactly the same angular speed as
  Earth — completes one orbit per year — without burning fuel to hold
  station.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  But L2 is an unstable saddle point. <break time="400ms"/>
  Drift left or right, and gravity pulls you further away.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  So JWST doesn't sit at L2 — it orbits around L2, in a slow loop that
  takes about six months per revolution.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  A <emphasis level="moderate">halo orbit.</emphasis>
  <break time="400ms"/>
  It never crosses the L2 point exactly; it traces a wide ellipse around it.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Why L2 specifically? <break time="500ms"/>
  Two reasons. <break time="400ms"/>
  First — Earth sits between JWST and the Sun, almost continuously.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  The telescope's enormous sunshield, the size of a tennis court,
  permanently blocks sunlight and the heat radiating from the Earth and
  the Moon.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  JWST operates at <emphasis level="moderate">forty Kelvin</emphasis> —
  about minus two hundred and thirty Celsius — because the heat-shield
  geometry never lets the cold side warm up.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Second — by orbiting the Sun at Earth's pace, JWST stays close enough
  for high-bandwidth radio links, but far enough that the Earth's thermal
  emissions don't blind its infrared detectors.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Hubble lives in low Earth orbit, five hundred fifty kilometers up,
  because it observes in visible and ultraviolet light.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  JWST lives at L2, one and a half million kilometers up, because it
  observes in the infrared.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Different problem. <break time="300ms"/> Different orbit. <break time="300ms"/>
  Same universe.
</speak>
