<speak>
  This chart is called a porkchop plot. <break time="500ms"/> It looks like a folded pork chop.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  It is the most efficient way ever invented to argue with the solar system about when to launch.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Every contour line is a constant amount of energy required to go from Earth to Mars.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The horizontal axis is the launch date — when you leave. <break time="400ms"/>
  The vertical axis is the arrival date — when you get there.

  <break time="700ms"/>

  At the bottom of the C, energy is lowest. <break time="400ms"/> That's the cheap window.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The further out you push from the bottom of the C, the more fuel you need.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  The more fuel you need, the heavier the rocket. <break time="400ms"/>
  The heavier the rocket, the more expensive everything else gets.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Mars's cheap window opens once every <emphasis level="moderate">twenty-six months</emphasis>.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Twenty-six months because that's how long it takes for Earth — which orbits the Sun once a year —
  and Mars — which orbits once every one point eight eight years — to line up in the geometry
  where a transfer ellipse from one to the other is shortest.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Look at the chart. <break time="300ms"/> Trace the bottom of the C. <break time="500ms"/>
  Notice how it bends. <break time="400ms"/> The cheapest day to leave isn't a single instant —
  it's a window two or three weeks wide.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Now look at the next opening, twenty-six months later. The C-shape is in the same place, but the
  energy values are different. <break time="400ms"/> Because Mars's orbit isn't a perfect circle.
  Some launch windows are cheap. <break time="300ms"/> Some are <emphasis level="moderate">very</emphasis> cheap.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Mariner 4 in 1964. <break time="200ms"/>
  Viking in 1975. <break time="200ms"/>
  Pathfinder in 1996. <break time="200ms"/>
  Curiosity in 2011. <break time="200ms"/>
  Perseverance in 2020.
  <break time="700ms"/>
  Every Mars launch in history sits on the bottom of one of these C-shapes.
  <break time="600ms"/>

  The solar system doesn't care what you can build. <break time="400ms"/>
  It cares what date your launch pad is open. <break time="500ms"/>
  The porkchop plot tells you which dates the solar system has agreed to.
</speak>
