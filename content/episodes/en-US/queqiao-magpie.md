<speak>
  Before Chang'e 4 could land on the far side of the Moon, China had to
  solve a basic communication problem.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The far side of the Moon never faces Earth. <break time="400ms"/>
  A spacecraft on the surface there has no direct radio path to anywhere
  on this planet.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The Moon itself is in the way.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  The solution was launched in May 2018. <break time="500ms"/>
  A relay satellite called <emphasis level="moderate">Queqiao</emphasis> —
  pronounced cheh-chee-ow — Chinese for
  <emphasis level="moderate">magpie bridge</emphasis>.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Queqiao orbits the Earth-Moon L2 Lagrange point — a different L2 from
  the Sun-Earth L2 where JWST lives. <break time="500ms"/>
  This one sits sixty-five thousand kilometers beyond the Moon, along the
  Earth-Moon line.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  From that position, Queqiao has line-of-sight to both Earth and the
  Moon's far side simultaneously.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  It is the relay that forwards every signal from Chang'e 4 and the Yutu-2
  rover back to ground stations on Earth.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Why is it called the magpie bridge? <break time="500ms"/>
  In Chinese folklore, there are two lovers — a weaver girl and a cowherd —
  separated permanently by the Milky Way, allowed to meet only once a
  year, on the seventh night of the seventh month of the lunar calendar.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  When that night comes, all the world's magpies fly up and form a bridge
  of feathers across the river of stars so the lovers can cross.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The lovers are the orbits of the Earth and the Moon.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Queqiao is the bridge.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Queqiao has been operating for over seven years now. <break time="500ms"/>
  Yutu-2 has been driving around the floor of the Von Kármán crater that
  whole time — over a kilometer of slow exploration, all of it transmitted
  home through the magpie bridge.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Chang'e 6 — which returned the first samples from the far side in 2024 —
  used Queqiao 2, a successor satellite, in the same halo orbit.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Every photograph the world has ever seen from the far side of the Moon
  arrived through this relay.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Without it, the far side stays exactly as silent as it was in 1959 —
  before Luna 3 first photographed it.
</speak>
