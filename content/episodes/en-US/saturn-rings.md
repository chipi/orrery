<speak>
  Click Saturn. <break time="500ms"/>
  Look at the rings. <break time="500ms"/>
  Now think about the numbers.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Saturn itself is about one hundred and twenty thousand kilometers across.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The main ring system stretches another two hundred and eighty thousand
  kilometers beyond the planet's edge.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  And those rings are, in places, only <emphasis level="moderate">ten meters thick.</emphasis>

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Ten meters. <break time="500ms"/>
  A two-story building.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  A disc the diameter of nearly the distance from the Earth to the Moon,
  and it's the depth of a small house.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  The rings are not solid. <break time="400ms"/>
  They are billions of pieces of water ice, most of them centimeter-sized,
  some meter-sized, a few house-sized.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Each piece is on its own Keplerian orbit. <break time="500ms"/>
  Pieces near the inner edge orbit Saturn in about five hours.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Pieces near the outer edge take fourteen.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  And then there are the gaps. <break time="500ms"/>
  The Cassini Division — three thousand kilometers of nearly empty space
  between the A ring and the B ring — was carved by the gravity of Mimas,
  one of Saturn's small inner moons.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Mimas is fifty thousand kilometers away. <break time="400ms"/>
  And yet, by orbital resonance — its two-to-one gravitational pull on any
  particle that tries to settle in that band — it has swept the Cassini
  Division clear.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  In September 2017, the Cassini spacecraft — having spent thirteen years
  in orbit around Saturn — was deliberately plunged into the planet's
  atmosphere. <break time="500ms"/>
  Its final twenty-two orbits, called the Grand Finale, took it weaving
  between the planet and the inner edge of the rings, photographing them
  from inside the gap.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Those photographs are what you see when you look at the rings on this screen.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Two hundred and eighty thousand kilometers wide. <break time="200ms"/>
  Ten meters thick. <break time="500ms"/>
  Read that again and let the ratio settle.
</speak>
