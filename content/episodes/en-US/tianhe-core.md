<speak>
  Tianhe — the core module of Tiangong — launched on April 29th, 2021.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Long March 5B rocket, Wenchang launch site, Hainan Island.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Tianhe carries the station's life support, attitude control, propulsion,
  three crew sleeping quarters, the main galley, and the forward docking
  ports.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Pressurized volume around fifty cubic meters — about half the volume of
  Zvezda, the analogous module at the back of the ISS.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Same function as Zvezda. <break time="300ms"/>
  Different generation. <break time="300ms"/>
  Different agency. <break time="300ms"/>
  Different rocket.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  The first crew, Shenzhou 12, arrived in June 2021 — six weeks after
  Tianhe reached orbit. <break time="500ms"/>
  Three astronauts. <break time="200ms"/>
  Three months. <break time="500ms"/>
  Since then, Tiangong has been continuously occupied.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  The first experimental lab — <emphasis level="moderate">Wentian</emphasis>,
  meaning "quest for the heavens" — joined Tianhe in July 2022.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  The second lab — <emphasis level="moderate">Mengtian</emphasis>, "dreaming
  of the heavens" — joined in October 2022.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Total assembly time, first module to fully crewed and fully assembled:
  <emphasis level="moderate">seventeen months.</emphasis>

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Compare to the International Space Station, which took twelve years and
  forty-two flights, across an entire decade of Space Shuttle and Soyuz
  launches.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Tiangong is smaller — about one sixth the ISS's mass.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  But it was built to a tighter timeline by a single agency on a single
  rocket family, optimized end-to-end for this one program.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  Look at the T-shape on screen. <break time="400ms"/>
  Tianhe is the upright. <break time="200ms"/>
  Wentian and Mengtian are the crossbar. <break time="400ms"/>
  Four solar arrays — two on each lab — provide about a hundred kilowatts.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Three Chinese astronauts live and work inside as you listen.
</speak>
