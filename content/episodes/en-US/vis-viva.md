<speak>
  Open the Orbits tab. <break time="500ms"/>
  Find the section called Vis-viva.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  What you're looking at is a single equation that determines the velocity
  of every spacecraft in this orrery, at every point along every orbit it
  flies.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  In symbols:
  <break time="400ms"/>
  v squared equals G times M, times the quantity two over r minus one over a.
  <break time="600ms"/>
  V for velocity. <break time="200ms"/>
  G for the gravitational constant. <break time="200ms"/>
  M for the mass of the central body — the Sun, for heliocentric orbits;
  Earth, for cislunar; the parent planet, for moons.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  R is the current distance from the central body.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  A is the orbit's semi-major axis — the average of the closest and furthest
  approaches.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  What it says, in plain words: <break time="500ms"/>
  <emphasis level="moderate">your velocity at any point in an orbit depends
  only on two things — where you are right now, and the size of the orbit
  you're in.</emphasis>

  <break time="900ms"/>

  When you're at perihelion — closest to the central body — r is small, so
  the bracket is large, so v is large. You move fast.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  When you're at aphelion — furthest out — r is large, the bracket is small,
  v is small. You move slowly.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Kepler's second law — equal areas in equal times — falls right out of
  this.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Mercury at perihelion moves at almost fifty-nine kilometers per second.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  At aphelion, only thirty-nine.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  Earth varies between twenty-nine and thirty-one. <break time="400ms"/>
  Halley's Comet at perihelion races past the Sun at fifty-five kilometers
  per second.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Out near Neptune, it crawls at one.

  <break time="900ms"/>

  Every transfer ellipse on the Plan screen is computed from this equation.
  <break time="400ms"/>
  Every porkchop contour. <break time="200ms"/>
  Every C three number. <break time="200ms"/>
  Every burn schedule for every mission you'll see fly on the Fly screen.

  <break time="800ms"/>

  One equation. <break time="300ms"/>
  Three variables. <break time="300ms"/>
  Six decades of spaceflight.
  <break time="500ms"/>
  This is what physics-first means.
</speak>
