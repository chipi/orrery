# ADR-092 — Analytics opt-out + browser privacy signals (GPC/DNT) + the /privacy disclosure page; no consent banner

> Status · Accepted · 2026-09-10 · Operator-directed (privacy audit of the Umami implementation) ·
> amends **ADR-081** (self-hosted Umami) · third narrow exception to the no-client-storage rule
> after **ADR-057** (locale) and **ADR-075** (tour resume) · the analytics half of the ladder in
> **ADR-082**

## Context

A privacy audit of the shipped Umami implementation (ADR-081) asked three questions: how much do we
actually track, do we need consent, and do we need an opt-out. The audit surfaced a gap between what
the code does and what the code (and the UI) claims.

**What is actually collected.** Umami's own autotrack records page, referrer, screen, language,
UA-derived browser/OS/device and IP-derived country per visit. On top of that sit **24 custom event
names across ~56 call sites in 18 files**: navigation *with per-route dwell time*
(`route-enter` / `route-exit`), click-level interaction on every route (`item-click` on markers,
stars, missions, modules), completion funnels (`mission-load` → `mission-complete`), entity
popularity, outbound link destinations, and free-text search.

That is **behavioural analytics, not audience measurement**, and the distinction is exactly what the
regulatory exemptions turn on. The volume was a deliberate product choice and stays — but it means
the "we just count pageviews" framing was never accurate, and the posture has to be defensible on
its own terms rather than by understating what is collected.

**Three specific defects.**

1. **`cmdk-search-hit` shipped the raw query.** `ScienceSearch.svelte` called
   `track('cmdk-search-hit', { query: query.toLowerCase().trim(), … })` with no truncation — while
   the sibling `trackSearch()` capped at 40 chars, `analytics.ts` documented the event as
   `{ query_len, … }`, and `/credits` promised users "collects no personal data". Whatever a user
   typed into the command palette landed verbatim in the dashboard. Three separate contracts, all
   contradicted by one call site.
2. **No way to object.** There was no opt-out, and `navigator.globalPrivacyControl` /
   `navigator.doNotTrack` were not consulted. The audience-measurement exemptions expect a means to
   object; we had none.
3. **Disclosure was one sentence.** GDPR Art. 13 transparency is mandatory whatever the lawful
   basis. The only user-facing text was `credits_storage_intro` on `/credits`, and there was no
   `/privacy` route at all (the layout's footer comments had reserved the slot since v0.7).

**On consent.** The ePrivacy Art. 5(3) trigger is *storing or accessing information on the user's
terminal equipment* — that is what makes cookie banners mandatory. Umami is cookieless and writes
nothing, so 5(3) is not engaged. GDPR still governs the processing itself (a daily-rotating IP+UA
hash is pseudonymous data), for which legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) is the applicable basis:
self-hosted, no third party, no ad tech, no cross-site identity, no profiling of individuals.
Legitimate interest requires transparency and a means to object — **not** consent.

## Decision

**No consent banner. An opt-out, honoured browser signals, and a real disclosure page instead.**

1. **Cap every free-text path.** New `trackSearchHit(query, tab, section)` in `analytics.ts` applies
   the same 40-char cap as `trackSearch` and emits `{ query_len, query, tab, section }`, matching the
   documented contract. `ScienceSearch.svelte` calls it instead of raw `track()`. The module header
   now states the rule explicitly: **every** free-text path goes through `trackSearch` /
   `trackSearchHit`; never pass a raw query to `track()`.

2. **A single suppression gate.** New `src/lib/analytics-optout.ts` exports `analyticsSuppressed()`
   — the OR of an explicit opt-out cookie and a browser privacy signal. `analyticsEnabled()` and
   `initAnalytics()` both consult it.

3. **`orrery_analytics_optout` — functional cookie #3.** Value `'1'`, `Max-Age` 1 year (mirrors
   `orrery_locale`), `SameSite=Lax`, `Secure` on https. Opting **back in deletes the cookie** rather
   than writing `'0'`, so the default state stores nothing at all and a user who never touches the
   toggle carries no cookie. Under ePrivacy this is a strictly-necessary cookie: it records the
   user's objection and exists only to be obeyed.

4. **GPC first, DNT alongside.** `navigator.globalPrivacyControl === true` suppresses. It is the
   signal with actual legal force — binding under CCPA/CPRA in California, recognised in Colorado
   and Connecticut. `navigator.doNotTrack === '1'` (plus the legacy `'yes'` / `window.doNotTrack` /
   `msDoNotTrack` spellings) also suppresses, but **DNT is not the reason this exists**: the W3C
   group closed in 2019 without a standard and Firefox dropped its toggle, so no jurisdiction
   requires it. It costs one expression to honour next to GPC. Building for DNT *alone* would have
   been the wrong target.

5. **Suppression happens before injection, not just at `track()`.** Umami's autotrack binds its own
   history listeners when the script loads and they cannot be unbound afterwards. An opted-out user
   therefore never receives the script at all, and the `/privacy` toggle **reloads the page** on
   change so that mid-session opt-out actually stops pageview collection rather than only our custom
   events.

6. **`/privacy` is the disclosure surface.** A prerendered route listing what is collected, what
   never is, where it goes, the cookies, crash reports, browser signals, and the toggle itself. It
   is linked from the footer ABOUT menu (the slot reserved for it since v0.7) and from the `/credits`
   storage card. `credits_storage_intro` changes "collects no personal data" → **"stores no personal
   data"**, which is the accurate claim: an IP is processed in memory to derive a country and then
   discarded.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- The "no consent banner" position is now defensible rather than asserted: cookieless + self-hosted
  + no sharing + **a working means to object** + real Art. 13 disclosure.
- One chokepoint. `analyticsSuppressed()` gates script injection and every event, so a future event
  cannot accidentally bypass the opt-out.
- The free-text leak is closed at the helper level, so the same mistake cannot recur by calling a
  helper — only by hand-rolling `track()` with a raw string, which the module header now forbids.
- GPC users are honoured automatically with no UI interaction at all.

**Negative**

- A third cookie. The no-client-storage rule (ADR-057) now has three exceptions. Each remains
  ADR-gated and none stores personal data, but the "we have two cookies" line in docs and the
  credits copy had to change, and the slope argument gets marginally weaker with each addition.
- The opt-out toggle reloads the page. Unavoidable given Umami's autotrack binding, and the copy
  says so, but it is a visible jolt.
- Opted-out and GPC users disappear from the dashboard entirely, so the numbers under-count real
  traffic by an unknown margin. That is the point, but it should not be read as a traffic drop.

**Neutral**

- No change to the event vocabulary, the env ladder (ADR-082), the mobile target switcher
  (ADR-083), or the edge vhost. Volume of tracking is unchanged by this ADR — it is disclosed and
  refusable, not reduced.
- Crash reports (ADR-067) follow the same gate conceptually and are described on `/privacy`.

## Alternatives considered

- **A consent banner.** Rejected: ePrivacy 5(3) is not engaged by cookieless analytics, so a banner
  would impose friction on every visitor for no legal gain, train users to click through consent
  dialogs, and imply the processing needs consent when legitimate interest applies. It would also be
  the single most visible change to a site whose whole design avoids chrome.
- **`localStorage` for the opt-out flag.** Rejected outright — ADR-057 bans client storage, and a
  privacy feature is the worst possible place to break that rule. A cookie is also the correct
  primitive: it is the narrowest, most inspectable, and user-clearable option.
- **DNT only.** Rejected. It is the intuitive request and the dead standard; GPC is the one with
  legal force. Honouring both costs nothing, so both are honoured — but the decision is
  GPC-primary.
- **Reducing what is tracked instead of disclosing it.** Considered and deferred. The dwell-time and
  click-level events earn their keep in product decisions. The honest fix is to say so plainly and
  let people refuse, not to quietly trim the list. Revisit if the dashboard shows events nobody
  reads.
- **`window.umami.disabled` / Umami's own opt-out.** Rejected: Umami's documented mechanism is a
  `localStorage` key, which ADR-057 forbids.

## Not covered / out of scope

- **Translations.** The `privacy_*` and `credits_storage_*` message keys ship **en-US only**; the
  other 13 locales fall back to en-US per the standard i18n rule. Running
  `scripts/translate-v07-ui.mjs --keys=…` requires an `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` that was not available in
  the implementing session. **Deferred, tracked as a follow-up.**
- **Server-side / edge-level suppression.** The gate is client-side. A user who blocks JS sends
  nothing anyway; there is no server-side pageview logging to gate.
- **A cookie-level "reject all" beyond analytics.** `orrery_locale` and `orrery_tour` remain
  strictly-functional and are not covered by the toggle.
- **Legal review.** This ADR records an engineering position reasoned from the structure of ePrivacy
  and GDPR. It is not legal advice, and the analysis shifts with the operator's establishment
  jurisdiction and audience mix.
