> Owner · Marko

> Builds on · #287 (planet-relative camera + 4K LOD + natural satellites + atmospheric halos)

> **Why this is a PRD.** Spans three subsystems: visual rendering (new 3D primitives + texture swaps + shader-ish overlays), data (fleet entries for active orbiters that aren't yet in `/fleet/orbiter/`, plus new "active-around-planet" relations), and UX (decision matrix for what's "always-on detail" vs what's gated by the Science Lens vs what lives in the HUD/panel). One owner, one categorisation decision, one source of truth before per-feature slices start.

## What

After #287 the user can fly into a planet, get the 4K texture + atmospheric halo + moons. The view is beautiful but **passive** — it shows a textured sphere with moons, nothing more. There's no reason to *stay* zoomed in.

This PRD locks the spec for turning the planet-focus pose into a **destination** with story-rich layers a user would actually explore:

- **Always-on detail** — physical features that are simply *there* (mission spacecraft orbiting, ring shadows, axial tilt, city lights). No toggle.
- **Science Lens additions** — conceptual physics overlays gated on the existing lens toggle (Hill sphere, Lagrange points, magnetic field, sub-solar point).
- **HUD / info-panel additions** — text + UI affordances (light-time, scale comparison, planet stats, prograde indicator).

The full set spans ~10 distinct features. This PRD groups them, picks the marquee 4 for the first slice, and surfaces the fleet-data implications.

## The META decision

**Where does each feature belong — zoom-in detail, Science Lens, or HUD?**

The principle:

| Category | What belongs here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Always-on detail** | Things that *exist* in physical reality at the depicted moment | A user shouldn't have to toggle a layer to see that Jupiter has rings, that Mars has spacecraft around it, or that Earth has city lights. These are facts. |
| **Science Lens** | Conceptual physics that's *invisible to the eye* | Magnetic fields, Lagrange points, Hill spheres — you can't *see* these by looking at the planet. Lens makes the physics-of-the-system visible by user choice. |
| **HUD / panel** | Reference info, scale anchors, navigation cues | Light-time, gravity, "Earth diameter at this scale" — text + tiny UI affordances. Always-on text-y stuff, not 3D geometry. |

Each candidate feature classified below.

## Out of scope

- **Real-time spacecraft positions** — pinned orbits are fine for v1; live ephemerides are a separate initiative.
- **Surface-route changes** — /earth /moon /mars stay independent. /explore zoom-in is the only route touched. **[SUPERSEDED 2026-07-08]** — the Science Lens now extends to the surface routes, and per [#382](https://github.com/chipi/orrery/issues/382) the Tactical Scan HUD follows. See the amendment at the bottom of this doc.
- **Heliocentric view** — wide zoom unchanged. Only the planet-focus pose (below `PLANET_LOD_IN_RATIO`) gets the new layers.
- **i18n strings for the new HUD labels** — Slice E ships en-US; locale propagation is a separate slice once strings stabilise.

## Risks + open questions

1. **Crowding.** Each layer adds visual weight. The previous Jupiter-focus screenshot was already busy with moons + halo + arrows. Need a clear visibility hierarchy. → Mitigation: per-slice screenshots in PR descriptions; user-pref toggles in Slice E if needed.
2. **Fleet-data debt.** Backfilling orbital params for the missing orbiters is real work — Akatsuki, Hope, MAVEN, ExoMars TGO need accurate altitude + inclination. → Slice A spec includes the data audit as Step 0.
3. **Decision: do magnetic field + sub-solar lens layers belong in Slice D, or do they actually belong in Slice A?** Reasonable counter-argument: magnetic field is a *physical* thing that exists (Earth's aurora is the visible proof). → Keep in lens for v1; revisit after Slice A ships and we see how busy the focus view feels.

## Amendment — 2026-07-08: Science Lens + Tactical Scan unified across surface routes

**Reconciles a doc-vs-code divergence and supersedes this PRD's original "surface routes stay independent" non-goal.** Tracked by [#382](https://github.com/chipi/orrery/issues/382).

### What changed since the original draft

The original PRD scoped every new layer to `/explore`'s planet-focus pose and explicitly kept the surface routes out. That boundary no longer holds:

- **Geophysics lens layers already shipped to the surface routes** (commit `49d59e3168`). `src/lib/surface-scene/SurfaceScene.svelte` now mounts per-body Science Lens overlays via `earth-geophysics-layers.ts` / `earth-magnetosphere-layer.ts` / `moon-lens-layers.ts` / `mars-lens-layers.ts`:
  - **Earth** — `magnetosphere` · `axial-tilt` · `mag-north` · `tides` · `hydrosphere`
  - **Moon** — `sub-earth` · `far-side`
  - **Mars** — `axial-tilt` · `dead-dynamo` · `polar-caps` · `mars-moons`
- **The lens infrastructure was always cross-route** — `science-lens.ts` (master toggle) + `science-layers.ts` (per-layer `<html>` attributes) + `ScienceLayersPanel.svelte` (per-route `layers` prop) are shared by `/explore`, `/fly`, `/earth`, `/moon`, `/mars`, `/iss`, `/tiangong`. Only the *content decision* (which overlays live where) was `/explore`-scoped.

### New intent

The **Science Lens and the Tactical Scan HUD are one unified cross-route system.** Body-level physics and facts (fields, tilt, satellites, gravity, atmosphere) should read the same wherever a body appears — whether you fly to it in `/explore` or stand on its surface map in `/moon` / `/mars` / `/earth`.

### Chosen surface-HUD candidates (three slices — tracked in #382)

The full `/explore` opportunity set was assessed against surface fit; most of it stays `/explore`-only (see below). Three body-intrinsic HUD elements were selected to bring to `/moon` · `/mars` · `/earth`:

**Slice 1 — Tactical Scan (`planet-stats`).** The one body-level overlay still missing surface-side; renders only on `/explore` today (`src/routes/explore/+page.svelte`).
- Extract the inline `PlanetStats` type + `PLANET_STATS` record + `.tactical-scan` overlay out of `explore/+page.svelte` into a shared `$lib` module (data + a small component).
- Add a **Moon** stats entry (absent today — the Moon is a satellite; only `SATELLITE_STATS` diameter exists, not the full gravity/atmo/temp set).
- Render in `SurfaceScene`, gated by the existing `planet-stats` layer; add `'planet-stats'` to the `/moon` · `/mars` · `/earth` panel filter.
- Reuse the existing `explore_scan_*` i18n messages (already present in all locales — no new strings).

**Slice 2 — Sub-solar point + terminator (`sub-solar`).** Pulsing noon marker + sharpened day/night line — "the Sun is that way," which reads better standing on a surface than in orbit.
- Derive the marker from **real geometry** (body rotation + Sun direction at load), not merely the aesthetic render-light position — Orrery's scientific-honesty bar applies. The surface scenes already carry a directional Sun light, so the vector is available; the work is anchoring it to the true sub-solar longitude.

**Slice 3 — Light-time / distance HUD.** "Mars is 13 light-minutes away right now."
- **Net-new** — not built even on `/explore`, and the surface routes have no sim clock. Compute an instantaneous Earth↔body distance at load from the same real-J2000-anchored positions `/explore` uses (`keplerPos` propagated to today), render as a static readout. Heaviest of the three; lowest visual return.

**Assessed but not chosen:** Earth-comparison ghost (E.2) — weaker on a dedicated single-body route than in `/explore`'s planet lineup.

### Still out of scope (intent unchanged, restated for the surface context)

- **`hill-sphere` + `lagrange-points`** — orbital-scale gravity-dominance volumes; geometrically outside a body-centered surface scene and meaningless at that zoom. Stay `/explore`-only.
- **`/fly` Flight Director / `coast` / `conics`** — mission-in-flight concepts (MET, Δv, trajectory physics) with no meaning on a static surface map. A "surface ops / rover telemetry" HUD would be a separate, larger initiative, not a port.

### Risk carried forward

PRD-023 Risk #1 (crowding) is sharper on the surface routes, which already carry the altitude HUD, lat/lon HUD, scale bar, and traverse chrome. Deliberate placement + desktop-and-mobile screenshots gate the surface Tactical Scan slice.
