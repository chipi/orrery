## Why this UXS exists

PRD-030 sets the *what/why* and RFC-032 the *how it's built*; this UXS owns the part that makes or breaks the epic — **how it feels to move through the universe**. The navigation is novel (zoom *and* warp, nested contexts, physics lenses), and without a pinned interaction model it gets designed ad-hoc in scene code — the chaos we're explicitly avoiding. This spec is the contract for movement, orientation, selection, transitions, and discovery, on desktop and mobile.

## The one rule: you always know where you are

With teleporting instead of continuous travel, orientation is the scarcest resource. Every screen carries a **breadcrumb / "you are here"**: `Solar System › Orion Spur › Milky Way › Local Group`. It's always visible, always clickable (each crumb warps back to that context). This is non-negotiable — it's what keeps discovery from becoming disorientation.

## Navigation model

- **Zoom within a context** — the existing /explore camera controls, unchanged. Scroll/pinch out keeps pulling back.
- **The boundary crossing (the signature moment)** — as you zoom out past the Kuiper/Oort, the solar system smoothly **collapses to a single luminous dot** and the real nearby-star field **fades in around it**, re-based into the Neighborhood context. Continuous, legible, cinematic — no hard cut. This is the emotional core of v2; it gets a prototype before code.
- **Warp between contexts** — beyond the Neighborhood, you don't crawl through empty black. Click a pin (a galaxy, the galactic center) → a **1–2 s cinematic warp** with a caption that *teaches the distance* ("warping 4.24 light-years to Proxima Centauri…", "2.5 million years to Andromeda…"). The warp motivates from a real click and always lands you oriented (breadcrumb updates, target framed).
- **Two doors in** — (a) **wander** and stumble on objects in the dark (the discovery joy), and (b) **jump** straight from a deep-sky gallery photo or an object index via `?goto=<id>` (browse-as-collection). Same objects, two ways.

## Selecting & understanding an object

Star, deep-sky, Milky Way, Local Group, Local Sheet, Virgo, Laniakea, Cosmic Web and black-hole panels share one body (`UniversePanelBody`, [UXD-023](decisions/UXD-023-star-and-outer-panels-follow-the-planet-pattern.md)): one hero or none, tabs on `PanelTabRow`, a star's constellation finder in its IN THE SKY tab. A black hole is picked at its own scale and opens its panel in place; the lensing render is the panel's action. The heroes of the outer-scale and black-hole panels come from `universe/panel-heroes.json` (gallery photos through the deep-sky loader, a photo of a part captioned "Pictured: …"; Triangulum shows NGC 604 since phase 4 of the galaxy pages), and the entity pages (`/explore/deep-sky|galaxies|structures/<id>`) show the same picture as one figure under the lede (UXS-001, ADR-096 items 17 and 18).

Click any star / planet / deep-sky object → the existing **Panel** opens (bottom-sheet mobile / right drawer desktop), with tactical detail from real data: name, distance ("light left in…"), type/spectral class, and — for exoplanet systems — the full per-planet treatment mirroring our solar-system planets. A deep-sky object's panel leads with its reviewed one-line `fact` and a `bio` paragraph in the reader's language (en-US fallback), then the catalogue values and their sources; only objects that open a panel show it (a photo-backed object or one with a culture door). Selection styling + hover mirror the current /explore conventions. Everything localized (i18n) like /science; every fact carries provenance.

**Select = focus** ([UXD-019](decisions/UXD-019-select-equals-focus.md)). Selecting an object, by canvas click, INDEX row, link or `?id=`, does three things: opens its Panel, rings it (a belt brightens instead; × closes the panel and the ring stays until another selection, Reset View or the chip clears it), and moves the camera onto it (a planet or moon at 8 radii, a small body at 14, a belt framed whole from the Sun; at the outer scales the camera pans to the member and eases in to 0.6 x the entry distance, and the scale's Reset gives it back). With a side drawer open the object sits in the middle of the space the drawer leaves. A chip turned off clears a selection of its kind; selecting an object whose chip is off turns the chip on. An id with no object in the scene is plain text, never a dead link. A mission linked from a body's panel opens in place, newest first.

**Hover and selection feedback** ([UXD-020](decisions/UXD-020-explore-readability-pins-rings-labels.md)): every pin at every scale (and the planet or small body under the cursor in the solar system) shows one thin ring on hover and selection, 44 px and screen-constant at the scales with pins, in the state blue `#889fe7`; the "you are here" anchor keeps a gold ring up at the seven levels that have one (not the solar system). A selected pin keeps its ring and name while another is hovered. Names are 12 px at every scale, hidden when they would overlap a more important one (the hovered and selected name is always shown), and a name does not move when its pin is hovered.

**The HUD numbers and names** ([UXD-021](decisions/UXD-021-explore-hud-scale-bar-picker-pills-references.md)): the scale bar at the lower right keeps one width (120 px, 64 px on a phone) at every zoom and its value follows the zoom, to three significant digits (a round number at a moving width is gone); the box around it keeps one size (the label sits under the bar) and, with a side panel open, sits left of the panel. The solar system and the neighbourhood draw a bar; the six schematic scales do not (they are not to scale). The scale picker's ladder opens leftward when the row is flush right and stays on screen; with a side panel open the row sits left of the panel. There is one RESET VIEW per scale, always drawn, disabled until there is something to reset: the HUD row's at the solar system, the navigator's at the other seven. The "← Vega" and "→ star" pills show only while ʻOumuamua or the craft is hovered or selected. The REFERENCES overlay draws the eight planets at true relative size and lists their names and diameters in an aligned grid, in the reader's language.

<a id="the-orbit-ruler"></a>**The orbit ruler** ([UXD-024](decisions/UXD-024-orbit-ruler-one-row-draws-its-regime.md); look in Design System §5.16): the lower-left reference of /explore, /earth, /moon and /mars. Every row is one line (the ruler grows from 208 to 280 px to its longest row; a name that cannot fit is cut with an ellipsis and carries the full name as its title). Hovering a row or focusing it with the keyboard draws that regime in the scene in the highlight blue (a band, a line, a shell, or a pin ring on a Lagrange point's residents; beyond the scene on /explore, dashed), a click or Enter opens the regime panel and the regime stays drawn while it is open. The regime colour stays on the row's dot. The header folds the ruler (`▾` / `▸`, `aria-expanded`); the choice is remembered and a window under 560 px high starts folded. On the Worlds the locate chip, the "AS SEEN FROM YOU" readout and the ruler are one column in the lower-left corner; on a phone the chip is the bar's first icon and the ruler is the drawer's Ruler tab. In the regime panel a resident with an image shows it as a 28 px photo ringed in its agency colour, one without keeps the dot; the photo is the thumb of the entity's blessed hero slot ([UXD-025](decisions/UXD-025-lists-carry-a-round-photo.md)). 23 of the 38 founding missions are links (on /explore they open in place), each with the mission's photo before the year, ringed in its agency colour (the other 15 keep the dot), and none ends in a country tag.

**The iconic missions legend and its boxes** ([UXD-026](decisions/UXD-026-iconic-missions-legend-and-milestone-boxes.md); look in Design System §5.14 and §5.15): ICONIC MISSIONS is the first layer chip and opens the legend under the HUD row. Its first line is the title, "Hover any spacecraft to read why it's iconic", with an (i) that opens the why-one-plane popover (title, two sentences, a "Read full" link to `/science/transfers/coplanar-trajectories`); then 18 rows of 28 px (36 px on touch), the list scrolling alone. A hovered or keyboard-focused row, or the craft's path under the pointer, shows its tagline in a card beside the legend, level with the row, so nothing in the list moves; a selection opens the mission panel. In the scene the highlighted mission shows a **milestone box** at each labelled waypoint (header `n/N · date`, the milestone name below, 12 px at every zoom, no mission name) and one **mission box** at Today (the name over where the craft is now). Boxes go through the shared label placement pass (§5.14): a box that would overlap another is hidden and returns on zoom, and the marker under the pointer shows its own. A click on a box or marker opens the mission (UXD-019).

**The orbit ruler's science cards** ([UXD-010](decisions/UXD-010-ruler-regimes-each-get-their-own-science-card.md), [UXD-022](decisions/UXD-022-science-link-lists.md)): each regime pane of a ruler (Explore, Earth, Mars, Moon) carries one `/science` card, its own best match for what the pane teaches, and no card repeats inside one ruler (GIANTS shows the giants' moon systems, BELT the asteroid belt, KUIPER the Kuiper belt, SCATTERED eccentricity, HELIOPAUSE the AU). Across the four rulers a concept may repeat where the physics is the same. The pair is the same in every locale.

## The INDEX on every scale

Every one of the eight scales (solar system, neighbourhood, Milky Way, Local Group, Local Sheet, Virgo, Laniakea, Cosmic web) has the same INDEX ([UXD-017](decisions/UXD-017-one-index-on-every-scale.md); look and parts in Design System §5.13): a left-edge handle labelled INDEX opens a 320 px rail with a search box, kind chips, a count and a list of every selectable object of that scene, whether or not it has a page. A row selects the object as a canvas click does, and takes the camera ([UXD-019](decisions/UXD-019-select-equals-focus.md)); a `↗` link beside the row opens the object's page where one exists. The INDEX stays open after a select.

- **One state.** One open flag for all eight scales. On a scale jump it stays open on a fine pointer and closes on a coarse one, where the rail covers most of the width.
- **Where it is not offered.** During a takeover (the black-hole lensing view, deep-sky immersion, an exoplanet's BodyScene), in the 2D view outside the solar system, and in Exhibit Mode.
- **Chips per scale.** After All, kind chips (the solar system: planets, dwarfs, asteroids with Arrokoth, moons, comets with ʻOumuamua, so every row but the Sun and the two belts is under a chip; the neighbourhood: planetary systems, culture doors, deep sky; the outer scales: the scale's kinds), plus Notable objects where the data has headliners; at most five besides All.
- **Solar-system rows are two lines** (C6, UXD-017 amendment): the name over a meta line of distance, size and listed moons (`1.52 AU · 6,779 km · 2 major moons`; a dwarf, asteroid or Kuiper object `2.77 AU · 939 km`; a comet its distance; a moon `Jupiter · 3,643 km`; a belt `2.2–3.2 AU`; the Sun "Star"), in the reader's number and unit forms. The other seven scales keep one line.
- **A round photo leads the solar-system rows** (L45-T2, [UXD-025](decisions/UXD-025-lists-carry-a-round-photo.md)): the Sun, every planet, every moon, both belts and 12 of 23 small bodies show the 28 px `EntityThumb` of their blessed hero image in the lead slot, ring the body's colour (the planets, the Sun) or neutral; the 11 small bodies with no image keep the dot. The other seven scales carry no photo (held, decision 3). A closed INDEX loads no image.
- **Black holes** sit in the scale their distance puts them in: Sagittarius A\* and Cygnus X-1 in the Milky Way, M87\* in Virgo, Gargantua (fiction, dashed and muted) in a Fiction section at the foot of the Cosmic web. Selecting one closes the INDEX and opens the lensing view.
- **Prerender.** `/explore` is one HTML file for every `?context=`, so all eight lists are in it, hidden unless on screen. Only rows with a page are written there, as links; the other rows join on hydration.
- **Deep-sky objects.** Every object of the neighbourhood's deep-sky section has a row, and a row is named as the object's page and panel name it: designation, then the common name (`M13 · Hercules Globular Cluster`, `M3 · NGC 5272`). Selecting one, by row, canvas click or `?deepsky=<id>`, opens its panel, rings it and turns the camera to it (select = focus); an object with a photo also warps in and immerses, one without opens the flat panel with no hero. The 120 objects with reviewed text have a page, `/explore/deep-sky/<id>` (ADR-096 item 17), and its `↗`. One real object has one page: the Milky Way globular rows (M3, M4, M5, M13, M15, M22, M92) and the Local Group rows of M32, M110, Andromeda and Triangulum link the deep-sky page of the same object, and take its name; the Local Group rows therefore appear in the prerendered HTML too. **Galaxies and structures** (ADR-096 item 18): the Local Group, Milky Way (Magellanic Clouds) and four structure scales link `/explore/galaxies/<id>` and `/explore/structures/<id>` through the same resolver (deep-sky first), where a page has its text: the en-US overlay files under `i18n-src/en-US/universe/galaxies` and `structures` decide, and today 29 galaxy and 30 structure pages exist (batches 05 and 06). A member with a page is a link row in the prerendered HTML and carries the `↗` once hydrated; a member without one stays a button.
- **Z-order.** The handle is at 44 and the rail at 45; the scale navigator rises to 46 while its ladder is open (Design System §4.7).
- The Worlds (`/moon`, `/mars`, `/venus`, `/earth`) keep their own `SurfaceIndexPanel`; a site's photo (L45-T2: the thumb of the image its panel shows, [UXD-025](decisions/UXD-025-lists-carry-a-round-photo.md)) is ringed in its nation colour, the nation legend's, the dot of a site with no photo is that colour, and the row's meta line ends with `In orbit` or `On land` (an Earth object keeps its own colour).

## Lenses (physics overlays)

Reuse the **Science-Lens** control already on /explore. Lenses are toggles on the current scene, not separate screens: **curvature** (spacetime grid warps around mass — labeled as an analogy), **time** (dilation readout + light-travel-time), **causality** (light-cone spheres), **property-space** (re-projects the same star field onto physical axes — the flyable Hertzsprung-Russell diagram). Only offer lenses meaningful in the active context. A lens never hides the breadcrumb or the exit.

## Culture doors

Optional overlay (off by default) that attaches a story card to a real object (Vega → *Contact*, Gargantua → *Interstellar*, Kepler-16b → Tatooine). Clearly badged **fiction over fact** — the real object's science stays primary; the story is a labeled layer. Entry point for later messaging/discussion.

## Responsive & performance (desktop + mobile)

- The **device budget** (RFC-032 C-C) governs dot density: the journey is identical on phone and desktop, the *count* adapts. Core navigation (zoom, warp, select, breadcrumb) is full parity everywhere.
- Heaviest lenses/shaders (gravitational lensing) may be **desktop-first with a graceful mobile tier** — never a hard "unsupported," always a legible fallback.
- Touch: pinch-zoom for within-context, tap-a-pin for warp; the breadcrumb is a tappable 44px target row.

## Accessibility

Extends the existing /explore a11y (RFC-031): the object index is keyboard-reachable (the DOM "mirror" pattern — every selectable object in a list, `?goto=` works without the canvas; one INDEX per scale, one Tab stop, arrow keys, Enter selects, see "The INDEX on every scale"), breadcrumb is keyboard-navigable, warps announce their destination to screen readers, reduced-motion shortens/skips the warp cinematics and the boundary-collapse animation.

## Signature moments that get a visual anchor before build

1. **Boundary crossing** — Sun → one dot among real stars.
2. **A warp** — the cinematic hop + distance caption + re-orientation.
3. **A lens toggle** — the map re-rendered through physics (curvature grid).

These three are prototyped/mocked on real screenshots and gated on Marko's approval before the corresponding slice ships.
