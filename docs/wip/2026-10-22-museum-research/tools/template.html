Museum hardware review

Real Orrery objects on display in museums, from three research passes (2026-10-22). Tick Yes for every row that should get a “See it in a museum” button. Replicas and models were left out. Rows marked uncertain rest on weaker evidence (usually Wikipedia or a photo, because the museum’s own site could not be read).

Decide these first

  1. Test articles (engineering, qualification and ground-test units that never flew): do they get a button? They are real hardware, not models.
  2. Flown parts (a recovered piece, e.g. Skylab debris, the Surveyor 3 camera, F-1 engine parts): button on the object’s page?
  3. Sister units (another unit of the same type that never flew and was not that mission’s backup, e.g. Lunokhod 3 for Lunokhod 1): button?
  4. Soyuz 7K-T capsules (Soyuz 12–40, flown by many guest cosmonauts) have no Orrery entry; agents mapped them to either “Soyuz T” or “Soyuz 7K-OK”. Map to one of those, add a 7K-T entry, or leave them out?
  5. Conflicting sources: Mars-3 and Venera-4 at the Moscow and Kaluga museums are originals per Russian Wikipedia and replicas per Commons; they are not in the table. The full-size Buran OK-ML1 is a test article in one source and a mock-up in another.
