## 1. Vega and Vega C

Card (Vega): agency ESA / Avio (built by Avio, operated by Arianespace; ESA Vega page); country Italy (lead nation; "Italy" not stated on opened pages, Wikipedia says developed by Avio); manufacturer Avio; first_flight 2012-02-13; status RETIRED (last flight 2024-09-05, Sentinel-2C); era 2011-now.
Card (Vega C): manufacturer Avio S.p.A.; first_flight 2022-07-13; status ACTIVE; operator Avio from 2025 (Arianespace 2022-2025).

Facts:
- Vega first flight 2012-02-13; 22 flights, 20 successes, 2 failures; last flight 2024-09-05 (Sentinel-2C) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_(rocket); first flight, 22 flights, 20 successes confirmed by ESA https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Transportation/Launch_vehicles/Vega].
- Vega specs: height 30 m, diameter 3 m, liftoff mass 137 t; stages P80, Zefiro 23, Zefiro 9, AVUM; ~1,500 kg to 700 km circular 90-degree orbit [ESA Vega page above]. Wikipedia: 1,450 kg to 400 km SSO (differs from ESA wording; use ESA). Stage lengths P80FW 11.7 m, Z23 8.39 m, Z9 4.12 m, AVUM 1.7 m (Wikipedia only).
- Failures: VV15 on 2019-07-11, FalconEye-1 lost, anomaly 130.85 s after liftoff just after Zefiro 23 ignition, cause thermo-structural failure of Z23 forward dome [https://www.esa.int/Newsroom/Press_Releases/Vega_Flight_VV15_failure_Arianespace_and_ESA_appoint_an_independent_inquiry_commission (search result; page not fetched), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_flight_VV15 (search result only)]. VV17 on 2020-11-17, SEOSAT-Ingenio and TARANIS lost [https://www.esa.int/Newsroom/Vega_flight_VV17_SEOSAT-Ingenio_TARANIS_mission_failure (search result only)]. NOTE: the VV15 and VV17 pages were seen only in search results, not fetched; dates are confirmed by the Wikipedia Vega summary I did fetch.
- Vega C first flight 2022-07-13 (LARES-2 plus six cubesats) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_C; date also on Avio https://www.avio.com/vega-c].
- VV22 2022-12-21 UTC (01:47:31 UTC), launch from Kourou, Zefiro 40 pressure loss, Pleiades Neo 5 and 6 (~1,977 kg) lost; root cause erosion of the throat insert (procured from KB Pivdenne, Ukraine) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_C_flight_VV22]. ESA calls the date 20 December local [https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2024/12/Vega-C_VV25 via search result]. Use 2022-12-21 UTC.
- Return to flight: VV25, Sentinel-1C, 2024-12-05 22:20 CET [ESA search result https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2024/12/Vega-C_VV25; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_C].
- Avio takes over commercialisation: ESA authorised in August 2024; VV30 was "the second Vega-C launch managed solely by Avio" per CEO Giulio Ranzo [https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/09/15/avio-launches-pair-of-climate-monitoring-european-spacecraft-to-study-plants-oceans-and-atmospheric-changes/; Wikipedia Vega C]. First Avio-only launch of 2026 was VV29, SMILE, 2026-04-09 06:29 UTC, 2,250 kg [https://www.avio.com/vega-c-flight-vv-29-4].
- Flight count: VV30 on 2026-09-15 03:21 CEST (Sentinel-3C 1,143 kg, FLEX 397 kg) was the 8th Vega C launch and 2nd of 2026 [Spaceflight Now URL above]. So Vega C: 8 flights, 7 successes, 1 failure (VV22) through 2026-11-25. Vega + Vega C = 22 + 8 = 30 (consistent with the VV30 designator). Next, VV31 (IRIDE), planned December 2026, exact date not found [https://europeanspaceflight.com/european-rocket-launches-in-2026/ page last updated 2026-11-24; spacelaunchschedule search result].
- Vega C specs: height 34.8 m (Spaceflight Now; Wikipedia), diameter 3.4 m, liftoff mass 210 t (Wikipedia Commons category summary and Vega C page); stages P120C (13.5 m, 3.4 m dia, 141.4 t propellant, 4,780 kN, 134 s), Zefiro 40 (7.6 m, 36.1 t, 1,035 kN avg), Zefiro 9 (3.9 m, 10.5 t, 255 kN), AVUM+ (737 kg propellant, Isp 316.3 s) [Avio https://www.avio.com/vega-c, page as returned]. Payload: up to 2,300 kg to LEO per Avio; Wikipedia states 2,300 kg to 700 km SSO. Avio's page as returned says "LEO"; SSO figure from Wikipedia only.
- Launch site: Ensemble de Lancement Vega (ELV), Guiana Space Centre, Kourou [Wikipedia Vega C; Vega ESA page says "Europe's Spaceport in French Guiana"].

Not found: ESA Vega C fact page (404 on the URL tried); fetched ESA/Arianespace VV15/VV17 pages; Avio's own statement of the Vega C SSO number; Vega C height from Avio.

Suggested best_known_for: "Italian small launcher, 22 flights 2012-2024; Vega C (2022-) returned to flight Dec 2024 and is now sold by Avio".

## 2. Isar Aerospace Spectrum (Germany)

Card: agency Isar Aerospace (private); country Germany; manufacturer Isar Aerospace; first_flight 2025-03-30; status ACTIVE; era 2011-now; epoch commercial-era.

Facts:
- Flight 1, 2025-03-30, liftoff 12:30 CEST (11:30 UTC) from Andøya, Norway. Isar: in-flight termination at ~30 s, flight safety system nominal, root causes an unintended vent-valve opening and loss of attitude control in the roll manoeuvre; vehicle fell into the sea; investigation done within two months [https://isaraerospace.com/mission-updates-overview]. DISCREPANCY: Wikipedia says control was lost at 18 s [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_(rocket)]; ESA says ~30 s test flight. Use ~30 s (Isar, ESA).
- Attempt history for flight 2 (Isar): 2026-01-21 scrub (pressurisation valve); 2026-03-25 abort in autosequence (vessel intrusion + fuel temperature); 2026-04-09 scrub (composite pressure vessel leak); 2026-06-15 scrub (fluid systems); 2026-09-05 success [Isar URL above].
- Flight 2 "Onward and Upward", 2026-09-05, 22:12 CEST (20:12 UTC), Andøya: success, all customer payloads released in SSO. Payloads five cubesats (TU Berlin, Univ. of Maribor, NTNU, TU Wien Space Team, EnduroSat) plus a Dcubed demonstration (Isar); ESA says six cubesats plus one experiment [https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Transportation/Boost/Isar_Aerospace_achieves_first_launch_to_orbit_from_continental_Europe]. Payload count differs (5+1 vs 6+1); not resolved. SpaceNews headline "Isar Aerospace reaches orbit on second Spectrum launch" (https://spacenews.com/isar-aerospace-reaches-orbit-on-second-spectrum-launch/): page returned HTTP 429, headline only from search.
- Flight 3: launch window 2026-11-26 to 2027-02-17, Andøya, payloads include AT-Astra (R-Space, Vienna) [Wikipedia Spectrum; search results newspaceeconomy.ca 2026-11-10; https://europeanspaceflight.com/european-rocket-launches-in-2026/ lists "November 26"]. Not flown as of 2026-11-25; result not found.
- Specs: 28 m, 2 m dia; 9 Aquila (stage 1) + 1 Aquila (stage 2), ten total; propane/LOX; 1,000 kg to LEO, 700 kg to SSO [Wikipedia Spectrum; ESA gives 28 m, 2 m, ten engines, 1,000 kg LEO]. Aquila thrust and mass: not found. Isar's own spec page not opened.
- Launch sites: Andøya (operational); Kourou planned 2027; Nova Scotia planned 2028 [Wikipedia; not confirmed by Isar].

Photos: Commons search found only Isar logos (Isar_Aerospace_logo.svg PD; Isaraerospace-logo2.png CC0). No freely licensed rocket photo found. ESA "Spectrum lifts off" (credit Isar Aerospace, ESA Standard Licence, not free): https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/09/Spectrum_lifts_off

## 3. PLD Space Miura 1 (Spain)

Card: agency PLD Space (private); country Spain; manufacturer PLD Space; first_flight 2023-10-07; status RETIRED (suborbital demonstrator; no second flight found); era 2011-now.

Facts:
- Flight: 2023-10-07 00:19 UTC (02:19 CEST), El Arenosillo, Huelva; apogee 46 km (planned 80 km, lowered for range-safety, flatter trajectory); ~306 s flight; splashed down in the Atlantic under parachute, then sank (probable water leak into fuel tank), not recovered [https://www.space.com/pld-space-first-launch-miura-1-rocket (306 s, 46 km, splashdown); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miura_1 (sank, range-safety reason, 12.7 m, 2,550 kg, TEPREL-B 30.2 kN)]. ESA image page confirms date/time [https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2024/02/Miura_1_launch]. Note: space.com says the vehicle was recovered-ready; Wikipedia says it sank. Use Wikipedia (more specific).
- Specs: 12.5 m (space.com) vs 12.7 m (Wikipedia); 100 kg to ~150 km design.
- Miura 5: first flight now targeted H1 2027 from Kourou (search summary of https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2026/10/pld-system-level-testing-miura-5-three-vehicles/; page returned 403, not opened). PLD investing EUR 35m in the Kourou complex (search result thenextweb). Miura 5 specs: not found (Wikipedia Miura 5 not fetched).

## 4. HyImpulse SR75 (Germany)

- Apogee DISCREPANCY: Wikipedia 50 km, parachuted back and recovered [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HyImpulse]; HyImpulse's own page 40 km apogee, 50 km downrange [https://hyimpulse.de/launch-with-us]. Use HyImpulse (primary) 40 km, flag the conflict. Target had been ~200 km (search summary).
- SR75 specs (HyImpulse): 11 m, 0.64 m dia, 75 kN paraffin/LOX hybrid, up to 250 kg to 200 km.
- SL1: HyImpulse says up to 600 kg to LEO (Wikipedia says 500 kg); 3-stage hybrid; first launch 2027 per later sources, 2026 per Nov 2024 source, no date on HyImpulse page; EUR 45M round Oct 2025 (search results: payloadspace.com/hyimpulse-secures-e45m-for-small-launch/; not opened). SL1 height: not found. First-launch site: not found.

## 5. Rocket Factory Augsburg RFA One (Germany)

Card: agency RFA; country Germany; manufacturer Rocket Factory Augsburg; first_flight not flown (null); status DEVELOPMENT.

Facts:
- 2024-08-19, SaxaVord: static fire of the first stage with all nine engines ended in an anomaly: fire, explosion, loss of the stage, major damage to the launch mount [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFA_One]. RFA primary statement: not found.
- UK launch licence Jan 2025 (Wikipedia). Stages and rocket delivered to SaxaVord Feb/March 2026; fully stacked July 2026; on 2026-07-28 RFA found an issue in testing and de-stacked; the NET 2026-08-10 window was cancelled; in November 2026 the first launch slipped to 2027, no window [https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/11/11/rocket-factory-augsberg-scottish-launch-slips/; https://europeanspaceflight.com/rfa-one-launch-from-saxavord-set-for-no-earlier-than-10-august/ (search result)]. No RFA One launch through 2026-11-25: confirmed absent from the European Spaceflight 2026 schedule page.
- Specs: ~30 m, 2 m dia (Wikipedia) / 2.15 m (ESA); 3 stages; stage 1 nine Helix at 100 kN each; RP-1/LOX; third stage Redshift OTV; 1,300 kg to SSO 500 km per Wikipedia vs 500 kg to SSO per ESA image caption [https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/09/RFA_team_in_front_of_first_RFA_One_rocket_on_the_launch_pad_at_SaxaVord_Spaceport]. DISCREPANCY unresolved; ESA's 500 kg is most recent. The 1,300 kg figure may be to LEO; not verified.

## 6. MaiaSpace Maia and Latitude Zephyr

Maia card: agency MaiaSpace (ArianeGroup subsidiary); country France; manufacturer MaiaSpace; first_flight not flown; status DEVELOPMENT.
- 2026-07-29: MaiaSpace dropped the suborbital test, goes straight to orbit; first flight H2 2027 (previous: H2 2026 per June 2025; "before April 2027" per Feb 2026) [https://europeanspaceflight.com/maiaspace-ditches-suborbital-test-flight-plans/; https://europeanspaceflight.com/maiaspace-pushes-inaugural-launch-of-maia-rocket-to-2027/ (2026-03-02)]. Site: former Soyuz pad (ELS), Kourou; occupancy agreement signed 2026-02-24.
- Specs (Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maia_(rocket)): 50 m, 3.5 m dia; 3 Prometheus (stage 1) + 1 (stage 2), methane/LOX; 500 kg LEO reusable, 1,500 kg expendable, 2,500 kg with Colibri kick stage. European Spaceflight says 1,500 kg expendable plus ~1,000 kg with kick stage. MaiaSpace's own page: not found.
- Photo: Maia_rocket_by_MaiaSpace.png, CC BY 4.0, Animalculum, https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ac/Maia_rocket_by_MaiaSpace.png (illustration). No photo found.

- 2026-07-01: Latitude and Etlaq (Oman) signed a letter of intent for a first "experimental launch" from Etlaq Spaceport in late 2027. European Spaceflight says the company did not name the rocket (Zephyr name apparently dropped); French Guiana ELM-Diamant facility continues, "construction works expected to resume shortly" [https://europeanspaceflight.com/?p=6710]. Earlier targets 2024, 2025, 2026 (search results).
- Specs: conflicting. 2022 European Spaceflight: 17 m, 1.2 m, 100 kg SSO, nine Navier Mk2, LOX/RP-1. Search summary: 19 m two-stage, up to 200 kg. Wikipedia: 100 kg LEO first variant, 200 kg from 2028 [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude_(company)]. Current specs: not found. Engine test photos below.

## 7. ICEYE (Finland)

Card: agency ICEYE Oy; country Finland; manufacturer ICEYE; first_flight 2018-01-12; status ACTIVE; era 2011-now; epoch commercial-era; category constellation.

Facts:
- ICEYE-X1 launched 2018-01-12 on ISRO PSLV-C40 (XL) from Sriharikota, ~70 kg (bus 85 kg per eoPortal), SSO ~505 km, first SAR satellite under 100 kg, Finland's first commercial satellite; operations ended June 2018 [https://eoportal.org/satellite-missions/iceye-x1; https://www.iceye.com/company/history; ICEYE press release URL https://www.iceye.com/press/press-releases/iceye-successfully-launches-worlds-first-sar-microsatellite-and-establishes-finlands-first-commercial-satellite (search result only)]. The eoportal constellation page started at ICEYE-X2 (2018-12-03, Falcon 9 SSO-A) and says "first satellite of the constellation December 2018": not used.
- Launched to date: 84 SAR satellites as of 2026-10-20 (Bandwagon-5, four satellites, Cape Canaveral; second October launch after Transporter-18 on 2026-10-01) [https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-launches-four-new-satellites-aboard-bandwagon-5-rideshare-mission]. Number currently on orbit: not found (satellitemap.space shows 48 active, unverified). Any launch after 2026-10-20: not found.
- Mass: ICEYE-X1 ~70 kg; standard bus ~85 kg; Gen4 200-220 kg (search summary of iceye.com; page itself gave no mass). Gen5 mass: not found.
- Resolution: ICEYE site "up to 16 cm", "down to 25 cm" imaging [https://iceye.com/satellites/design]; 25 cm announced March 2020 (satnews search result). ICEYE-X1 10 m x 10 m (eoPortal).
- Launch vehicles used (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICEYE): PSLV-XL, Falcon 9 (Transporter and Bandwagon rideshares), Soyuz-2 variants, Electron. Per-satellite assignment: not found. Not primary-verified.
- Founded 2014, Espoo; Aalto spin-off; founders Rafal Modrzewski and Pekka Laurila (Wikipedia).

Photos: ICEYE_SAR_satellite_Netherlands.png and ..._2.jpg, CC BY-SA 4.0, Defensie Nederland: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e3/ICEYE_SAR_satellite_Netherlands.png ; https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a5/ICEYE_SAR_satellite_Netherlands_2.jpg. Logo file listed as public domain (https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/14/Iceye_logo_black.png); a trademark may still apply. No launch photo found.
