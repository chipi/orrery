## Launch
- Date/time: 11 Dec 2022, 07:38 UTC (0738 GMT) - [SFN-L]. NSSDCA (search summary only, page itself errored when fetched): 07:38:13 UT - https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=2022-168A
- Vehicle: Falcon 9, booster B1073.5 (5th flight) - [SFN-L]
- Pad: SLC-40, Cape Canaveral SFS - [SFN-L]
- Rideshare: NASA Lunar Flashlight (CubeSat, 31 lb) - [SFN-L]. Only this one found.
- Separation: Hakuto-R at T+46:59; Lunar Flashlight at T+53:09 - [SFN-L]. (WP says 47 min.)

## Lander
- Name: HAKUTO-R Series 1 lander, Mission 1 (ispace). Tags in NSSDCA: "ispace Series 1 Lunar Lander".
- Dry mass about 340 kg - [ISP-DES]. Launch (wet, fuelled, with payloads) about 1,000 kg, about two-thirds propellant - [SFN-L], [SFN-PRE]. Wet mass is a rounded figure; exact propellant mass: not found.
- Size: 2.3 m tall, 2.6 m wide with legs extended - [ISP-DES], [SFN-PRE]. Four legs (NSSDCA search summary: footprint 2.6 x 2.6 m).
- Payload capacity: 30 kg target [ISP-DES]; about 24 lb (11 kg) of customer payloads actually carried [SFN-PRE].
- Propellants: hydrazine + nitrogen tetroxide - [SFN-L]. Propulsion by ArianeGroup: main system with apogee engine and bi-propellant thrusters; RCS of hydrazine thrusters - [ISP-DES].
- Thruster counts (one main engine, six "assist" thrusters, eight RCS thrusters): from a search summary of SpaceNews/Wikipedia, not opened as primary. Treat as unverified.
- Main engine thrust, Isp: NOT FOUND (ispace design release, ArianeGroup articles, Gunter's page all omit them; ariane.group pages opened).
- Manufacturers: initial assembly JAL Engineering (Narita, 2021); final assembly/integration/test ArianeGroup, Lampoldshausen - [ISP-DES]. Landing legs etc.: not found.
- Power: 350 W peak, body-mounted panels + Li-ion; X-band comms (NSSDCA search summary only).

## Payloads
- Rashid rover (UAE, MBRSC/Emirates Lunar Mission): 10 kg (22 lb), 53 x 53 cm - [SFN-PRE]
- SORA-Q (JAXA/Tomy/Sony/Doshisha): 250 g, about 8 cm wide - [SFN-PRE]
- Total customer payloads about 11 kg - [SFN-PRE]
- Sakanaction "SORATO" music disc - [WP]. Mass: not found.
- Other customer payloads (Japanese companies etc.): not itemised in sources opened; not found.

## Cruise
- Trajectory: low-energy ballistic lunar transfer, about 3 months, "a million miles from Earth" - [ISP-DES], [SFN-L]. Max distance from Earth 1.38 million km (855,000 mi) - [SFN-PRE]. Total path about 1.4 million km - [WP].
- Lunar orbit insertion: 21 Mar 2023, first LOI burn at 10:24 JST (= 01:24 UTC, my conversion) - ispace Success-7 release https://www.ispace-inc.com/?p=1152 (burn time) ; date also in [SFN-PRE] ("LOI March 21").
- Post-LOI orbit: elliptical about 100 km x 6,000 km (search summary of ispace release; not opened as primary). 100 km circular orbit reached 13 Apr (NSSDCA search summary; [SFN-PRE] says burn 13 Apr, 10 min, to reach the 100 km orbit).
- Orbit before descent: 100 km circular (altitude from which descent began - [ISP-RES]).

## Descent and loss
- Planned site: Atlas crater, SE edge of Mare Frigoris - [SFN-PRE]. Coordinates 47.5 N, 44.4 E (NSSDCA search summary only). Three backup sites (Lacus Somniorum, Sinus Iridum, Oceanus Procellarum) - [WP]. Site changed Feb 2021 - spacepolicyonline https://spacepolicyonline.com/?p=62380
- Descent start: 26 Apr 2023 00:40 JST = 25 Apr 15:40 UTC, from about 100 km altitude - [ISP-RES]; [SFN-PRE] gives 1540 UTC. Descent about 1 hour.
- Scheduled landing: about 01:43 JST = 16:43 UTC - [ISP-RES]. [SFN-PRE] and NSSDCA give 16:40 UTC (rounded).
- Deceleration: completed as planned; lander reached below 1 m/s vertical at about 5 km above surface - [ISP-RES].
- Fault: passing a cliff/crater rim about 3 km high, the measured altitude jumped relative to the pre-set estimate; software judged the altimeter value abnormal and discarded all later sensor data. Lander believed it was at 0 m while actually about 5 km up - [ISP-RES]. Contributing: simulation did not include the lunar terrain on the approach path; landing site changed Feb 2021 - spacepolicyonline link above.
- After the scheduled landing time the lander kept descending slowly until propellant ran out, then free-fell - [ISP-RES].
- Impact time: NOT FOUND. Only bound: after about 16:43 UTC plus fuel burn-out. (WP's "16:40 UTC" is the landing time/loss of contact, not a measured impact.)
- Speed and altitude at loss of signal: about 5 km, near-zero vertical speed at the time the lander stopped slowing; impact speed not stated ("not survivable" - NSSDCA search summary). Exact numeric impact speed: NOT FOUND.
- Result: Success 1-8 of 10 milestones done; 9 (landing) and 10 failed - [ISP-RES]. CTO Ujiie: "not a hardware failure" (press, via search summary).

## Impact site (LRO)
- LRO imaged the area 26 Apr 2023 (before-image M192675639R, after M1437131607R); 10 NAC images, region about 40 x 45 km - [LROC], [NASA-LRO]. Announced 23 May 2023.
- Location 47.581 N, 44.094 E; at least four prominent debris pieces; higher-reflectance area about 60-80 m across - [LROC], [NASA-LRO].
- Credit NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University. LROC site says "all rights reserved" but NASA page says NASA content is public domain; Commons lists the images as Public domain.
