## Descent and landing
| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Mons Mouton, ~160 km (100 mi) from the south pole; intended 84.78 S, 29.13 E (search snippet of LROC) | collectSPACE above; LROC https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1401 (not opened; snippet) |
| Actual coordinates (LROC) | 84.7906 S, 29.1957 E, inside a 20 m diameter crater | LROC https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1408 (opened) |
| Offset from target | 250 m (820 ft) per Intuitive Machines; "more than 1,300 ft (400 m)" per NASA | SpacePolicyOnline URL above (both numbers) |
| Descent orbit insertion | 2025-03-06 5:33 a.m. EST = 10:33 UTC (conv.) | collectSPACE |
| Main-engine relight | ~15 min before touchdown (about 17:15 UTC, conv.). Wikipedia gives PDI 17:27 UTC (Wikipedia only; conflicts). | collectSPACE; Wikipedia |
| Powered-descent profile | start ~4,000 mph (~1.8 km/s) | Spaceflight Now https://spaceflightnow.com/2025/03/07/intuitive-machines-lunar-lander-healthy-but-apparently-on-its-side/ |
| Pitch-over | at ~2 km (1.25 mi) altitude the lander pitched to mostly upright | collectSPACE |
| Terminal phases (planned) | guided descent at ~30 m (98 ft) altitude at ~3 m/s (10 ft/s); braking at ~10 m (32 ft) to ~1 m/s (3 ft/s); then terminal descent on inertial measurements only (no camera or laser, because of dust); final ~2.2 mph (1 m/s) | NASA https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2025/03/06/im-2-vertical-terminal-descent-begins ; Spaceflight Now |
| Touchdown time | 2025-03-06 ~11:30 a.m. CST = ~17:30 UTC (NASA); Wikipedia 17:28:50 UTC; collectSPACE "12:30 p.m. EST" | https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-receives-some-data-before-intuitive-machines-ends-lunar-mission/ ; Wikipedia IM-2 |
| Actual touchdown speed | Search snippet (Aerospace America; page returned HTTP 403, not opened): hit at 10 to 13 m/s because the software judged the vehicle ~400 m above the surface. UNVERIFIED. Intuitive Machines (LROC caption): "hit the surface faster than intended". Horizontal speed: not found. | https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/how-intuitive-machines-is-planning-to-make-its-third-moon-landing-a-successful-one/ ; https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1408 |
| What went wrong | Laser altimeter: "signal noise and distortion that did not allow for accurate altitude readings"; noisy "all the way until touchdown"; low-angle south-pole light; navigation crater database built from LRO imagery at 100 km that "could not accurately account for how craters appear at lower altitudes". Altimeter cause theories (thermal stress or vibration on connectors): thespacebucket page only. | Space.com https://www.space.com/astronomy/moon/heres-why-the-private-athena-lunar-lander-toppled-over-on-the-moon ; Spaceflight Now https://spaceflightnow.com/2025/05/14/intuitive-machines-ceo-points-to-issues-that-prevented-upright-touchdown-during-im-2-moon-landing/ ; https://thespacebucket.com/the-athena-lunar-lander-couldnt-measure-its-distance-from-the-surface/ (that page's "30 km" is a typo for NASA's 30 m) |
| Final attitude | on its side in a crater; solar panels not facing the Sun; tilt angle in degrees: not found | LROC caption above; Spaceflight Now |
| Power | ~100 W, dust-coated panels (Wikipedia only) | Wikipedia IM-2 |
