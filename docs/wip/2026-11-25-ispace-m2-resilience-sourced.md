## Launch
- 15 Jan 2025, 06:11:39 UTC - [S7], [S5]. Deployed from stage at 07:44:24 UTC - [S7].
- Vehicle: Falcon 9 Block 5 B1085-5 (Flight 425) - [WP]. Pad: LC-39A, Kennedy Space Center - [WP].
- Rideshare: Firefly Blue Ghost Mission 1 (same Falcon 9) - [WP], [CBS]. Only that one found.

## Lander
- Dry mass 340 kg; launch mass about 1,000 kg - [WP]; "about 2,200 lb fully fuelled" - [SPC]. Wet-mass split / propellant mass: not found.
- Size: 2.3 m tall [SPC]; width 2.3 m or 2.5 m (WP text and its infobox disagree), ispace figure not found.
- Manufacturer: ispace (Japan-led); Series 1 design, same as M1 per [WP]. Main engine thrust/Isp, RCS: NOT FOUND (same ArianeGroup-supplied system as M1; no primary figures).
- New laser range finder (LRF) flew for the first time because the original supplier stopped making it - Payload https://payloadspace.com/ispace-blames-faulty-hardware-for-failed-lunar-landing/
- Payloads - [S7]: Takasago Thermal water electrolyzer; Euglena food-production module; NCU Taiwan deep-space radiation probe; Bandai Namco Research Institute alloy plate; TENACIOUS micro-rover (ispace-EUROPE, Luxembourg); Moonhouse (Mikael Genberg model house); UNESCO memory disk. Six payloads total - [SPO].
- Masses: TENACIOUS 5 kg, 26 cm tall x 31.5 cm wide x 54 cm long - [SPO]. Moonhouse 3.5 oz (about 99 g, my conversion), 4.7 in x 4 in - [CBS]. All others: NOT FOUND.

## Cruise
- Burn 16 Jan 2025 19:40:18 UTC, 16 s, at 250,000 km from Earth - [S5].
- Lunar flyby: closest approach 22:43 UTC, 14 Feb 2025, about 8,400 km - [S5]. Moon image taken at 14,439 km altitude.
- Low-energy transfer about 2 months; max distance 1.1 million km; all deep-space manoeuvres done 24 Apr 2025 10:00 UTC - [S7].
- Lunar orbit insertion: 6 May 2025 20:41 UTC (7 May 05:41 JST), main-engine burn about 9 min - [S7].
- Final circular orbit about 100 km reached 28 May 2025 - [WP] (ispace Success-8 release, 31 May, was not retrievable). CBS: 62-mile circular orbit.
- Trajectory type: low-energy ballistic transfer with a lunar flyby - [S5], [S7].

## Descent
- Planned site: Mare Frigoris, 60.5 N, 4.6 W (= 355.4 E) - [SFN], [LROC1412]. LROC image of the site posted 16 May 2025: wrinkle-ridge terrain, about 5 km patch.
- Scheduled landing: 5 Jun 2025 19:17 UTC (04:17 JST 6 Jun) - [SPC], [WP].
- Descent start about 2:10 pm EDT = 18:10 UTC, 66-minute descent - [CBS].
- Planned phases (from [SFN] via page summariser; verify against ispace's own landing-sequence slide before use): Phase 1 DOI at 100 km (L-67 to L-66 min); Phase 2 coast 100 to 20 km (L-66 to L-13 min); Phase 3 braking 20 to 3 km, 6,000 to 380 km/h (L-13 to L-2 min); Phase 4 braking and pitch 3 to 1 km, 380 to 120 km/h (L-2 to L-1 min); Phase 5 terminal 1 km to 100 m, 120 to 2 km/h (L-1 min to L-20 s); Phase 6 landing 100 m to 0, 2 to 0 km/h (last 20 s). [SPO] confirms 100 km orbit to 20 km before main-engine braking.
- Last telemetry: 192 m (630 ft) above surface, speed "faster than expected", about 1 min 45 s (SPC) / 1:40 (SPO) before scheduled touchdown - [SPC], [SPO]. Speed number: 42 m/s per search summary of [SPC]-adjacent reporting (page I opened did not give it); Payload says 42 m/s; a "187 km/h" figure also circulates (not opened). 42 m/s and 187 km/h disagree (151 vs 187 km/h). Treat speed as UNRESOLVED; use "about 42 m/s" only with that caveat.
- Cause (ispace): LRF anomaly; LRF was slow to give valid altitude values, lander could not decelerate enough; attitude was near vertical in the final phase - [TCA]. Two candidate mechanisms: installation direction / abnormal attitude, or in-flight LRF degradation - [TCA]. Four possible direct causes (surface reflectivity, laser angle, lower laser power, performance degradation at speed or radiation) - Space.com https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/private-japanese-moon-lander-crashed-due-to-laser-errors-ispace-says
- Impact time: NOT stated by ispace. Derived: last telemetry about 105 s before 19:17 UTC, so impact about 19:15 UTC (my arithmetic, not sourced). [LROC1456] says "5 June 2025 18:13 UTC", which conflicts with the 19:17 UTC schedule and the 18:10 UTC descent start; do not use 18:13 without ispace confirmation.
- Cost: 1.5 billion yen extra development for later missions - [TCA]; ($10.3 M) Payload link above.

## Impact site (LRO)
- 60.4445 N, 355.4120 E (= 4.588 W), elevation -2431.6 m; "dark smudge with subtle bright halo" - [LROC1456]. LRO imaged it 11 Jun 2025 from about 80 km (Universe Today https://universetoday.com/articles/nasas-lro-views-ispace-hakuto-r-mission-2-moon-lander-impact-site); LROC post dated 20 Jun 2025. Image width 1,040 m; survey area 3,000 m.
- Distance from planned point (60.5 N 4.6 W): about 0.05 deg lat: not stated by source (my estimate about 1.7 km north-south; not sourced).

## Investigation reports
- ispace Technical Cause Analysis, 24 Jun 2025 - [TCA].
- External Review Task Force (CAST method), report 27 Mar 2026, co-chairs O. de Weck (MIT) and N. Kotake (Keio); 12 experts incl. JAXA, NASA JPL, ESA, Draper; seven recommendations: terrain-relative navigation, use remaining fuel to reduce landing risk, vendor selection, more testing resources, FDIR, ispace-Draper interaction, risk management - [ERTF].
- Later missions: Mission 3 2028, Mission 4 2029, Mission 5 2030, Mission 2.5 orbiter as early as 2027 - https://www.ispace-inc.com/2026/03/30/statement-regarding-certain-reports-in-domestic-media/
