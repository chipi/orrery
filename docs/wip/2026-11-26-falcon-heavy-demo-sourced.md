## Launch
- 6 Feb 2018, 20:45 UTC, LC-39A, Kennedy Space Center - [WFT], [HOR]. Planned window 1:30-4:00 pm EST (18:30-21:00 UTC); the 20:45 UTC liftoff is therefore inside the 21:00 close - [KIT] (window); exact liftoff second: NOT FOUND.
- Side cores flight-proven: one flew Thaicom 8 (May 2016), the other CRS-9 (July 2016) - [KIT]. Serials: side B1023.2 (ex Thaicom 8, 27 May 2016) and B1025.2 (ex CRS-9, 18 Jul 2016), centre B1033.1 new, second stage new - [WFT] only (fetch summary).
- Block 3 / Block 4 labels for these cores: NOT FOUND (the only "Block" hits in [WFT] concern Falcon 9 versions).
- Thrust: 27 Merlin engines, "more than 5 million pounds of thrust" - [KIT], [NASA]; [WFT] "five million pounds-force (22 MN)". Liftoff mass: NOT FOUND.
- Planned mission: land all three cores; second stage to place the Roadster in a "precessing Earth-Mars elliptical orbit around the sun" - [KIT].

### 1. Side boosters vs a Falcon 9 first stage
- Nose cone replaces the interstage: [WFT] fetch: "Side boosters equipped with a nose cone have different aerodynamic properties than the usual Falcon 9 boosters with a cylindric interstage. For this reason, SpaceX equipped them with larger and sturdier grid fins made of titanium." [WFH] fetch: "two additional Falcon 9 first stages with aerodynamic nose-cones mounted outboard serving as strap-on boosters". Teslarati/Spaceflight Now wording (search summary only, no page opened): the cone "takes the place of the interstage"; side boosters "linked to the center core at the nosecone, the interstage, and octaweb".
- Conflict on grid fins: [WFT] says larger, titanium, on the side boosters; [WFH] fetch says "four retractable grid fins at the top of each of the three Falcon 9 boosters, which extend after separation" and gives no size/material difference. Spaceflight Now (https://spaceflightnow.com/2018/02/05/first-falcon-heavy-launch-blends-spacex-style-raw-power-and-the-unknown/) fetch: "Like the Falcon 9's first stage, the boosters will unfurl grid fins for added stability." Titanium for the demo's two side cores specifically: only [WFT] (fetch summary), unconfirmed.
- Attachment: [KIT]: the side cores "are connected to the center core at its base and at the vehicle's interstage." No strut/fitting description in [KIT]. [SFN-2022] https://spaceflightnow.com/2022/01/27/spacex-gives-converted-falcon-heavy-side-booster-new-life/ : booster "was previously fitted with an aerodynamic nose cone and attachment fixtures when it flew as a side booster"; ground team "removed the former side booster's nose cone and other unique hardware". [LZ] search summary only: side boosters reconfigurable to Falcon 9 (reverse not possible for a centre core).
- Nose cone shape, length, material; side-booster length/height; position of the fittings; colour differences: NOT FOUND. Only [WFH]: vehicle length at launch 70 m (230 ft) (whole stack, not a booster). Soot: a Global News piece (https://globalnews.ca/news/4023080/spacex-falcon-heavy-video-offers-close-up-view-of-rocket-boosters-landing , fetch) quotes a resident's footage of the booster "blowing off the large amount of soot that accrued during the launch" on its pad; no colour description.
