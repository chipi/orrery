## A. Roman - launch
- Launched 30 Aug 2026, 7:26 a.m. EDT (= 11:26 UTC; WP gives 11:26:04 UTC), Falcon Heavy, LC-39A, KSC - [RL], [RWP] (Block 5, "Flight 13" per WP only). Pre-launch expectation (07:26 EDT) confirmed.
- Separation from upper stage 7:57 a.m. EDT, about 31 min after liftoff (= 11:57 UTC; conversion mine) - [RL]; "31 minutes" also [RN]. Space.com live page (https://space.com/news/live/nancy-grace-roman-telescope-live-updates-nasa-begins-spacecraft-commissioning-september-2026, pre-launch plan) said 31 min 31 s.
- Staging: side boosters separated T+2:24, centre-core MECO planned T+3:51 - [RB] (planned times from the launch-day blog).
- Side boosters: landed at Landing Zone 2 and Landing Zone 40, Cape Canaveral SFS; one on first flight, the other on its third (previously GOES-U and Viasat-3 F3) - [RL]; LZ-2/LZ-40 also https://www.collectspace.com/news/news-083026a-nancy-grace-roman-space-telescope-launch.html .
- Centre core: "not recovered, to ensure Falcon Heavy had enough performance" (= expended) - (search summary only). Not stated in [RL], [RB], [RN] or collectSPACE as fetched. Treat as unverified.
- Deployments: solar array / sun shield deployed 1 h 23 min after launch (ground-confirmed), lower instrument sun shade deployed with it - [RN], [RC]. High-gain antenna and deployable aperture cover: pending in [RN]; "deployed 1 Sep 2026" - (search summary only).
- Transfer: "three-month journey" to L2 - [RN], [RC], [RL]. Insertion about 100 days after launch, early December 2026 - [RNW], [RWP]. Conflict: pre-launch Space.com live page says transit about 30 days (see URL above); not supported by NASA. Use three months.
- First mid-course correction 31 Aug 2026 (used about 40 lb of a 441 lb budget); second planned late Sep; station-keeping about every 28 days after insertion; fuel for at least 22 years - [RNW].
- L2 distance about 930,000 mi (1.5 million km) from Earth opposite the Sun - https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5673 . Orbit type: quasi-halo ("a large loop around L2", larger than the Moon's orbit - search summary only). Halo size in km: NOT FOUND (NASA pages opened do not give it; a NTRS paper https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240004229/downloads/AAS_ASC_2024_Konitzer_ToStrives.pdf appeared in search but was not opened).

## A. Roman - spacecraft (model inputs)
- Deployed height: over 42 ft, "12.8 m" with aperture cover up - [RSVS] and (search summary only) NASA drone page; "12.7 m long" - [RFAQ]. Conflict 12.7 vs 12.8 m; both from NASA.
- Deployed width: solar panels "over 14 ft (4.3 m)" [RSVS]; "more than 14 ft (4.4 m)" [RFAQ]. Conflict 4.3 vs 4.4 m.
- Bus: 2.5 m wide x 2 m tall, 3,800 kg - (search summary only, NASA page unopened).
- Mass: launch 4,166 kg, dry 4,059 kg, payload 2,191 kg - (search summary only). [RWP] says launch mass "9,174 kg (20,225 lb)": conflicts and is inconsistent with the 4,166 kg (9,184 lb) figure; likely lb/kg mix-up, do not use. Authoritative opened source for mass: NOT FOUND.
- Primary mirror 2.4 m, 186 kg (410 lb) - https://science.nasa.gov/mission/roman-space-telescope/roman-observatory . Three-mirror anastigmat, focal length 19 m f/7.9 - [RWP]. Barrel-shaped optical tube (same NASA page).
- WFI: 300.8 Mpx, 18 H4RG-10 detectors, 0.28 deg^2 FOV - [RWP]; "300-megapixel" [RESA]. Coronagraph Instrument: 575-825 nm, dual deformable mirrors, technology demonstration - [RWP], [RESA].
- Solar Array Sun Shield doubles as thermal shield - [RL]. Aperture-cover and antenna dimensions: NOT FOUND. Antenna type (high-gain, deployed on a boom) - [RN] mentions it; size NOT FOUND.
- Quasi-halo orbit at L2 - [RFAQ].

## B. SPHEREx
- Launch 11 Mar 2025, 11:10 p.m. EDT = 03:10 UTC 12 Mar - [SWP] (12 Mar 03:10:00 UTC); 7:10 p.m. PDT quoted in [SQF] is a PST/PDT mismatch (quick-facts says "7:10 p.m. PST", the actual date was PDT); launch day 11 Mar per [SQF] URL set and JPL mission page https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/missions/spherex/ . Falcon 9 Block 5, Vandenberg SLC-4E, rideshare with the four PUNCH smallsats - [SWP].
- Orbit: polar, along the day-night terminator, about 98 min, 14.5 orbits/day - [SJPL], [SMAP]. Altitude: [SWP] says 700 km, 97 deg; 650 km (404 mi) and dawn-dusk Sun-synchronous - (search summary only, NASA press kit snippet). Conflict 650 vs 700 km, unresolved.
- Mass 502 kg (1,107 lb) - [SQF]. Height 2.6 m, 3.2 m wide and deep - [SQF]. Cost about $488 million - [SQF].
- Photon shields: three concentric cones, each about 19 mm thick, honeycomb aluminium between reflective aluminium sheets; shield stack 1.7 m tall, 3.2 m diameter - [SJPL]. Telescope tilted inside to avoid pole gaps - Commons image page below.
- Telescope: 20 cm aperture, FOV 11.3 x 3.5 deg; six 2048 x 2048 H2RG arrays (41 mm); 102 bands (six filters x 17) - [SJPL]. Conflict: [SWP] says 96 bands and "2.6 m tall, 3.2 m wide"; NASA says 102; use 102.
- Solar array 2.67 x 1.02 m, about 750 W - [SJPL]. Bus builder BAE Systems, Boulder (formerly Ball) - [SJPL], [SWP]. Payload Caltech / JPL; data at IPAC.
- Prime mission 27 months incl. one month each commissioning and decommissioning - [SQF]; [SWP] says 25 months. Science ops began 1 May 2025 [SWP]; NASA says mapping began in May [SMAP].
- First all-sky map completed Dec 2025, 102 colours; four all-sky maps planned in the prime mission; status operational - [SMAP]. Anything after Dec 2025 (second map date): NOT FOUND.
