## 1. ISS

| Crew | Gidzenko, Krikalev, Shepherd [W-Soyuz_TM-31] | Cabana, Sturckow, Ross, Currie, Newman, Krikalev [W-STS-88] | Hurley, Behnken [W-Crew_Dragon_Demo-2] |

Case for each (my judgement, from the sourced facts):
- TM-31: "the first Soyuz spaceflight to dock with the ISS", start of continuous occupation from 31 Oct 2000 and still unbroken per the article [W-Soyuz_TM-31]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_1 says Gidzenko flew the approach and the crew stayed 136 days (2 Nov 2000 to 19 Mar 2001), and calls it the beginning of an uninterrupted human presence. The strongest "lasting result": every later expedition descends from it. Crew mix (two Russians, one American) is the programme's partnership in miniature.
- STS-88: "first Space Shuttle mission to the ISS", the first link of US and Russian hardware (Unity to Zarya) [W-STS-88]. The foundational assembly step, but not a capsule docking and no resident crew; its Zarya capture and PMA mating are a different scene (grapple, then thruster-driven mating) from what W9 describes.
- Demo-2: "first crewed orbital spaceflight launched from the US since the last Shuttle mission in 2011, and the first ever operated by a commercial provider" [W-Crew_Dragon_Demo-2]. A turning point in how nations buy crew transport; second only to TM-31 in lasting weight, and the most recent (not a reason to rank it).

## 2. Mir

| | Soyuz T-15 | Soyuz TM-2 | STS-71 (Atlantis, Mir-18 exchange) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch UTC | 13 Mar 1986 12:33:09, Soyuz-U2, Baikonur [W-Soyuz_T-15] | 5 Feb 1987 21:38:16, Soyuz-U2, Baikonur 1/5 [W-Soyuz_TM-2] | 27 Jun 1995 19:32:19, Atlantis, LC-39A [W-STS-71] (3:32 p.m. EDT: https://www.nasa.gov/history/space-station-20th-sts-71-first-shuttle-mir-docking/) |
| Docking UTC / port | 15 Mar 1986 13:38:42 Mir core forward; 6 May 1986 16:57:52 Salyut 7 aft; 26 Jun 1986 19:46:07 Mir forward again [W-Soyuz_T-15] | 7 Feb 1987 23:27:40, Mir aft [W-Soyuz_TM-2] | 29 Jun 1995 13:00:16, Kristall forward (APAS) [W-STS-71]; 9:00 a.m. EDT, 216 nmi over Lake Baikal region (same NASA page) |
| Crew | Kizim, Solovyov [W-Soyuz_T-15] | Romanenko, Laveykin (launch); Viktorenko, Faris landed [W-Soyuz_TM-2] | Gibson, Precourt, Baker, Harbaugh, Dunbar + Solovyev, Budarin up; Thagard returned [W-STS-71] |

Case for each:
- T-15: first crew to Mir, and "the first time a spacecraft visited and docked with two space stations in one mission" (Mir then Salyut 7 on 6 May, back on 26 Jun); last Salyut 7 crew; Kizim became the first human to spend a full year in space (cumulative) per https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_EO-1. A first and a passing of the torch between station generations; the Mir-to-Salyut hop is a unique story but a long, two-station scene for /fly.
- TM-2: first crewed Soyuz-TM flight and the second Mir crew; the resident crew used it as lifeboat, including during the Kvant-1 docking scare (module passed within 10 m, docked 11 Apr) [W-Soyuz_TM-2]. Strong for Soyuz-TM and long-duration practice; weaker as a single marquee docking.
- STS-71: first Shuttle-Mir docking, 100th US crewed launch, first Shuttle crew exchange; Thagard's near-four-month Mir stay was a US record; inaugurated Phase One of the ISS programme; largest structure in orbit at the time (nearly 500,000 lb) [https://www.nasa.gov/history/space-station-20th-sts-71-first-shuttle-mir-docking/; W-STS-71 gives about 225 t]. Docked "off by less than one inch and 0.5 degree" [https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/sts71/sts-71.htm]. Direct ancestor of the ISS partnership.

Conflicts: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir says Soyuz T-15 launched "March 1987"; [W-Soyuz_T-15] and Mir_EO-1 say 1986. I used 1986 (two pages agree; the Mir page is wrong on its face).

## 3. Tiangong

| | Shenzhou 12 | Shenzhou 13 | Shenzhou 14 | Shenzhou 15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Launch UTC | 17 Jun 2021 01:22:31, CZ-2F/G, Jiuquan [W-Shenzhou_12] (9:22 Beijing: https://en.people.cn/n3/2021/0617/c90000-9862248.html) | 15 Oct 2021 16:23:56 [W-Shenzhou_13] | 5 Jun 2022 02:44 [W-Shenzhou_14] | 29 Nov 2022 15:08:17 [W-Shenzhou_15] |

| Crew | Nie Haisheng, Liu Boming, Tang Hongbo | Zhai Zhigang, Wang Yaping, Ye Guangfu | Chen Dong, Liu Yang, Cai Xuzhe | Fei Junlong, Deng Qingming, Zhang Lu |

Case for each:
- SZ-12: first crew to the station; first Chinese crewed flight since 2016; about 6.5 h launch to dock (Shenzhou 11 in 2016 took over 40 h, per search summary of https://en.people.cn/n3/2021/0617/c90000-9862248.html and others); 90-day stay, two EVAs; first test of a radial rendezvous on departure (16 Sep) for SZ-13 [W-Shenzhou_12]. The "first crew to the station" slot, parallel to TM-31 and T-15.
- SZ-13: first planned six-month stay; Wang Yaping first Chinese woman to spacewalk (7 Nov 2021); first radial docking; 182 d 9 h 32 min [W-Shenzhou_13]. Strong human milestone, weaker "docking" milestone.
- SZ-14 / SZ-15: SZ-15 is "the first Chinese crew handover in space" with six taikonauts aboard, after the T-shaped station completed with Wentian and Mengtian (Jul, Oct 2022); "the last mission focused primarily on assembly" [W-Shenzhou_15]. SZ-14 begins permanent habitation by overlap [W-Shenzhou_14]. The turning point from construction to permanent operation; SZ-15 is the cleaner single choice.

## 4. Timeline detail for the strongest candidates (what W9 needs)

### ISS: two contenders, both with sourced detail
Soyuz TM-31 (Expedition 1):
- Rendezvous profile: two-day approach after six orbit-raising manoeuvres; final approach on forward TV camera relayed to Korolev; automatic Kurs docking at 12:21:40 Moscow = 09:21:40 UTC (mine); then PrK hatches [https://www.russianspaceweb.com/iss-soyuz-tm31.html]. 09:14:50 UTC: 80 m, 0.4 m/s (Gidzenko) per search summary only of https://spaceref.com/?p=143933 (page not opened).
- Conflict: 09:21:03 [W-Soyuz_TM-31], 09:21 [Expedition_1], 09:21:40 (russianspaceweb). Use the 09:21 minute; second differs by source.
- Docking system: SSVP probe-and-drogue (active probe on Soyuz, passive drogue on Zvezda aft: soft-capture latches, retract, eight hard-capture locks, then pressure equalisation; Zvezda aft is an SSVP-G4000 passive port) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSVP_docking_system].
- Soft capture time vs hard dock time: NOT FOUND. Burn times of the two-day approach: NOT FOUND.
Crew Dragon Demo-2:
- About 19 h, five thruster burns; first = Phase burn; Approach Initiation burn about 90 s at 8:11 a.m. EDT (12:11 UTC, mine) to enter the ISS approach ellipsoid; waypoints 0, 1, 2 at 1,300 ft / 720 ft / 66 ft below the station; manual control at waypoint 2; Go/No-Go hold there; capture at 10:16 a.m. EDT, 16-17 min ahead of the planned 10:29 [https://www.americaspace.com/2020/05/31/demo-2-docks-at-space-station-expedition-63-expands-to-five-crew/]. Hurley piloted by touchscreen to 220 m and the capsule then docked autonomously [W-Crew_Dragon_Demo-2]. Soft capture 14:16 UTC, hard capture 14:27 UTC (12 hooks) [W-Crew_Dragon_Demo-2].
- Docking system: NASA Docking System implementing IDSS (soft capture with six servo legs, then 12 hook pairs for hard capture; first used by Crew Dragon on Demo-1, March 2019) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Docking_System_Standard].
- Individual burn times, closing speed: NOT FOUND (NASA blog URL https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/commercialcrew/2020/05/31/crew-dragon-docks-to-the-space-station/ gave 404; nasaspaceflight.com gave 403).

### Mir: STS-71
- Launch window exactly 10 min 19 s; two days of periodic engine firings to 8 nmi behind Mir; terminal phase initiation burn; radar tracking; R-bar approach from directly below; manual control about half a mile below; station-keeping 250 ft; closing about 0.1 ft/s (0.107 ft/s per W-STS-71); completion at 30 ft; contact 13:00:16 UTC with under 25 mm lateral and 0.5 degree misalignment [https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/sts71/sts-71.htm; W-STS-71].
- Docking system: Atlantis's APAS-95 mated to one of two APAS-89 on Kristall (APAS-89 reduced the APAS-75 outer diameter from 2030 to 1550 mm, petals inward; APAS-95 was the Shuttle variant, an active capture ring captures the passive ring) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Androgynous_Peripheral_Attach_System; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir for the ports]. W-STS-71 names the shuttle's system "APDS", which is a different name for the same hardware class; I did not verify the name.
- TI burn time, MCC times, soft-capture time: NOT FOUND (the NASA page gives no UTC).
- If T-15 were picked instead: SSVP drogue ports (Mir's "Konus" drogues, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir), times in section 2, rendezvous detail NOT FOUND.

### Tiangong: Shenzhou 12 (alternatively 15)
- Launch 01:22:31 UTC, docking 07:54 UTC: 6 h 31 min (mine); about 6.5 h "fast autonomous rendezvous"; spacecraft first goes in front of the station, U-turns and docks on the forward port; station about 35 t, Shenzhou about 8 t; three-part stack with Tianzhou-2 [https://en.people.cn/n3/2021/0617/c90000-9862248.html; fast-rendezvous and U-turn lines from search summary only of https://www.pressreader.com/china/global-times/20210618/281492164271056]. Wikipedia's radial test: on undocking 16 Sep the crew tested the radial rendezvous used for SZ-13 [W-Shenzhou_12].
- Docking system: Chinese Docking Mechanism, APAS-based, 800 mm passage, soft capture then hard capture (12 latches), conforms to IDSS per GB/T 34512-2017, first flown by unmanned Shenzhou 8 to Tiangong-1 on 2 Nov 2011 [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Docking_Mechanism].
- Orbit-adjustment burn times, soft-capture vs hard-lock times: NOT FOUND (no CMSA release opened).

## 5. Existing Earth-orbit dockings: is a timeline findable?
- Apollo-Soyuz: YES at docking level: launch Soyuz 19 15 Jul 1975 12:20:00, Apollo 19:50:00; docking 17 Jul 16:09:09; undocking 19 Jul 12:12:00; APAS-75 with docking module; rendezvous burns NOT FOUND [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo%E2%80%93Soyuz_Test_Project; APAS-75 flown only on ASTP: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Androgynous_Peripheral_Attach_System].
- Skylab 2: YES, partial: launch 25 May 1973 13:00, soft dock 26 May 09:56 (49 min then undocked 10:45), hard dock 15:50 after the probe fix [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylab_2]; only the Wikipedia summary read.
- Skylab 3: launch 28 Jul 1973 11:11:00, dock 19:37:00 (8 h 26 min) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylab_3]. Skylab 4: launch 16 Nov 1973 14:01:23, dock 21:55:00 (about 7 h 54 min per the page), forward port [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylab_4]. Burns NOT FOUND.
- Soyuz 11: launch 6 Jun 1971 04:55:09; docking time NOT FOUND on Wikipedia (only "7 June"); the page gives 24 min from 6-7 km to 9 m, manual from 100 m, docking sequence 3 h 19 min [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_11]. Search summary only: 07:49 UTC; a 1971 paper gave 10.45 Moscow (07:45 UTC); that summary's own sum gives 08:14; unresolved conflict.
- Gemini 8: launch 16 Mar 1966 16:41:02, docking 23:14 UTC (MET about 6 h 33 min); rendezvous burns listed (5 s burn at 1 h 34 min, 59 ft/s sideways over the Pacific, 2.6 ft/s near Mexico); thruster runaway after docking [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_8]. Best-documented of the set.
- Apollo 9: launch 3 Mar 1969 16:00:00; CSM transposition/docking 19:01:59 (MET 3 h 02 min, mine); LM undock 7 Mar 12:39:06; redock 19:02:26; LM jettison 21:22:45 [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_9]. Rendezvous burn detail NOT FOUND.
- None of these were cross-checked against NASA mission reports or the Apollo/Skylab flight journals; the Wikipedia values need a primary source before they go in the catalogue.

## 6. Recommendations (mine; the operator picks)
- ISS: Soyuz TM-31 / Expedition 1. Longest-lasting result (continuous occupation since 31 Oct 2000), clean capsule-to-aft-port docking that matches W9's scene, SSVP hardware sourced. Alternative if a US-side first is wanted: Demo-2, which has the best-published approach timeline.
- Mir: STS-71. Largest first for both nations, ISS Phase One, and the best-documented approach (R-bar, 250 ft hold, 0.1 ft/s, <25 mm). It needs the Shuttle model and Kristall/APAS, not a capsule. Capsule alternative: Soyuz T-15 (first crew, two-station story).
- Tiangong: Shenzhou 12 for "first crew to the station"; Shenzhou 15 if the operator prefers the turning point (handover, completion). Not both.
