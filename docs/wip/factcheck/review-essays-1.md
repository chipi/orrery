## navigation.json — "Finding Mars in the Dark"

### 🔴 N-1 — "five billion kilometres" to Pluto

**Quoted claim:** "New Horizons crossed five billion kilometres to Pluto over nine and a half years"

**Why wrong:** The total journey distance to Pluto was approximately 4.8 billion km (3 billion miles). NASA's own mission press release is titled "NASA's Three-Billion-Mile Journey to Pluto" and confirms this figure. Pluto's average distance from the Sun is ~5.9 billion km, but the actual path traveled was shorter.

**Correct fact:** ~4.8 billion km (3 billion miles)

**Sources:**
- https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-three-billion-mile-journey-to-pluto-reaches-historic-encounter/
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Horizons

### 🟠 N-2 — "once-in-176-year alignment" (inconsistent with delta-v essay)

**Quoted claim:** "a once-in-176-year alignment that would let a single probe bank one assist into the next into the next"

**Why problematic:** The delta-v essay correctly states "once-in-175-years." The Wikipedia Grand Tour program article says "once every 175 years." Gary Flandro's own Wikipedia biography says "175 years." The figure 176 appears in some popular sources (e.g., 19FortyFive, WION News) but the primary technical sources converge on 175. The two essays in this atlas are internally inconsistent on this number, and 175 is the better-supported figure.

**Correct fact:** once every 175 years (per Wikipedia Grand Tour, Gary Flandro biography, NASA history)

**Sources:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_program ("The particular alignment occurs once every 175 years")
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Flandro ("Such alignment occurs every 175 years")
- https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4219/Chapter11.html

## comms.json — "Running the Solar System on a Whisper"

### 🔴 C-1 — Received signal power stated inconsistently and incorrectly

**Quoted claim:** "the power arriving at a 70-metre dish is less than a femtowatt. A femtowatt is 10⁻¹⁵ watts. The number often quoted is closer to 10⁻¹⁸, a fraction of an attowatt: a billionth of a billionth of a watt."

**Why wrong — two errors:**

1. Internal math error: 10⁻¹⁸ W = 1 attowatt (by definition: atto = 10⁻¹⁸). The essay calls 10⁻¹⁸ "a fraction of an attowatt" but 10⁻¹⁸ IS one attowatt, not a fraction of one.

2. The actual received power at current Voyager 1 distances (~24 billion km) is closer to 10⁻²¹ W — a femtowatt (10⁻¹⁵) is 1,000 times too high, and 10⁻¹⁸ (attowatt) is still 1,000 times too high. The correct order of magnitude is around 10⁻²¹ W (a zeptowatt).

**Correct fact:** The received signal power is ~10⁻²¹ W. The phrase "a billionth of a billionth of a watt" describes 10⁻¹⁸ (= 1 attowatt), not the actual received power. The NRAO source says the power is "less than an attowatt."

**Sources:**
- https://public.nrao.edu/ask/how-strong-is-the-signal-from-the-voyager-1-spacecraft-when-it-reaches-earth/ (states 10⁻²¹ W)
- https://spacedaily.com/t-nasas-deep-space-network-can-detect-a-signal-as-weak-as-a-billionth-of-a-billionth-of-a-watt/ ("a billionth of a billionth of a watt" = 10⁻¹⁸ W)

Note: The essay's caption says "around a millionth of a billionth of a watt" (10⁻²¹) which is actually more accurate — but the body text says 10⁻¹⁸, so the body is inconsistent with its own caption.

### 🟠 C-2 — DSS-63 at Madrid

**Quoted claim:** "DSS-63 at Madrid"

**Why potentially misleading:** The Madrid complex is located at Robledo de Chavela, ~65 km west of Madrid. "Madrid" is the conventional shorthand but the dish is not in Madrid. **Verdict:** Acceptable shorthand; not flagged as error.
