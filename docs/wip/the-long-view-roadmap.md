# The Long View — series roadmap

*The editorial arc + idea backlog for Orrery's essay section. A living document: dump labels in §3, shape them into the spiral in §2. Companion to [essay-voice.md](../guides/essay-voice.md). Draft · July 2026*

## 1. The one big story

The series is not a pile of standalone explainers. It is **one story told in a spiral** — humanity reaching outward, one hard-won capability at a time, each essay a full arc in itself but leaning on the last and setting up the next. The connective tissue is deliberately *quiet*: not "Part 3 of 8," not "in our last essay," but **recurring motifs, shared characters (the same missions and craft recur, seen from new angles), and the [cross-link spine](../guides/essay-voice.md)** that lets a reader fall from one essay into the atlas and back out into the next. You should be able to read any one cold — and feel, if you read them in order, that they were woven.

The spiral has bookends. It **opens by going into the dark** — [Finding Mars in the Dark](the-long-view-navigation-prototype.md), navigation, the Star-Trek-into-the-unknown chapter: *there are no roads out there, so how do we even find our way?* It should **close by looking back** — the grand synthesis of the whole journey so far. Between them, it circles through the fundamentals of *going*, the machines that *do* the going, and what it takes to *arrive and survive*.

## 2. The spiral (proposed arc)

Three movements. Order is a suggestion, not a mandate — each essay stands alone, and we can resequence as the backlog grows. What matters is the **weave**: the hand-off note at the end of each ("Read next") points along the spiral, and the motifs (the fixed sky, the tyranny of delta-v, the speed-of-light lag, the human detail) recur so the set accretes into one argument.

**Movement I — Into the dark** *(the fundamentals of going)*
1. **Navigation** — *Finding Mars in the Dark.* How we find our way where there are no landmarks. **[DRAFTED — the opener.]** Hands to →
2. **The grammar of delta-v** — why you never fly straight, why gravity is free fuel, why every gram of propellant is rationed. (Navigation's "aiming at a moving planet" beat opens straight into this.) Hands to →
3. **Talking across the solar system** — deep-space comms, the light-lag, the Deep Space Network. (Navigation's "the ground does the flying" beat seeds it.)

**Movement II — The machines** *(how we build the going)*
4. **Reusable launchers** — the economics of leaving Earth; SpaceX, Blue Origin, and now China all landing boosters. The most contemporary hook in the set.
5. **New propulsion** — the long arc from chemical fire to solar sails, nuclear-thermal, and plasma/ion; the sixties dream paused and now restarting. Forward-looking; a natural idea-seeder for missions.
6. **Power in the dark** — how you keep a spacecraft alive as sunlight thins with distance: RTGs vs solar, the plutonium problem.

**Movement III — Arrival & the long view** *(what it all adds up to)*
7. **The seven minutes** — entry, descent, landing; why arriving is harder than the whole cruise, across Mars, Venus, Titan, the Moon.
8. **The whole journey so far** — the grand synthesis: the heroic interplanetary missions, their purpose, what each taught us. The capstone that references the entire series and the whole atlas — the "looking back" bookend.

> **Launch batch:** pile up **5–6** before going live (your call which — a clean first set is 1, 2, 4, 5 + one of 3/7/8), then ~one a month. The backlog below is where the rest wait.

## 4. How the weave works (mechanics)

So the chain is felt, not announced:

- **The hand-off.** Each essay's **Doors** beat ("Read next") points to its neighbours in the spiral — usually one along the arc, one into the atlas. Never "next episode"; a genuine "if this interested you, here."
- **Recurring motifs.** A small set of images/ideas thread the whole series — *the fixed sky, the tyranny of delta-v, the speed-of-light lag, the anonymous human doing the impossible math.* When one recurs in a new essay, it's a chord the returning reader hears.
- **Shared cast.** The same missions recur, seen from new angles: [Voyager](/missions?id=voyager-1) is a navigation story here, a gravity-assist story there, a power-in-the-dark story a third time. The atlas is the ensemble cast.
- **Feed-forward.** Where an essay reaches for something the atlas doesn't yet model, that's a to-do — a new [`/science`](/science) section or a mission worth adding. **Essays pull the product forward**; log those in the notes column above.
- **The capstone closes the loop.** "The whole journey so far" deliberately references the earlier essays, so the last one makes the spiral visible — the reader looks back and sees the shape.
