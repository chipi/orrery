Saturn

Saturn's density is 0.687 g/cm³ — less than water. Its ring system spans 282,000 km but is only tens of metres thick.

The least dense planet — it would float on water. The ring system spans 282,000 km but is only metres thick. The 26.73° tilt means Earth sees the rings at different angles across Saturn's 29-year orbit.

SCIENCE

  • Axial Tilt and Seasons Saturn's 26.7° tilt is what makes its rings appear to open and close over the 29-year orbit — and what gives the planet hemispheric seasons that last more than seven Earth years. Why Earth has seasons, why Uranus rolled onto its side, and why Venus spins backward.
  • Moons of the System Saturn has ~274 confirmed moons — the most of any planet — with Titan large enough to have its own dense atmosphere and Enceladus actively venting water from a subsurface ocean. Galileo's four-night observation in 1610 ended geocentrism. The system's moons are still ending old assumptions today.
  • Kepler's Three Laws Cassini's 13-year orbital campaign was built around Kepler's laws — every ring-crossing and moon flyby was a Keplerian arc designed years in advance. Three observational rules that turned planetary motion into a closed-form science a century before Newton explained why.
  • Apsides — Perihelion, Aphelion, Perigee, Apogee Saturn's orbit is gentle (eccentricity 0.054) but its 9.6 AU mean distance means perihelion and aphelion differ by 100 million km — Cassini timed its arrival to use the inbound speed. The two extreme points on every elliptical orbit: closest approach, and farthest.

MISSIONS

  • Pioneer 11 — NASA, 1979 (first Saturn flyby; ring detail and Titan distant view)
  • Voyager 1 + 2 — NASA, 1980 / 1981 (close flybys; ring structure, Titan haze, F-ring shepherding)
  • Cassini-Huygens — NASA / ESA / ASI, 2004–17 (13-year orbiter; Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005; Grand Finale ring-crossings)
