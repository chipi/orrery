Apollo CSM Block II

Lunar-capable Command + Service Module

The Command Module was the only piece of Apollo that came home — everything else was spent and thrown away along the route. That made it the part that could not fail on reentry, and reentry from the Moon was the hardest version of the problem: the capsule met the atmosphere at nearly eleven kilometres a second, half again as fast as anything returning from Earth orbit, wrapped in a fireball hot enough to boil steel. Its answer was an ablative heat shield engineered to char and peel away in a controlled way, carrying the heat off with it. Block II was the version that flew crews — the ground-up redesign forced by the Apollo 1 fire — and it brought every astronaut who set out for the Moon back alive.

The lunar-capable Command and Service Module that carried Apollo astronauts to lunar orbit between 1968 and 1972. Fifteen crewed flights in total: Apollo 7–17 (eleven Moon-targeted missions), three Skylab crew rotations (1973–1974), and the 1975 Apollo-Soyuz Test Project — including Apollo 13’s improvised lifeboat configuration.

MISSIONS

  • Apollo 7 1968 First crewed Apollo flight — Earth-orbit CSM shakedown
  • Apollo 8 1968 First humans to leave low Earth orbit + orbit the Moon
  • Apollo 11 1969 First humans on another celestial body
  • Apollo 9 1969 First crewed LM flight — Earth-orbit LM separation + rendezvous tests
  • Apollo 10 1969 Full lunar-landing dress rehearsal — LM descended to ~14.4 km altitude
  • Apollo 12 1969 Second crewed Moon landing — pinpoint touchdown 163 m from Surveyor 3
  • Apollo 13 1970 The mission that turned a near-disaster into NASA's most celebrated rescue
  • Apollo 14 1971 Third lunar landing — Apollo 13's lost target reached; Shepard's golf shots
  • Apollo 15 1971 First J-mission — extended stay, first Lunar Roving Vehicle, "Genesis Rock"
  • Apollo 17 1972 Last crewed mission to the Moon (and the only Apollo with a trained geologist)
  • Apollo 16 1972 First Moon landing in the lunar highlands — Descartes; John Young's "lunar Grand Prix"
  • Skylab 2 1973 First crewed Skylab visit — repaired the damaged workshop and saved the programme
  • Skylab 3 1973 Second Skylab crew — 59 days, deployed twin-pole solar shield to replace parasol
  • Skylab 4 1973 Longest Skylab stay — 84 days, observed Comet Kohoutek, disputed "Skylab strike" rest day
  • Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (ASTP) 1975 First international human spaceflight cooperation; first US-USSR rendezvous + docking

Operating sites

  • Apollo 11 Mare Tranquillitatis The first humans on the Moon. Watched live by ~600 million people — roughly one in six people on Earth at the time. The laser retroreflector placed by Armstrong and Aldrin still receives pulses today, measuring the Earth-Moon distance to millimetre precision. Footprints in the airless regolith will survive unchanged for a million years.
  • Apollo 12 Oceanus Procellarum The precision landing — Conrad touched down within ~163 m of the uncrewed Surveyor 3 probe (1967). They retrieved pieces of it for return to Earth, showing spacecraft survive years on the Moon undamaged by radiation or micrometeorites. A proof-of-concept for future rendezvous missions.
  • Apollo 14 Fra Mauro Alan Shepard, the first American in space (1961), became the only person to play golf on the Moon. He smuggled a club head and two balls aboard. He claimed one 'went miles and miles' — LRO images suggest about 37 m (40 yards). Still: the furthest sporting achievement in history.
  • Apollo 15 Hadley–Apennine First mission with the Lunar Roving Vehicle — drove 27.9 km. Scott left a small aluminium figurine, 'Fallen Astronaut', and a plaque listing all astronauts and cosmonauts who died in the space race. It is the only artwork on the Moon, left without public announcement at the time.
  • Apollo 16 Descartes Highlands The only mission to land in the lunar highlands. Charles Duke left a photo of his family sealed in a bag on the lunar surface. On the back he wrote: 'This is the family of Astronaut Duke from Planet Earth. Landed on the Moon, April 1972.' It will outlast civilisation.
  • Apollo 17 Taurus-Littrow Valley The last time humans visited the Moon. Gene Cernan was the last person to walk on its surface. Before climbing the ladder, he said: 'We leave as we came, and God willing, we shall return.' Harrison Schmitt, the only trained geologist on the Moon, collected samples that reshaped our understanding of lunar geology.

Crewed

  • Space Shuttle Columbia OV-102 — the first Shuttle orbiter to fly to space (STS-1, 1981-04-12); lost on re-entry STS-107 2003-02-01
