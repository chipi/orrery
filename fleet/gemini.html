Gemini

Two-person crew; rendezvous and EVA practice

The Gemini spacecraft was the first one Americans could actually fly. Mercury's astronaut mostly rode a capsule the ground controlled; Gemini's two-person crew got thrusters, a flight computer, and the ability to change their own orbit — to chase down another craft and dock with it. That was its entire reason to exist: not to go anywhere new, but to be a machine for practicing the maneuvers Apollo would need. Roomier than Mercury but still cramped, split into a crew module and an equipment section like Apollo after it, across ten flights it turned rendezvous, docking, and two-week endurance from open questions into things a crew could simply do.

The two-person spacecraft that taught NASA how to rendezvous, dock, and walk in space — the techniques that made Apollo possible. Two uncrewed test flights (Gemini 1 and 2) in 1964–1965 preceded ten crewed missions (Gemini 3–12) in 1965–1966, every one a step toward the Moon.

MISSIONS

  • Gemini 3 / GT-3 1965 First crewed Gemini; first orbital maneuver by a crew
  • Gemini 4 / GT-4 1965 First American spacewalk (Ed White)
  • Gemini 6A / GT-6A 1965 First crewed space rendezvous
  • Gemini 7 / GT-7 1965 14-day endurance flight; rendezvous target for Gemini 6A
  • Gemini 8 / GT-8 1966 First docking of two spacecraft; emergency return
  • Gemini 12 / GT-12 1966 Solved EVA — Aldrin's spacewalks; final Gemini

CREW

  • Gemini 3 (1965-03-23) · Molly Brown · Grissom + Young — first Gemini crewed flight; 3 orbits; Young smuggled corned-beef sandwich on board —
  • Gemini 4 (1965-06-03) · McDivitt + White — first American EVA (Ed White, 23 min); 4-day mission —
  • Gemini 5 (1965-08-21) · Cooper + Conrad — 8-day duration record; first fuel-cell-powered crewed flight —
  • Gemini 6A (1965-12-15) · Schirra + Stafford — first crewed rendezvous (with Gemini 7, within 30 cm); skipped Gemini 6 after Agena failed —
  • Gemini 7 (1965-12-04) · Borman + Lovell — 14-day endurance record (stood for years); target for Gemini 6A rendezvous —
  • Gemini 8 (1966-03-16) · Neil Armstrong + Scott — first space docking (with Agena target); emergency abort after stuck thruster spun the stack —
  • Gemini 9A (1966-06-03) · Stafford + Cernan — Cernan EVA exhausted him + fogged faceplate, informing Apollo EVA design —
  • Gemini 10 (1966-07-18) · Young + Collins — first multi-Agena rendezvous (own Agena 10 + Gemini 8s docked Agena); Collins recovered Gemini 8 ELS —
  • Gemini 11 (1966-09-12) · Conrad + Gordon — direct-ascent first-orbit rendezvous; record-altitude 1374 km on Agena tether —
  • Gemini 12 (1966-11-11) · Lovell + Aldrin — last Gemini; 5+ hours EVA proved choreographed crew tasks were feasible; cleared Apollo EVAs —
