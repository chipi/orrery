H-IIA

Workhorse for JAXA's flagship missions

Japan's H-IIA launched 50 successful missions between 2001 and June 2025 for JAXA's flagship missions including Hayabusa, Hayabusa2, Himawari, and SLIM. Dependable liquid-propellant workhorse that became one of the most reliable launchers ever built.

MISSIONS

  • Akatsuki 2010 Recovered from a 5-year solar-orbit hold to achieve Venus orbit on a backup attempt; the only Venus orbiter active in its final years of operation
  • Hayabusa2 2014 Second asteroid sample return (Ryugu, 2020); first artificial impact crater on an asteroid; first subsurface asteroid material returned to Earth
  • Hope Probe 2020 First Arab interplanetary mission
  • SLIM 2024 First precision landing within 100 m of target on another world

Engines

  • LE-7A Japan's staged-combustion hydrogen core, two decades of H-II service
  • LE-5B Japan's restartable upper stage, flying from H-IIA through H3

Fleet

  • Akatsuki Japan's first Venus orbiter; recovered from a 2010 orbit-insertion failure to study cloud-top dynamics
  • Hayabusa2 Asteroid sample return from Ryugu
  • SLIM Pinpoint lunar landing; toppled but operational

Launcher

  • H3 Replaces H-IIA / H-IIB at lower cost
  • Proton-M Successor to Proton-K; modernised heavy lift
  • Soyuz-FG Crewed Soyuz launches 2001-2019
  • Atlas V United Launch Alliance's evolved Atlas
  • Long March 2F Crew-rated for all Shenzhou flights
  • Soyuz-2 Modern Soyuz workhorse — carries Soyuz MS crew + Progress MS cargo to the ISS
