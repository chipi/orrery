Kourou ELA-3

Ariane 5's home pad — JWST + Galileo + a quarter of geostationary commercial sats over a decade

Europe chose French Guiana in the 1960s because 5.2°N is just five degrees off the equator — the geometry adds about 460 m/s of free eastward velocity for geostationary launches, and the Atlantic to the east is empty. ELA-3 hosted Ariane 5 from its maiden flight in June 1996 — which failed 37 seconds after launch due to a software error — through the successful final mission in July 2023: James Webb, the Galileo navigation system, BepiColombo, Rosetta, Herschel + Planck, JUICE, and the bulk of Western commercial GEO traffic for two decades. ELA-4 next door is now active with Ariane 6.

Launch site

  • Kourou ELS (Soyuz launch complex) Europe's Soyuz pad: first Soyuz outside Baikonur or Plesetsk (2011), 27 launches, eight for Galileo
  • Kourou ZLV (Vega launch complex) Kourou's first launch site: Europa II, Ariane 1 and Giotto on this ground, rebuilt in 2001 for Vega and Vega-C
  • Kourou ELA-4 Ariane 6's home pad — Europe's geostationary access after Ariane 5
  • Tanegashima Yoshinobu LP-1 Japan's heavy-lift gateway — H-IIA + H3, HTV / HTV-X cargo to the ISS
  • Wallops Launch Pad 0 (MARS LP-0A and 0B) Antares pad for Cygnus ISS cargo (2013-2023) and the Minotaur V launch of the LADEE Moon probe
  • Alcântara Space Center Brazil's equatorial launch centre: VLS-1 failures, the 2003 pad explosion, sounding rockets
