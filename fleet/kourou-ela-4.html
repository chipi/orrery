Kourou ELA-4

Ariane 6's home pad — Europe's geostationary access after Ariane 5

The sister pad to ELA-3 at the Centre Spatial Guyanais. Construction started in 2015; Ariane 6's maiden flight VA262 lifted from here on July 9, 2024. ELA-4 inherits the same 5.2°N equatorial advantage that made Ariane 5 the most successful Western geostationary launcher of its era, with a redesigned ground-segment for higher cadence: horizontal-integration assembly building, sliding mobile gantry, full integration of the new ESC-B upper stage. Europe's strategic-autonomy launcher for institutional payloads (Galileo upgrade, Copernicus, ESA science missions) plus commercial GEO traffic.

Launch site

  • Kourou ELS (Soyuz launch complex) Europe's Soyuz pad: first Soyuz outside Baikonur or Plesetsk (2011), 27 launches, eight for Galileo
  • Kourou ELA-3 Ariane 5's home pad — JWST + Galileo + a quarter of geostationary commercial sats over a decade
  • Kourou ZLV (Vega launch complex) Kourou's first launch site: Europa II, Ariane 1 and Giotto on this ground, rebuilt in 2001 for Vega and Vega-C
  • Starbase Orbital Pad A First Starship orbital flight test; designed for the full reusable Mars-bound stack
  • Rocket Lab Launch Complex 1 First private orbital launch site: Rocket Lab's Electron from Mahia, New Zealand
  • Wenchang LC-101 China's heavy-lift pad — Long March 5 to Tianhe, Chang'e 5/6, Tianwen-1, and the lunar return programme
