Launch Complex 39A

Launched Apollo 8, 9 and 11–17 (Apollo 9 in Earth orbit; Apollo 10 flew from 39B); today flies Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, and Crew Dragon

Launch Complex 39A was built around an idea nearly as radical as the rocket it served: don't build the rocket at the pad. Instead, stack the Saturn V inside the cavernous Vehicle Assembly Building, then carry the whole ~3,000-tonne stack out to the pad — standing upright — on a crawler-transporter creeping along at walking pace. That "clean pad" approach let one site turn around the largest rocket ever flown far faster than assembling it in place ever could. The design outlived Apollo by half a century: the same pad launched most of the Space Shuttle fleet and, leased to SpaceX in 2014, now flies Falcon Heavy and astronauts on Crew Dragon. Good infrastructure, it turns out, is the quietest kind of moonshot — build it once and it keeps paying off.

Built for Saturn V, kept for the Shuttle, leased to SpaceX in 2014. From Pad A: Apollo 4 (the first Saturn V), Apollo 11 (the first lunar landing), Apollo 13, Apollo 17 (the last lunar landing), Skylab, eighty-two Space Shuttle flights including the first STS-1, and since 2017 every Falcon Heavy and every Crew Dragon to the ISS. The same flame trench, the same crawlerway, three eras of US human spaceflight.

MISSIONS

  • Apollo 8 1968 First humans to leave low Earth orbit + orbit the Moon
  • Apollo 11 1969 First humans on another celestial body
  • Apollo 9 1969 First crewed LM flight — Earth-orbit LM separation + rendezvous tests
  • Apollo 12 1969 Second crewed Moon landing — pinpoint touchdown 163 m from Surveyor 3
  • Apollo 13 1970 The mission that turned a near-disaster into NASA's most celebrated rescue
  • Apollo 14 1971 Third lunar landing — Apollo 13's lost target reached; Shepard's golf shots
  • Apollo 15 1971 First J-mission — extended stay, first Lunar Roving Vehicle, "Genesis Rock"
  • Apollo 17 1972 Last crewed mission to the Moon (and the only Apollo with a trained geologist)
  • Apollo 16 1972 First Moon landing in the lunar highlands — Descartes; John Young's "lunar Grand Prix"
  • X-37B OTV-5 (USA-277) 2017 USA-277 — 780 days on orbit, KSC Shuttle Landing Facility
  • Inspiration4 2021 First all-civilian orbital spaceflight; raised $243M for St. Jude Children's Research Hospital
  • X-37B OTV-7 (USA-358, USSF-52) 2023 USA-358 / USSF-52 — 434 days on orbit, Vandenberg SFB
  • Polaris Dawn 2024 First commercial spacewalk; highest Earth orbit by humans since Apollo 17 (1408 km)

Fleet

  • Saturn V Sent humans to the Moon
  • Apollo CSM Block II Lunar-capable Command + Service Module
  • Apollo Lunar Module First crewed lunar lander; Eagle, Antares, Falcon...
  • Lunar Roving Vehicle First crewed rover; Apollo 15, 16, 17
  • Falcon 9 Block 5 Reusable booster reshaped commercial launch
  • X-37B OTV USSF reusable robotic spaceplane — multi-year classified orbital missions, the only US spaceplane operational after the Shuttle's retirement
  • Crew Dragon First commercial human-rated orbital spacecraft
  • Falcon Heavy Three-core super-heavy reusable booster

Launch site

  • Launch Complex 39B Apollo 10 + Skylab + 53 Shuttle flights; now NASA's SLS / Artemis pad
  • Cape Canaveral Launch Complex 36B (LC-36B) USAF Atlas-Centaur pad — Mariner 10, Pioneer 11 (1973), Pioneer Venus (1978); now Blue Origin New Glenn (leased 2015, flew 2025)
  • Launch Complex 34 (LC-34) NASA pad at Cape Canaveral (1961-68) — first Saturn I; Apollo 1 fire site 1967-01-27; Apollo 7 first crewed launch 1968-10-11
  • Woomera Range Complex Australia's first satellite, WRESAT (1967), and Britain's Black Arrow launch of Prospero (1971)
  • San Marco Platform Italian sea platform off Kenya that launched Uhuru, the first satellite dedicated to X-ray astronomy
  • Plesetsk Cosmodrome Russia's northern military cosmodrome — more launches than any other site
