Launch Complex 39B

Apollo 10 + Skylab + 53 Shuttle flights; now NASA's SLS / Artemis pad

Pad 39B carried Apollo 10 (the lunar dress rehearsal), Skylab 2/3/4 crew launches, and fifty-three Space Shuttle flights — the first of which was the ill-fated STS-51-L Challenger in January 1986. Rebuilt as a clean pad in the late 2000s — no permanent service tower, mobile launcher platform per mission — to host the next generation of vehicles. Artemis I lifted from 39B in November 2022; Artemis II and III will follow.

MISSIONS

  • Apollo 10 1969 Full lunar-landing dress rehearsal — LM descended to ~14.4 km altitude
  • Skylab 2 1973 First crewed Skylab visit — repaired the damaged workshop and saved the programme
  • Skylab 3 1973 Second Skylab crew — 59 days, deployed twin-pole solar shield to replace parasol
  • Skylab 4 1973 Longest Skylab stay — 84 days, observed Comet Kohoutek, disputed "Skylab strike" rest day
  • Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (ASTP) 1975 First international human spaceflight cooperation; first US-USSR rendezvous + docking
  • Artemis II 2026 First crewed flight beyond low-Earth orbit since Apollo 17 (1972) — and the mission that inspired this project
  • Artemis III 2027 First crewed test of the commercial lunar landers — Orion plans to dock with one or both in Earth orbit
  • Artemis IV 2028 First crewed lunar landing since Apollo 17 (1972)
  • Artemis V 2028 Second crewed landing of the Artemis era — the start of a lunar base

  • Saturn IB Lifted Apollo 7, Skylab crews, and ASTP
  • Skylab First U.S. space station
  • Soyuz 7K-OK First Soyuz generation; Komarov's fatal flight
  • SLS Block 1 Heavy lift for Artemis lunar program
  • Orion Crew vehicle for the Artemis lunar program

Launch site

  • Launch Complex 39A Launched Apollo 8, 9 and 11–17 (Apollo 9 in Earth orbit; Apollo 10 flew from 39B); today flies Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, and Crew Dragon
  • Cape Canaveral Launch Complex 36B (LC-36B) USAF Atlas-Centaur pad — Mariner 10, Pioneer 11 (1973), Pioneer Venus (1978); now Blue Origin New Glenn (leased 2015, flew 2025)
  • Launch Complex 34 (LC-34) NASA pad at Cape Canaveral (1961-68) — first Saturn I; Apollo 1 fire site 1967-01-27; Apollo 7 first crewed launch 1968-10-11
  • Plesetsk Site 43 Plesetsk's workhorse Soyuz pad at 62.9°N — the world's highest-latitude active orbital launch complex
  • Kourou ZLV (Vega launch complex) Kourou's first launch site: Europa II, Ariane 1 and Giotto on this ground, rebuilt in 2001 for Vega and Vega-C
  • Woomera Range Complex Australia's first satellite, WRESAT (1967), and Britain's Black Arrow launch of Prospero (1971)
