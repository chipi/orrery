Orion

Crew vehicle for the Artemis lunar program

The crew vehicle for NASA’s Artemis program, built jointly with ESA (the European Service Module is provided by Airbus). Designed to support up to four astronauts on multi-week missions to lunar orbit. Flew uncrewed on Artemis I in 2022. Flew its first crewed mission (Artemis II) in April 2026 with a four-person crew on a lunar flyby trajectory.

MISSIONS

  • Artemis II 2026 First crewed flight beyond low-Earth orbit since Apollo 17 (1972) — and the mission that inspired this project
  • Artemis III 2027 First crewed test of the commercial lunar landers — Orion plans to dock with one or both in Earth orbit
  • Artemis IV 2028 First crewed lunar landing since Apollo 17 (1972)
  • Artemis V 2028 Second crewed landing of the Artemis era — the start of a lunar base

Operating sites

  • Artemis IV Lunar South Pole (specific site TBD) Planned as the first crewed lunar landing since Apollo 17 in 1972 — a gap of more than 55 years, and the first ever near the south pole. The target is the water ice in permanently shadowed craters, which may one day supply drinking water, oxygen and rocket propellant. Artemis IV took this role in 2026, when NASA turned Artemis III into an Earth-orbit test; it is planned for early 2028.
