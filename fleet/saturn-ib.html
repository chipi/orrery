Saturn IB

Lifted Apollo 7, Skylab crews, and ASTP

Not every Apollo job needed the Moon rocket. The Saturn IB was the pragmatic option — a crew-rated lifter for the work that stayed in Earth orbit: Apollo 7's shakedown of the redesigned spacecraft, the ferry flights to Skylab, and the handshake-in-orbit of Apollo-Soyuz. Its first stage was famously a bundle of tanks borrowed from the earlier Redstone and Jupiter missiles, clustered together — proven parts doing a new job rather than a clean-sheet design. It's the least glamorous rocket in the program, and a quiet lesson in not overbuilding: match the machine to the mission.

Saturn IB is an orbital launcher built by Chrysler / Douglas / IBM in USA. Lifted Apollo 7, Skylab crews, and ASTP.

MISSIONS

  • Apollo 1 (AS-204) 1967 Fatal pre-launch pad fire 1967-01-27 killed Grissom + White + Chaffee
  • Apollo 7 1968 First crewed Apollo flight — Earth-orbit CSM shakedown
  • Skylab 2 1973 First crewed Skylab visit — repaired the damaged workshop and saved the programme
  • Skylab 3 1973 Second Skylab crew — 59 days, deployed twin-pole solar shield to replace parasol
  • Skylab 4 1973 Longest Skylab stay — 84 days, observed Comet Kohoutek, disputed "Skylab strike" rest day
  • Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (ASTP) 1975 First international human spaceflight cooperation; first US-USSR rendezvous + docking

Fleet

  • Apollo CSM (Block I) Original Apollo CSM design — never flew crewed; killed Apollo 1 crew on the pad 1967-01-27. Redesign produced the Block II flown 1968-1975
  • Launch Complex 34 (LC-34) NASA pad at Cape Canaveral (1961-68) — first Saturn I; Apollo 1 fire site 1967-01-27; Apollo 7 first crewed launch 1968-10-11
  • Apollo CSM Block II Lunar-capable Command + Service Module
  • Skylab First U.S. space station
  • Launch Complex 39B Apollo 10 + Skylab + 53 Shuttle flights; now NASA's SLS / Artemis pad
  • Soyuz 7K-OK First Soyuz generation; Komarov's fatal flight
