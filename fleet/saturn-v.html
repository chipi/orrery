Saturn V

Sent humans to the Moon

The Saturn V existed to solve one brutal piece of arithmetic: putting a crew on the Moon means throwing roughly 45 tonnes onto a path out of Earth's gravity, and in 1961 nothing on Earth could throw a fraction of that. So von Braun's Marshall team built the largest machine that had ever flown — five F-1 engines at its base, each the most powerful single-chamber rocket engine ever fired at the time, together burning some thirteen tonnes of propellant every second. Everything about it was sized by the destination, not by what looked reasonable. That it flew thirteen times and never once failed to reach space is the part that still doesn't quite seem possible.

The 110.6 m three-stage rocket that put 24 humans on a translunar trajectory and 12 of them on the lunar surface. Thirteen launches between 1967 and 1973, zero losses on ascent. The most powerful rocket to reach orbit for over five decades until Starship's Super Heavy surpassed it in 2024.

MISSIONS

  • Apollo 8 1968 First humans to leave low Earth orbit + orbit the Moon
  • Apollo 11 1969 First humans on another celestial body
  • Apollo 9 1969 First crewed LM flight — Earth-orbit LM separation + rendezvous tests
  • Apollo 10 1969 Full lunar-landing dress rehearsal — LM descended to ~14.4 km altitude
  • Apollo 12 1969 Second crewed Moon landing — pinpoint touchdown 163 m from Surveyor 3
  • Apollo 13 1970 The mission that turned a near-disaster into NASA's most celebrated rescue
  • Apollo 14 1971 Third lunar landing — Apollo 13's lost target reached; Shepard's golf shots
  • Apollo 15 1971 First J-mission — extended stay, first Lunar Roving Vehicle, "Genesis Rock"
  • Apollo 17 1972 Last crewed mission to the Moon (and the only Apollo with a trained geologist)
  • Apollo 16 1972 First Moon landing in the lunar highlands — Descartes; John Young's "lunar Grand Prix"

Engines

  • F-1 The most powerful single-chamber engine ever flown
  • J-2 The engine that sent Apollo to the Moon — twice

Fleet

  • Apollo CSM Block II Lunar-capable Command + Service Module
  • Launch Complex 39A Launched Apollo 8, 9 and 11–17 (Apollo 9 in Earth orbit; Apollo 10 flew from 39B); today flies Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, and Crew Dragon
  • Apollo Lunar Module First crewed lunar lander; Eagle, Antares, Falcon...
  • Launch Complex 39B Apollo 10 + Skylab + 53 Shuttle flights; now NASA's SLS / Artemis pad
  • Lunar Roving Vehicle First crewed rover; Apollo 15, 16, 17

Launcher

  • Saturn IB Lifted Apollo 7, Skylab crews, and ASTP
  • Titan II GLV Workhorse for Project Gemini
  • Atlas LV-3B Lifted Mercury astronauts to orbit
  • Atlas SLV-3D Centaur 1970s NASA outer-planet workhorse — Pioneer 10/11, Helios 1/2, Mariner 9/10, Pioneer Venus
  • Mercury-Redstone First US human-rated launcher — variant of the Army's Redstone IRBM. Launched Freedom 7 (1961-05-05) and Liberty Bell 7 (1961-07-21)
  • Space Shuttle stack Orbiter + ET + SRBs; 135 missions

You can see this in

  • Space Center Houston, Houston — A complete Saturn V of unflown flight stages, in Rocket Park
  • U.S. Space & Rocket Center, Huntsville, Alabama — SA-500D, the Saturn V dynamic test vehicle
  • Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex, Merritt Island, Florida — A complete Saturn V of real, unflown stages, at the Apollo/Saturn V Center
  • INFINITY Science Center, Pearlington, Mississippi — S-IC-15, an unflown Saturn V first stage
