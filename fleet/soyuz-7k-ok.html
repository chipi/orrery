Soyuz 7K-OK

First Soyuz generation; Komarov's fatal flight

The Soyuz 7K-OK was the first of the line — the original version of the spacecraft that would outlive them all. It introduced the three-module layout every Soyuz since has kept: a spherical orbital module to live and work in, a compact descent module to ride home in, and a service module for power and propulsion. It was also the version that killed Vladimir Komarov on its first crewed flight and, in an improved form, the three cosmonauts of Soyuz 11 — the hard early schooling that turned a rushed design into a reliable one. Crude and dangerous at the start, its basic architecture proved so sound that, refined over decades, it is still carrying crews to orbit today.

The first generation of Soyuz, with all the architectural choices that have endured for sixty years: orbital module sphere, descent module, service module with deployable solar arrays. Tragically introduced by Soyuz 1 (Komarov’s fatal flight, 1967).

MISSIONS
