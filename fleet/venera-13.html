Venera 13

First colour images of the Venusian surface — survived 127 minutes in 457 °C / 89-atm conditions

Soviet Venus lander pair (with Venera 14). Launched 1981-10-30 on a Proton-K / D-1. Venus arrival 1982-03-01: the 760 kg titanium descent capsule entered the atmosphere at 11.2 km/s, deployed a parachute through the upper haze, then jettisoned it at 47 km and free-fell through the lower atmosphere with a disc-brake for terminal descent. Surface touchdown at 7.5°S, 303°E (Phoebe Regio volcanic flank). First panoramic colour images of the Venusian surface; first X-ray fluorescence soil-composition analysis (basaltic, like terrestrial ocean floor); first acoustic recordings from another planet. Operated 127 minutes — far beyond the 32-minute design life — before silicon-electronic failure in the 457 °C / 89-atmosphere CO₂ environment.
