Vostok-K (8K72K)

Soviet R-7-derived launcher that put Gagarin in space — Vostok 1 (1961-04-12) and 5 more Vostok flights through 1963

The rocket that launched Gagarin was a weapon that changed careers. The Vostok-K was a human-rated version of Korolev's R-7 — the world's first intercontinental ballistic missile, built to throw a warhead across the planet, now asked to lift a person into orbit. It was the same lineage that had lofted Sputnik, and it is, astonishingly, the same lineage still flying: the Soyuz rocket that carries crews to the Space Station today is a direct descendant, which makes the R-7 family by far the most-launched rocket in history. Few machines built for war have had so long or so peaceful a second life.

Vostok-K (GRAU index 8K72K) was the human-rated derivative of Korolev's R-7 ICBM, with a Blok-E upper stage and crew-rated parameters. Three-stage liquid-propellant (LOX + kerosene), 38 metres tall, 287 tonnes lift-off mass. Launched all six crewed Vostok flights (1-6, 1961-1963) plus a handful of uncrewed precursors. Reliability evolved from early R-7 teething problems (3 of 6 first-stage failures before Gagarin) to perfect record across the crewed series. The Vostok-K design lineage continues today as the Soyuz-2 launcher (the only crew-capable Russian launch vehicle of 2026).

MISSIONS

  • Vostok 1 1961 First human in space; first crewed orbital flight
  • Vostok 2 1961 First day in space — 17 orbits over 25 hours
  • Vostok 3 1962 First dual-flight rendezvous — flew within ~6.5 km of Vostok 4
  • Vostok 4 1962 Second half of first dual flight — first communications between two crewed spacecraft
  • Vostok 5 1963 Longest solo Earth-orbital flight — 4 days 23 hours, 81 orbits
  • Vostok 6 1963 First woman in space — Valentina Tereshkova, 48 orbits

Engines

  • RD-107 / RD-108 Sputnik's engine — still flying humans 65 years later

Fleet

  • Vostok First crewed spacecraft (Gagarin, 1961)
  • Baikonur Site 1/5 (Gagarin's Start) Sputnik 1 (1957). Vostok 1 — Gagarin (1961). Five hundred and forty Soyuz launches from a single pad.

Launcher

  • R-7 / Vostok rocket Carried Sputnik and Gagarin to orbit
  • Voskhod (11A57) Vostok-K successor — more thrust for the heavier Voskhod capsule. Launched Voskhod 1 (1964) + Voskhod 2 (1965 Leonov EVA)
  • Soyuz (11A511) R-7-derived launcher for the first-generation Soyuz spacecraft — flew Soyuz 1 (1967) and Soyuz 11 (1971)
  • Proton-K The Soviet / Russian heavy-lift workhorse 1967-2012 — Salyut, Mir, Venera, Vega, Luna sample-return
  • N1 Soviet Moon rocket; four launches all failed
  • Soyuz-U Most-launched orbital rocket in history
