Vostok

First crewed spacecraft (Gagarin, 1961)

The Vostok spacecraft was the first machine to carry a human into space and bring one back, and its design is a study in doing the minimum that works. Reentry is the hardest part of coming home, so Vostok used the one shape that survives it from any angle — a sphere — which meant the automatic systems never had to point the craft precisely; it would tumble in and still come down heat-shield-heavy. The catch was that the sphere landed too hard for a person, so the cosmonaut ejected near the ground and parachuted separately, a fact hidden for years to protect the records. Crude, secretive, and completely effective, it flew six people to orbit and lost none — and its capsule-plus-service-module layout became the pattern for Soyuz, still flying today.

The spherical descent module that returned Yuri Gagarin from orbit on 1961-04-12 — the first human spaceflight. Six crewed flights between 1961 and 1963, including Valentina Tereshkova’s solo orbital mission as the first woman in space.

MISSIONS

  • Vostok 1 1961 First human in space; first crewed orbital flight
  • Vostok 2 1961 First day in space — 17 orbits over 25 hours
  • Vostok 3 1962 First dual-flight rendezvous — flew within ~6.5 km of Vostok 4
  • Vostok 4 1962 Second half of first dual flight — first communications between two crewed spacecraft
  • Vostok 5 1963 Longest solo Earth-orbital flight — 4 days 23 hours, 81 orbits
  • Vostok 6 1963 First woman in space — Valentina Tereshkova, 48 orbits
