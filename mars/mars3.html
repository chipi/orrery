Mars 3 lander

Approx. 45°S, 158°W (Ptolemaeus Crater)

First soft landing on Mars. The lander touched down successfully and began transmitting data — for roughly 14.5 seconds. A planet-wide dust storm at the time of arrival is the leading explanation for the silence that followed. Despite the brevity, Mars 3 proved a Martian soft landing was possible: a foundational engineering result built on by every Mars lander since.

First successful Mars EDL — proved soft-landing was a solved problem before American hardware tried it.

Mars 3 touched down in Terra Sirenum on December 2, 1971 — the first lander to survive Martian atmospheric entry and reach the surface, five days after sister Mars 2 crashed. Communications stopped after 14.5 seconds, returning a single partial degraded greyscale image fragment. The lander likely arrived in the middle of a planet-encircling dust storm. Mars 3's exact resting location is still debated; HiRISE imaged candidate hardware in 2013.

  1. Hardware — Soviet 4MS-class lander, first Mars touchdown of any kind
  2. Launch — May 28, 1971 — Proton-K from Baikonur
  3. Surface — Terra Sirenum — landed December 2, 1971
