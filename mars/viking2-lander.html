Viking 2 lander

Utopia Planitia

Operated 1,281 days at 48°N — the highest-latitude Mars surface mission until Phoenix. Detected the first frost on Mars in September 1977 — water ice confirmed — with an iconic high-resolution image captured in May 1979. The lander's biological experiments returned the same ambiguous results as Viking 1's: chemistry that mimicked metabolism, but without unambiguous biology.

Demonstrated Mars surface operations could survive multi-year northern-hemisphere seasonal cycles.

Viking 2 touched down in Utopia Planitia on September 3, 1976 — the second of the twin Viking landers, on the opposite side of Mars from Viking 1. Its 1,316-sol mission included the first observation of water-ice frost on Mars (during the polar winter), repeating Viking 1's biology experiments at a much colder, higher-latitude site. Killed in April 1980 by a battery failure.

  1. Hardware — Twin of Viking 1, identical instruments
  2. Surface — Utopia Planitia — landed September 3, 1976
  3. Science — Frost, dust storms, and the first Martian winter
