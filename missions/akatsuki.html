JAXA · 2010 · FLOWN

Akatsuki

Recovered from a 5-year solar-orbit hold to achieve Venus orbit on a backup attempt; the only Venus orbiter active in its final years of operation

AGENCY
Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency / Institute of Space and Astronautical Science
DEPARTURE
2010-05-20
ARRIVAL
2015-12-07
TRANSIT
2027 days
∆V
~1.2 km/s
VEHICLE
H-IIA 202
PAYLOAD
517 kg (incl. 196 kg propellant)

Akatsuki — "dawn" — was Japan's first Venus mission. Launched 2010-05-20 from Tanegashima on an H-IIA 202, paired with the IKAROS solar-sail demonstrator. The planned Venus orbit insertion 2010-12-07 failed when salt deposits blocked a check valve between the helium pressurant tank and the fuel tank; the engine, starved of fuel, ran oxidiser-rich and overheated, and the probe entered safe mode about 152 seconds into the burn. JAXA kept the spacecraft alive in solar orbit for five years, twelve correction maneuvers, until the next Venus encounter window. On 2015-12-07 a 1228-second burn using four attitude-control thrusters — never designed for orbital insertion — placed Akatsuki into a highly elliptical 400 × 440,000 km, 3°-inclination orbit. One of the most dramatic mission-saves in spaceflight history. Contact was lost in 2024; JAXA declared operations complete on 2025-09-18.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — H-IIA 202 from Tanegashima, paired with IKAROS solar sail.
  2. TCM-1 — First mid-course correction.
  3. VOI-1 FAILURE — 2010-12-07. OME throttle anomaly 152s into the burn; spacecraft sails past into solar orbit.
  4. SOLAR-ORBIT HOLD — 12 maneuvers over 5 years to maintain a Venus re-encounter trajectory.
  5. VOI-R2 SUCCESS — 2015-12-07. RCS-thruster 1228s burn into 400 × 440,000 km, 3° orbit.
  6. SCIENCE OPERATIONS — UV/IR cloud-feature tracking; super-rotation studies; bow-shaped gravity waves above Aphrodite Terra. Contact lost 2024; operations declared complete 2025-09-18.
