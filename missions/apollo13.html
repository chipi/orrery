Apollo 13

The mission that turned a near-disaster into NASA's most celebrated rescue

Apollo 13 never landed, and it is the mission everyone remembers. Two days out, an oxygen tank tore itself apart and turned a routine flight to the Fra Mauro highlands into a four-day problem of keeping three men alive in a dying spacecraft a quarter-million miles from home. What saved them was not heroics; it was preparation and a roomful of people working the problem in real time, improvising a way to breathe and a way home out of whatever was already bolted to the vehicle. NASA called it a successful failure. It is closer to the truth to say it is the flight that proved the agency was as good as it claimed to be.

Apollo 13 was meant to be the third crewed lunar landing, targeted at the Fra Mauro highlands. At 55 hours 54 minutes into the flight an oxygen tank in the Service Module ruptured, crippling the Command-Service Module's electrical and life-support systems. With landing scrubbed, the crew — James Lovell, Jack Swigert, and Fred Haise — powered down the CSM, moved into the Lunar Module Aquarius as a lifeboat, and used the LM's descent engine for a Pericynthion+2-hour burn that placed them on a fast free-return trajectory. They swung past the Moon at 254 km altitude on 1970-04-15 and splashed down safely in the South Pacific on 1970-04-17. The mission is studied today as a model of disciplined real-time engineering improvisation.

EVENTS

  1. SATURN V LAUNCH — Liftoff from KSC LC-39A. Center engine of S-II second stage shut down two minutes early — outer engines compensated.
  2. TLI — Trans-lunar injection burn by S-IVB; CSM + LM commit to cislunar trajectory.
  3. MCC-2 — Mid-course correction shifts the spacecraft off the free-return trajectory onto a Fra Mauro approach.
  4. OXYGEN TANK #2 RUPTURE — SM cryo-stir command initiates a short-circuit fire and tank rupture. "Houston, we've had a problem." Power and water dwindle; LM Aquarius becomes the lifeboat.
  5. FREE-RETURN BURN — 34-second LM descent-engine burn returns the spacecraft to a free-return trajectory.
  6. LUNAR FLYBY — Closest approach 254 km altitude above the far side of the Moon — at 400,171 km, the farthest humans had travelled from Earth (record held 1970–2026, surpassed by Artemis II in April 2026).
  7. PC+2 BURN — Pericynthion-plus-2-hour burn shortens the return-to-Earth time by ten hours.
  8. SPLASHDOWN — CSM re-entry and South Pacific splashdown, recovered by USS Iwo Jima.
