Apollo 15

First J-mission — extended stay, first Lunar Roving Vehicle, "Genesis Rock"

By Apollo 15 the race was long over, and the science took the wheel — literally. This was the first flight to carry the Lunar Rover, which turned a two-mile walking radius into a proper eighteen-mile field expedition, and it set down not on a safe flat plain but in a valley between a mountain range and a winding canyon. Scott and Irwin came home with the "Genesis Rock," a four-billion-year-old shard of the Moon's original crust that confirmed how it had formed. Apollo had stopped visiting the Moon and started studying it — and it kept getting better at that right up until it was cancelled.

1971-07-26

1971-07-30

4 days

PAYLOAD
48599 kg launched (CSM Endeavour (CSM-112) + LM Falcon (LM-10))

The first "J-mission" — extended-stay scientific Apollo with the new Lunar Roving Vehicle. The LRV gave Scott + Irwin a 27.9 km traverse range (vs the 3.5 km of walking-only Apollo 14), opening the dramatic Hadley-Apennine site at the foot of a 4.5-km mountain range and the edge of the sinuous Hadley Rille. Three EVAs totalling 18 h 35 m, 77 kg of samples returned, including the famous "Genesis Rock" (sample 15415) — a 4.1-billion-year-old anorthosite confirming the magma-ocean origin of the lunar highlands crust. Scott's televised "hammer-feather drop" verified Galileo's law of gravitation live. Worden in lunar orbit operated a SIM-bay full of remote-sensing instruments (mapping cameras, gamma-ray, X-ray, alpha-particle spectrometers) and performed the first deep-space EVA on the way home (~39-minute EVA at ~317 000 km from Earth to retrieve the film canisters). Apollo 15's recovery was marred by one of three parachutes failing to deploy — CM splashed down hard but crew was safe.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Saturn V (AS-510) from Kennedy Space Center LC-39A 1971-07-26 13:34 UTC.
  2. TLI — Trans-lunar injection.
  3. LOI — Lunar orbit insertion. SPS ∆v ~0.91 km/s.
  4. TOUCHDOWN — Falcon lands at Hadley-Apennine 1971-07-30 22:16 UTC — landing beside Hadley Rille at the foot of the Apennine mountains.
  5. FIRST LRV TRAVERSE — EVA-1: First Lunar Roving Vehicle deployment + 10.3 km traverse to Elbow Crater + the rim of Hadley Rille.
  6. GENESIS ROCK — EVA-2: At Spur Crater, Scott + Irwin recover sample 15415 — a 4.1-billion-year-old anorthosite from the original lunar crust.
  7. HAMMER-FEATHER DROP — EVA-3 / pre-ascent: Scott televises a hammer + feather dropped together; both hit the regolith simultaneously, verifying Galileo's 1589 prediction live.
  8. ASCENT — Falcon lifts off after 66 h 55 m on surface. 77 kg samples + first ascent televised from the LRV-mounted camera (operated remotely by Houston).
  9. TEI — Trans-Earth injection. SPS ∆v ~1.0 km/s.
  10. DEEP-SPACE EVA — Worden performs first-ever EVA in cislunar space (~39 min at ~317 000 km from Earth) to retrieve SIM-bay film canisters.
  11. SPLASHDOWN — CM splashed down in North Pacific 1971-08-07 20:46 UTC. One of three main parachutes failed to deploy fully; landing hard but safe. Recovery by USS Okinawa.
