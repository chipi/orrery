Beresheet

First privately-funded mission to reach the Moon; first Israeli lunar mission

AGENCY
SpaceIL (Israeli non-profit) + Israel Aerospace Industries
DEPARTURE
2019-02-22
ARRIVAL
2019-04-11
TRANSIT
48 days
∆V
~6 km/s (highly elliptical phasing orbits, not direct TLI)
VEHICLE
Falcon 9 Block 5 (rideshare with Nusantara Satu)
PAYLOAD
585 kg launch mass; first private spacecraft to reach lunar orbit; lost during powered descent ~150 m above the surface

SpaceIL's Beresheet (Hebrew: 'In the beginning') launched as a shared-ride passenger on a Falcon 9 in February 2019. Instead of a direct trans-lunar injection, the small lander performed weeks of perigee-raising phasing burns to climb from a parking orbit out to the Moon — a fuel-saving trajectory pioneered for low-budget missions. After successful lunar orbit insertion on 4 April 2019, an inertial measurement unit reset during descent caused the main engine to shut down. Beresheet crashed in Mare Serenitatis. SpaceIL committed to a successor (Beresheet 2) within days.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Falcon 9 rideshare from Cape Canaveral 2019-02-22.
  2. PHASING BURN — First of seven perigee raises to climb from LEO to lunar approach.
  3. TCM 1 — Trajectory correction during multi-orbit climb.
  4. TCM 2 — Second TCM. Apogee approaching lunar capture distance.
  5. LUNAR APPROACH — Final translunar burn — Beresheet on direct lunar trajectory.
  6. LUNAR ORBIT INSERTION — Capture into elliptical lunar orbit 2019-04-04.
  7. DESCENT ANOMALY — IMU reset during powered descent → engine shutdown → crash in Mare Serenitatis 2019-04-11.
