Chandrayaan-3

First soft-landing near the lunar south pole

AGENCY
Indian Space Research Organisation
DEPARTURE
2023-07-14
ARRIVAL
2023-08-23
TRANSIT
40 days
∆V
~3.5 km/s
VEHICLE
LVM3 M4 (formerly GSLV Mk III)
PAYLOAD
3900 kg launch mass; 1726 kg lander+rover

Following the loss of Chandrayaan-2's Vikram lander in 2019, ISRO's third lunar mission was deliberately conservative — focused on the lander rather than carrying an orbiter. Vikram touched down at 69.4° S latitude, the closest any spacecraft had come to a lunar pole at the time (IM-2 landed closer in 2025). The Pragyan rover made the first in-situ measurement of sulfur near the lunar south pole and took in-situ thermal measurements. India became the fourth nation to soft-land on the Moon, landing closer to a lunar pole than any spacecraft before it — a region where future water-ice mining may become possible.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — LVM3 M4 (formerly GSLV Mk III) from Sriharikota.
  2. LOI — Lunar orbit insertion (multiple Earth and lunar orbit-raising burns used).
  3. LANDER SEPARATION — Vikram lander separates from propulsion module.
  4. TOUCHDOWN — Vikram lands at Shiv Shakti Point near south pole 2023-08-23 12:33 UTC.
  5. PRAGYAN DEPLOYED — Rover rolls down ramp; begins surface science.
  6. LUNAR NIGHT — Lander/rover put into hibernation as lunar night begins.
