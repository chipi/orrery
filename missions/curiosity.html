Curiosity

First nuclear-powered Mars rover; first sky-crane landing

Curiosity is a nuclear-powered laboratory the size of a small car, and getting it down was the hard part. Too heavy for airbags, it was lowered onto Mars in 2012 by a rocket-powered sky crane in a sequence so risky its planners called it seven minutes of terror — and it worked on the first try. Since then it has drilled its way across Gale Crater and answered the question it was sent to ask: the crater once held a lake, with the chemistry a living thing could have used. Mars had been habitable. Curiosity is still driving, climbing a mountain of layered rock that reads like the planet’s own history book.

The flagship mission that defined modern Mars exploration. Curiosity is car-sized, plutonium-powered, and has driven 36+ km across Gale Crater since landing in 2012. Its instruments confirmed Mars once had habitable conditions — flowing water, complex organics, the chemistry life would need. The 'seven minutes of terror' EDL sequence, ending with a sky-crane lowering the rover on cables, is now NASA's standard for delivering large payloads to Mars surface.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Atlas V 541 lifts off from Cape Canaveral SLC-41.
  2. TMI BURN — Trans-Mars injection sends MSL onto a heliocentric Mars-bound trajectory.
  3. TCM-1 — First mid-course correction.
  4. TCM-3 — Third trajectory correction; refines the Gale Crater target.
  5. EDL — SEVEN MINUTES OF TERROR — Atmospheric entry, supersonic parachute, sky-crane retro-rockets, rover lowered on cables.
  6. TOUCHDOWN — Curiosity arrives at Aeolis Palus, Gale Crater, 2012-08-06 05:17:57 UTC.
  7. FIRST IMAGES — Hazcam returns first low-resolution image of the surface.
