Europa Clipper

First mission dedicated to characterising the habitability of an icy moon; largest spacecraft NASA has ever launched for a planetary mission (6065 kg, 100-m² solar arrays)

AGENCY
National Aeronautics and Space Administration / Jet Propulsion Laboratory / Applied Physics Laboratory
DEPARTURE
2024-10-14
ARRIVAL
2030-04-11
TRANSIT
2005 days
∆V
~3.5 km/s (with Mars + Earth gravity assists)
VEHICLE
Falcon Heavy
PAYLOAD
6065 kg (largest spacecraft NASA has ever launched for a planetary mission; 100-m² solar arrays for outer-system power)

Europa Clipper goes to Jupiter to study Europa — a 3,121-km icy moon believed to harbour a global liquid-water ocean beneath a 15-25 km ice shell, the most accessible potentially-habitable environment in the solar system. Launched 2024-10-14 on a Falcon Heavy at C3 41.7. A Mars gravity assist in March 2025 bent the trajectory toward Earth; an Earth gravity assist 2026-12 delivers the final push outward; arrives Jupiter 2030-04-11 for orbit insertion. The orbital tour executes 49 close Europa flybys over four years — passing within 25 km of Europa's surface on the closest passes, designed to map the entire moon while limiting the spacecraft's dose in Jupiter's brutal radiation belts. Instruments: REASON ice-penetrating radar, MASPEX mass spectrometer (samples plume material if any), E-THEMIS thermal imager (finds active geysers), magnetometer + plasma instruments confirm ocean depth from induced magnetic response.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Falcon Heavy from Kennedy LC-39A.
  2. MARS GRAVITY ASSIST — 2025-03-01. Bends trajectory toward Earth re-encounter.
  3. EARTH GRAVITY ASSIST — 2026-12-03. Final acceleration outbound to Jupiter.
  4. JUPITER APPROACH — 2030-04-11. Final approach to JOI.
  5. JUPITER ORBIT INSERTION — Orbit-insertion burn. 49 Europa flybys over 4 years follow.
