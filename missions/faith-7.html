Faith 7 / MA-9

Last solo American orbital flight; 22 orbits / 34 hours

Faith 7 closed Mercury, and did it the hard way. Gordon Cooper flew twenty-two orbits over a day and a half — longer than every previous Mercury flight combined — and then, in the final hours, his automatic control system failed. So he flew the reentry by hand, lining the capsule up against the horizon and the stars and firing his retros on a spoken count, and put it down very close to the target. It was the last time an American orbited the Earth alone, and a fitting last word for a program built by test pilots: when the automation quit, the pilot brought it home.

Gordon Cooper's 22-orbit flight — last Mercury mission and last solo American orbital flight ever (all subsequent US programmes flew multi-person crews). Duration 34 h 19 min — more than all previous Mercury flights combined. Cooper conducted Earth observation, photography, and ate in space. Late in the mission a power-supply failure forced manual control of all attitude + retro-fire — Cooper used the window's painted alignment marks against Earth's horizon for orientation, achieving the most accurate splashdown of any Mercury flight (about 6 km (4 mi) from the recovery ship USS Kearsarge). Capsule Faith 7 on display at Space Center Houston, TX.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Atlas LV-3B from Cape Canaveral LC-14 1963-05-15 13:04 UTC.
  2. ORBIT INSERT — Insertion into 161 × 267 km Earth orbit.
  3. POWER FAILURE — Power-supply failure; Cooper switches to manual attitude control + retro-fire — earned his "best stick" reputation.
  4. SPLASHDOWN — Pacific Ocean about 6 km (4 mi) from the recovery ship after 22 orbits / 34 h 19 min. Recovery by USS Kearsarge.
