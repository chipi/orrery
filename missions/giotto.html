Giotto

ESA's first deep-space mission; closest comet flyby ever attempted (Halley, 596 km, 1986); first close-up imaging of a cometary nucleus

AGENCY
European Space Agency (lead) — international consortium of European universities + institutes for the 10 onboard instruments
DEPARTURE
1985-07-02
ARRIVAL
1986-03-14
TRANSIT
255 days
∆V
~1.5 km/s (Halley intercept)
VEHICLE
Ariane 1
PAYLOAD
960 kg spin-stabilised drum-shaped probe with a layered Whipple-Kevlar dust shield (rear bumper kept the science instruments live through the 68 km/s relative-velocity dust storm of the Halley coma). 10 instruments — Halley Multicolour Camera, mass spectrometers, dust impact detectors, magnetometer, plasma instruments.

Giotto was Europe's pillar of the Halley armada — a spin-stabilised 960 kg probe wrapped in a layered Whipple-Kevlar dust shield. Launched 1985-07-02, it flew through Halley's coma at 68 km/s relative velocity on 1986-03-14 with closest approach 596 km, returning the first close-up images of any cometary nucleus (an irregular 15 × 7.2 × 7.2 km elongated body with concentrated dust jets). A dust impact ~7.6 seconds before closest approach destabilised the spin axis and killed the camera, but the other instruments kept transmitting through the coma. After hibernating in heliocentric orbit, Giotto reactivated 1990 + used Earth gravity assist to retarget to comet 26P/Grigg-Skjellerup, flying past it at 200 km on 1992-07-10 — the closest cometary flyby ever performed.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Ariane 1 lifts Giotto from Kourou — ESA's first deep-space mission.
  2. HALLEY FLYBY — 596 km closest approach to comet 1P/Halley; first close-up imagery of a cometary nucleus.
  3. EARTH FLYBY — Earth gravity assist retargets Giotto to comet 26P/Grigg-Skjellerup.
  4. GRIGG-SKJELLERUP FLYBY — 200 km closest approach — the closest cometary flyby on record.
