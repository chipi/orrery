Hayabusa

First asteroid sample ever returned to Earth (~1,500 grains of Itokawa dust, sub-milligram total)

AGENCY
Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency / Institute of Space and Astronautical Science
DEPARTURE
2003-05-09
ARRIVAL
2005-09-12
TRANSIT
857 days
∆V
~2.2 km/s (ion engines)
VEHICLE
M-V-5
PAYLOAD
510 kg (incl. 60 kg of xenon for four μ10 ion thrusters)

JAXA's Hayabusa (Japanese for 'falcon') flew the most heroic mission-save in spaceflight history. Launched 2003-05-09 on an M-V-5 with four μ10 ion thrusters — the first microwave-discharge ion engines ever flown. Arrived at 25143 Itokawa 2005-09-12 — a 540×270×210-m rubble-pile near-Earth asteroid. Two touchdowns (2005-11-20 + 25), both with sample-collection projectile failures, though regolith was kicked into the collection horn anyway. Then a hydrazine leak crippled attitude control, two of its three reaction wheels failed, communications lost for seven weeks, the Earth-return window slipped by three years. Engineers assembled a single working ion engine from the surviving halves of two damaged engines and nursed Hayabusa back to Earth. Sample capsule recovered at Woomera AUS 2010-06-13 returned ~1,500 grains of Itokawa dust (sub-milligram total) — confirming the asteroid's S-type composition and rubble-pile structure, and proving that asteroid sample return was possible.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — M-V-5 from Uchinoura Space Center.
  2. EARTH GRAVITY ASSIST — 2004-05-19.
  3. ITOKAWA ARRIVAL — 2005-09-12. Station-keep at 20-km gate position.
  4. TOUCHDOWNS — Two surface contacts 2005-11-20 + 25. Sample-collection projectiles fail to fire on command.
  5. RECOVERY ATTEMPT — Hydrazine leak, 2 reaction wheels dead, comms lost 7 weeks.
  6. ION ENGINE FRANKENSTEIN — One working engine assembled from surviving halves of two damaged ones.
  7. SAMPLE RETURN — 2010-06-13. ~1,500 grains of Itokawa dust (sub-milligram total) recovered at Woomera. First asteroid sample ever returned.
