InSight

First to detect marsquakes; first interplanetary launch from US West Coast

AGENCY
National Aeronautics and Space Administration / Jet Propulsion Laboratory
DEPARTURE
2018-05-05
ARRIVAL
2018-11-26
TRANSIT
205 days
∆V
~5.5 km/s
VEHICLE
Atlas V 401
PAYLOAD
694 kg at launch (358 kg lander)

InSight gave Mars its first stethoscope. Its seismometer, deployed by a robotic arm onto the regolith, recorded over 1,300 marsquakes — including a magnitude-5 in 2022, the largest ever detected on another planet. The data revealed Mars's interior structure: a 1,800-km-radius molten core, a thick crust, and surprising mantle layering. The mole heat-flow experiment failed when the regolith proved too cohesive for its self-hammering design. The mission ended when dust accumulation on solar panels reduced power below operational threshold.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Atlas V 401 from Vandenberg AFB — first interplanetary mission ever launched from the US West Coast.
  2. TCM-1 — First mid-course correction.
  3. EDL — Atmospheric entry, parachute, terminal-descent rocket landing at Elysium Planitia.
  4. TOUCHDOWN — Lander arrives 2018-11-26 19:54 UTC.
  5. SEISMOMETER DEPLOYED — Robotic arm places SEIS instrument on the surface; its wind shield was added in February 2019.
  6. MISSION END — Final low-power transmission 2022-12-15. Solar panels too dust-covered to recharge.
