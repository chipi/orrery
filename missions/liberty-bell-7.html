Liberty Bell 7 / MR-4

Second American suborbital flight; hatch malfunction lost the capsule (recovered 1999)

Liberty Bell 7 flew flawlessly and is remembered for the thirty seconds after it stopped flying. Gus Grissom's suborbital hop repeated Shepard's — proof the first flight hadn't been a fluke — but on the water the new explosive hatch fired on its own, the capsule flooded, and it sank to the floor of the Atlantic while Grissom nearly drowned in his filling suit. Whether he had bumped the trigger or it had gone off by itself was argued for the rest of his life and never settled. The flight's real lesson was quieter and more useful: the most dangerous part of a spaceflight isn't always the spaceflight.

PAYLOAD
Mercury capsule #11 (1832 kg); Gus Grissom

Gus Grissom's 15-min suborbital flight — second American in space, first Mercury flight with an explosive escape hatch. After splashdown the hatch fired prematurely, the capsule flooded, and Liberty Bell 7 sank in 4900 m of water; Grissom escaped but his suit took on water and he was nearly drowned during helicopter pickup. The cause of the hatch firing remained controversial for decades; Grissom maintained he had not bumped the firing pin. Recovered 1999-07-20 by an Oceaneering/Discovery Channel expedition, restored and now displayed at the Kansas Cosmosphere & Space Center, Hutchinson KS. The Tom Wolfe book / 1983 film The Right Stuff dramatised this flight + the hatch controversy.

EVENTS
