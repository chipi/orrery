Lucy

First mission to the Jupiter Trojans; visits 11 bodies across 8 asteroids and their satellites in a single 12-year trajectory — a record number of objects on separate solar orbits

AGENCY
National Aeronautics and Space Administration / Goddard Space Flight Center / Southwest Research Institute / Lockheed Martin
DEPARTURE
2021-10-16
ARRIVAL
2027-08-12
TRANSIT
2126 days
∆V
~6 km/s (with 3 Earth gravity assists)
VEHICLE
Atlas V 401
PAYLOAD
1550 kg (821 kg dry)

Lucy reaches for the L4 and L5 Lagrange clouds — populations of primordial asteroids leading and trailing Jupiter by 60°, believed to preserve material from the outer protoplanetary disc. Three Earth gravity assists (2022, 2024, 2030) shape a single trajectory that visits seven Trojan asteroids plus bonus main-belt targets. First asteroid encounter: 152830 Dinkinesh (2023-11-01, ~790 m), at which imaging revealed the contact-binary satellite Selam. Bonus main-belt: Donaldjohanson 2025-04. L4 swarm: Eurybates + moon Queta (2027-08), Polymele (2027-09), Leucus (2028-04), Orus (2028-11). After EGA-3 (2030-12), the trailing L5 swarm: Patroclus-Menoetius binary (2033-03) — the largest Trojan binary known, a fitting climax for the mission's namesake (Lucy the hominid fossil; Patroclus-Menoetius the two characters that fell together in the Iliad).

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Atlas V 401 from Cape Canaveral SLC-41.
  2. EGA-1 — First Earth gravity assist 2022-10-16.
  3. DINKINESH — 2023-11-01. First asteroid flyby — ~790 m main-belt body; satellite Selam (contact binary) discovered.
  4. DONALDJOHANSON — Main-belt bonus flyby 2025-04. C-type contact-binary asteroid about 8 km long.
  5. EURYBATES + QUETA — 2027-08. First Trojan encounter. 64-km C-type + 1-km moon.
  6. POLYMELE — 21-km P-type Trojan with unnamed satellite.
  7. LEUCUS — 35-km D-type Trojan, slow rotator (446 h).
  8. ORUS — 51-km D-type Trojan.
  9. EGA-3 — Final Earth assist 2030-12. Heads to L5 cloud.
  10. PATROCLUS-MENOETIUS — 2033-03. Mission climax: 113-km + 104-km binary Trojan.
