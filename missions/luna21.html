Luna 21 / Lunokhod 2

Second Soviet lunar rover; longest off-world drive (~39 km) for over 40 years

AGENCY
Soviet Academy of Sciences (continued by Roscosmos)
DEPARTURE
1973-01-08
ARRIVAL
1973-01-15
TRANSIT
7 days
∆V
~3 km/s
VEHICLE
Proton-K / D
PAYLOAD
5700 kg launch mass; 836 kg Lunokhod 2 rover (traversed 39 km — longest rover distance until 2014)

Luna 21 delivered Lunokhod 2, the Soviet Union's second crewed-controlled lunar rover, to Le Monnier crater on the eastern edge of Mare Serenitatis in January 1973. The rover was driven from a control room in Crimea by a five-person team and traversed approximately 39 km over four lunar days — a record off-world distance that held until NASA's Opportunity rover passed it on Mars in 2014. Lunokhod 2 returned 80,000 television frames and 86 panoramas before dust contamination of its solar arrays ended operations.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Proton-K from Baikonur 1973-01-08.
  2. TLI — Trans-lunar injection on the Blok-D upper stage.
  3. TCM — Mid-course correction during cruise.
  4. LANDING — Soft landing in Le Monnier crater 1973-01-15. Lunokhod 2 rolled off ramps a few hours later.
  5. ROVER ACTIVE — Five-person ground crew in Crimea began driving operations. Total traverse: ~39 km.
