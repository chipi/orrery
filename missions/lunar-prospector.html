Lunar Prospector

Neutron-spectrometer hydrogen finding at both lunar poles

AGENCY
National Aeronautics and Space Administration / Ames Research Center
DEPARTURE
1998-01-07
ARRIVAL
1998-01-11
TRANSIT
4 days
∆V
~4 km/s (TLI + LOI)
VEHICLE
Athena II
PAYLOAD
126 kg dry (296 kg at launch); carried 5 science instruments

The third NASA Discovery-class mission and the first NASA-managed dedicated lunar orbiter of the post-Apollo era. Launched 1998-01-07 on an Athena II from Cape Canaveral, Lunar Prospector was captured into an 11.6-hour lunar orbit on 1998-01-11 and lowered to its 100-km polar mapping orbit on 16 January. The 296-kg spinning spacecraft carried five instruments: a gamma-ray spectrometer for global crustal composition, a neutron spectrometer for hydrogen / water-ice detection at the poles, an alpha-particle spectrometer for radon gas (indirect volcanism detection), a magnetometer + electron reflectometer for crustal magnetism, and a Doppler gravity experiment. Feldman-team analysis of the neutron data (Feldman et al., Science 281 + 284) found sharp neutron-flux deficits over both lunar poles — consistent with 10–300 million tonnes of water ice (1998 estimate) in permanently shadowed polar craters. Mission ended 1999-07-31 with deliberate impact in Shoemaker crater hoping to release a detectable water-vapour plume; none was seen. The hydrogen finding stood until LCROSS and Chandrayaan-1 / M3 (2009) provided independent confirmation.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Athena II from Cape Canaveral SLC-46 1998-01-07 02:28 UTC.
  2. TLI — Trans-lunar injection.
  3. LOI — Lunar orbit insertion 1998-01-11 — captured into an 11.6-hour orbit; lowered to the 100 km polar mapping orbit on 16 January.
  4. POLAR HYDROGEN — Neutron-spectrometer detects sharp neutron-flux deficits over both lunar poles — strong hydrogen signal, consistent with polar water ice.
  5. SHOEMAKER IMPACT — Deliberate impact in Shoemaker crater 1999-07-31 09:52 UTC. No water-vapour plume detected from Earth.
