Mariner 9

First spacecraft to orbit another planet

AGENCY
National Aeronautics and Space Administration / Jet Propulsion Laboratory
DEPARTURE
1971-05-30
ARRIVAL
1971-11-14
TRANSIT
168 days
∆V
~1.6 km/s
VEHICLE
Atlas SLV-3C Centaur
PAYLOAD
998 kg (incl. 439 kg propellant)

Mariner 9 made every orbiter that followed possible. After a 15-minute MOI burn placed it into Mars orbit on 1971-11-14, the planet below was unrecognisable — a global dust storm had hidden everything except the four Tharsis volcano summits. JPL waited it out. Through January 1972 the dust settled and Mariner 9 returned 7,329 images covering ~85% of the Martian surface, revealing Olympus Mons (the largest volcano in the solar system), Valles Marineris (the canyon system that bears its name), dried river channels suggesting an ancient liquid era, and the first close-ups of Phobos and Deimos. The mission ended 1972-10-27 when attitude-control gas ran out; the spacecraft was expected to stay in orbit for decades; whether it has since re-entered is unknown.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Atlas SLV-3C / Centaur from Cape Canaveral LC-36B.
  2. TCM-1 — First mid-course correction.
  3. TCM-2 — Approach trim pin-targets the MOI periapsis.
  4. MOI — 15-min liquid-fuel main-engine burn places Mariner 9 into a 1398 × 17,917 km, 64.4° orbit — first ever orbit of another planet.
  5. DUST CLEARS — Global storm subsides; imaging begins.
  6. END OF MISSION — 1972-10-27. Attitude-control gas exhausted.
