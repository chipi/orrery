MAVEN

First mission dedicated to studying how Mars lost its atmosphere

AGENCY
National Aeronautics and Space Administration / Goddard Space Flight Center
DEPARTURE
2013-11-18
ARRIVAL
2014-09-22
TRANSIT
308 days
∆V
~3.5 km/s
VEHICLE
Atlas V 401
PAYLOAD
2454 kg

MAVEN — Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution — answers the question 'where did Mars's air go?' Its elliptical orbit dips into the upper atmosphere, sampling ions and neutrals as the solar wind strips them away. Findings: Mars loses about 100 g of atmosphere per second to space — over billions of years that's enough to explain the missing climate. The spacecraft also served as a critical relay for surface assets, including Curiosity and Perseverance, until it lost contact in December 2025; NASA declared the mission over in June 2026.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Atlas V 401 from Cape Canaveral SLC-41.
  2. TCM-1 — First trajectory correction.
  3. MARS ORBIT INSERTION — 33-minute main engine burn captures MAVEN into a 35-hour elliptical science orbit.
  4. FIRST SCIENCE — Begins atmosphere-sampling 'deep dip' campaigns to periapsis altitudes ~125 km.
