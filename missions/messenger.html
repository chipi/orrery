MESSENGER

First spacecraft to orbit Mercury; first complete surface map of the innermost planet

AGENCY
National Aeronautics and Space Administration / Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory
DEPARTURE
2004-08-03
ARRIVAL
2011-03-18
TRANSIT
2418 days
∆V
~0.9 km/s (Mercury orbit insertion)
VEHICLE
Delta II 7925H-9.5
PAYLOAD
Mercury orbiter; sunshade-protected (front-side 370 °C, spacecraft behind it ~20 °C); seven instruments including imaging, spectrometers, magnetometer, laser altimeter

MESSENGER (MErcury Surface, Space ENvironment, GEochemistry, and Ranging) used six gravity assists across seven years — Earth, Venus twice, Mercury three times — to slow into Mercury orbit on 2011-03-18. Sunshade-protected with the sunshade at 370 °C and the spacecraft behind it at about room temperature (~20 °C). Discoveries included water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters, previously unknown “hollows” geological features, and confirmation that Mercury's iron core occupies ~85 % of the planet's radius (the largest core-to-radius ratio of any planet in the solar system). Mission ended with a controlled impact onto Mercury’s surface 2015-04-30 after fuel ran out.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Delta II 7925H lifts MESSENGER on a six-flyby trajectory.
  2. EARTH GRAVITY ASSIST — First of six assists to slow the trajectory enough to enter Mercury orbit.
  3. MERCURY ORBIT INSERTION — Main-engine burn places MESSENGER into Mercury orbit after seven years of cruise.
