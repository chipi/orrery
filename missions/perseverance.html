Perseverance

First helicopter on another planet (Ingenuity); first sample caching on Mars

Perseverance went to Mars to look for the dead. Landed in 2021 on the floor of Jezero Crater — a basin holding the delta of a river that fed a lake billions of years ago, exactly the kind of place life would have left traces — it is the first rover built specifically to search for signs of ancient life, and to do something no mission had done: seal Martian rock into tubes and set them aside for a future spacecraft to carry home. It also delivered Ingenuity, the small helicopter that made the first powered flight on another planet. If the samples it is caching are ever returned, they will be the first pieces of another planet’s surface brought back to Earth.

Perseverance is the most ambitious Mars surface mission ever flown. The rover cached samples in tubes meant for Mars Sample Return, but NASA and ESA cancelled that mission in 2026, so the tubes have no planned retrieval mission. Ingenuity, the first helicopter on another planet, flew 72 missions before retiring with damaged rotors in January 2024 — proving controlled flight is possible in Mars's thin atmosphere. MOXIE, an oxygen-from-CO2 demonstrator, produced the first locally-made oxygen on another planet, a rehearsal for crewed Mars missions.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Atlas V 541 from Cape Canaveral SLC-41 — same vehicle class as Curiosity.
  2. TMI BURN — Trans-Mars injection.
  3. TCM-1 — First mid-course correction.
  4. EDL — Sky-crane delivers rover to Jezero Crater 2021-02-18 — site of an ancient river delta.
  5. TOUCHDOWN — Perseverance touches down at Jezero 20:55 UTC.
  6. INGENUITY FIRST FLIGHT — First powered controlled flight on another planet — 39.1 seconds at 3 m altitude.
  7. INGENUITY RETIRED — Rotor blade damaged on 72nd flight 2024-01-18; helicopter grounded, repurposed as a stationary weather and data station storing readings for future retrieval.
