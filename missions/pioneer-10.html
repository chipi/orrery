Pioneer 10

First spacecraft through the asteroid belt and the first to fly past Jupiter (1973-12-04)

Pioneer 10 went first, into two dangers nobody had measured. No spacecraft had crossed the asteroid belt — thought to be a shooting gallery of debris — or survived Jupiter’s radiation, believed lethal to electronics. Pioneer 10 did both: it crossed the belt unharmed (the belt, it turned out, is mostly empty), reached Jupiter in December 1973 as the first spacecraft ever to do so, and returned the first close images of the giant planet. Then it became the first human-made object aimed out of the solar system for good. Its value was proof — it turned the unknown hazards on the road to the outer planets into known ones, so the Voyagers could be built to face them.

Pioneer 10 was the trail-blazer — first to cross the asteroid belt (which we then learned was much less hazardous than predicted), first to image Jupiter at close range, first to reach a heliocentric distance greater than Pluto’s perihelion (1983). It carries the Pioneer plaque designed by Carl Sagan, Frank Drake, and Linda Salzman Sagan — humanity’s first deliberate interstellar message. Final successful contact 2003-01-23 from ~80 AU; the spacecraft now drifts toward the constellation Taurus, with an encounter near Aldebaran in roughly two million years.
