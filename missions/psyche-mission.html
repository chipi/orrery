Psyche

First mission to a metal-rich M-class asteroid; first interplanetary spacecraft to use Hall-effect thrusters

AGENCY
National Aeronautics and Space Administration / Jet Propulsion Laboratory / Arizona State University / Maxar Technologies
DEPARTURE
2023-10-13
ARRIVAL
2029-08-01
TRANSIT
2118 days
∆V
~6.6 km/s
VEHICLE
Falcon Heavy
PAYLOAD
2747 kg launch mass (1648 kg dry; 1085 kg xenon propellant)

Psyche reaches for the exposed nickel-iron core of a differentiated proto-planet — a 226-km metal body that may be the closest analogue to Earth's own core anywhere accessible. Launched 2023-10-13 on a Falcon Heavy from Kennedy LC-39A. Four SPT-140 Hall-effect ion thrusters spiral the trajectory outward; a Mars gravity assist in May 2026 (4,609 km closest approach) added the energy ion thrust alone couldn't deliver in time. Arrival planned 2029-08, followed by a 26-month four-orbit-phase descent (A → B → C → D) mapping the asteroid's gravity, magnetism, topography, and composition. If the metal-core hypothesis is right, Psyche will look unlike anything else we've visited — bare metal surface, iron-sulphide deposits, none of the rubble-pile chaos of typical asteroids.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Falcon Heavy from Kennedy LC-39A.
  2. ION CRUISE BEGINS — Four Hall-effect ion thrusters take over. Continuous low-thrust acceleration over 4+ years.
  3. MARS GRAVITY ASSIST — 2026-05-15. Closest approach 4,609 km — Mars flyby completed.
  4. PSYCHE ARRIVAL — Ion-thrust gravitational capture into 700-km orbit. 26-month mission, four orbit phases.
