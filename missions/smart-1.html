SMART-1

First European spacecraft to reach the Moon; first mission to reach the Moon under ion propulsion

AGENCY
European Space Agency
DEPARTURE
2003-09-27
ARRIVAL
2004-11-15
TRANSIT
415 days
∆V
~3.9 km/s (cumulative ion-drive over 13 months)
VEHICLE
Ariane 5G (shared payload)
PAYLOAD
367 kg launch mass; 1.2 kW solar-electric Hall-effect ion thruster

ESA's first lunar mission and the first European spacecraft to reach the Moon. SMART = Small Missions for Advanced Research in Technology — and the key technology was a Hall-effect solar-electric ion thruster (PPS-1350-G), at the time the largest such thruster ever flown. Launched 2003-09-27 as a shared payload on an Ariane 5 from Kourou, the 367-kg spacecraft used its 1.2-kW xenon ion drive over 13 months to slowly raise its Earth orbit, eventually captured by lunar gravity on 2004-11-15 — a 13-month low-thrust spiral rather than the usual days-long chemical transfer. Operated for about 22 months in a polar lunar orbit returning AMIE camera imagery, X-ray fluorescence elemental maps of the Moon (D-CIXS), and infrared mineral spectroscopy (SIR). Deliberately impacted Lacus Excellentiae (34.4° S, 46.2° W) on 2006-09-03 05:42 UTC — the impact flash was observed live from the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Ariane 5G from Kourou 2003-09-27 23:14 UTC — shared payload with Insat-3E + e-Bird.
  2. ION DRIVE START — PPS-1350-G Hall-effect ion thruster begins 13-month low-thrust spiral.
  3. LUNAR CAPTURE — Captured by lunar gravity 2004-11-15 — first European spacecraft at the Moon.
  4. D-CIXS MAPPING — D-CIXS X-ray fluorescence delivers elemental maps of the Moon.
  5. LACUS EXCELLENTIAE IMPACT — Deliberate impact at 34.4° S, 46.2° W on 2006-09-03 05:42 UTC. Impact flash observed from CFHT.
