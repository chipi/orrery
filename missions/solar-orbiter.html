Solar Orbiter

First mission designed to image the Sun's polar regions in detail; complements Parker Solar Probe's in-corona measurements with high-latitude remote sensing

AGENCY
European Space Agency (lead) / Airbus Defence and Space (spacecraft) / NASA (launch + 2 instruments: SoloHI and Heavy Ion Sensor)
DEPARTURE
2020-02-10
ARRIVAL
2020-06-15
TRANSIT
126 days
∆V
~3.5 km/s
VEHICLE
Atlas V 411
PAYLOAD
1800 kg (incl. 209 kg of instruments + heat shield)

ESA-led mission with a NASA launch and two instruments (SoloHI and the Heavy Ion Sensor). Where Parker Solar Probe goes closer than anyone has ever been, Solar Orbiter goes higher — using repeated Venus gravity assists over a decade to climb out of the ecliptic toward ~33° heliographic inclination. From that vantage point its instrument suite (PHI, EUI, SPICE, STIX, METIS, SoloHI + four in-situ packages) images the Sun's polar magnetic fields, traces solar-wind acceleration in real time, and links remote and in-situ measurements simultaneously. First perihelion 2020-06-15 at 0.51 AU. Perihelion P5 on 2022-10-12 at 0.29 AU (inside Mercury's perihelion).

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Atlas V 411 from Cape Canaveral SLC-41.
  2. TCM-1 — First mid-course correction.
  3. P1 — FIRST PERIHELION — 2020-06-15, 0.51 AU. Initial science begins.
  4. VGA-3 — 2022-09-04. Begins inclination climb out of the ecliptic.
  5. P5 — 0.29 AU — 2022-10-12, 0.29 AU. Inside Mercury's perihelion.
  6. POLAR IMAGING — Heliographic latitude exceeds 17° — first close-up polar magnetic field images.
