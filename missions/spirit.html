Spirit

First of the Mars Exploration Rover twins; longest-lived rover until passed by Opportunity

Spirit was built for ninety days and worked for six years. One of a pair of golf-cart-sized geologists landed in 2004, it was sent to read the rocks for evidence of a wetter Martian past — and it found it, uncovering minerals that only form in water and, in one place, silica deposits like those left by hot springs on Earth. It climbed hills no one had planned for it to reach and survived winters it was never designed to see, until a wheel failed and it bogged down in soft sand. It kept transmitting from where it was stuck until the Martian cold finally took it.

Spirit (MER-A) bounced 28 times across the floor of Gusev Crater on 2004-01-04 and rolled to a stop 250 m from its first impact. Designed for a 90-sol primary mission, it would survive 2,208 sols. Drove 7.7 km across Gusev's plains, climbed the Columbia Hills, and uncovered hematite-bearing rocks that signalled persistent ancient water. In 2009-04 a wheel broke through a sulphate crust at "Troy" and Spirit ground to a halt in soft soil; the team converted it to a stationary platform. Final transmission 2010-03-22; mission declared ended 2011-05-25. Twinned with Opportunity, which landed three weeks later on the opposite side of Mars.

EVENTS

  2. TCM-1 — First mid-course correction.
  3. TCM-4 — Final pre-EDL trim.
  4. EDL — Heat shield, parachute, retro-rockets, airbags — 6 minutes of hell into Gusev Crater.
  5. TOUCHDOWN — Bounced 28 times. Rolled to rest at 14.57°S, 175.47°E.
  6. STUCK AT TROY — Wheel breaks through sulphate crust; rover bogs down in soft soil. 2009-04.
