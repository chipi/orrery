Sputnik 1

First artificial satellite to orbit Earth — start of the Space Age

Sputnik 1 did almost nothing, and that was the point. No camera, no experiment worth the name — a radio, a battery, and a polished shell chosen so the world could watch the rocket that carried it. Its whole job was to reach orbit first and be heard doing it, and for twenty-one days it was: a beep on the shortwave that anyone on Earth could tune in, and that Washington could not switch off. The machine mattered far less than the fact of it — proof, broadcast to everyone at once, that the Soviet Union had beaten the United States to orbit and flew a rocket that could reach any city on the map. It burned up over the new year. The race it started ran all the way to the Moon.

Sputnik 8K71PS (R-7 Semyorka derivative)
PAYLOAD
83.6 kg polished aluminium-magnesium sphere, Ø 58 cm; two transmitters (20.005 MHz + 40.002 MHz), three silver-zinc batteries (1 W radio output), no instruments beyond a thermometer + pressure sensor

Korolev's polished aluminium sphere — 58 cm wide, 83.6 kg, two transmitters on 20.005 and 40.002 MHz — became the first human-made object to reach orbit on 1957-10-04 19:28 UTC. The 215 × 939 km, 65.1° trajectory beeped over America that night and again every 96 minutes, audible on shortwave to anyone who tuned in. Batteries depleted on 1957-10-26 (after 21 days, ~315 orbits); the satellite decayed and burned up on re-entry 1958-01-04 after roughly 1440 orbits. The flight was an OKB-1 side project: Korolev needed a lightweight payload for his still-developmental R-7 ICBM and pitched 'PS-1' (Prosteyshiy Sputnik — 'simplest satellite') to Khrushchev as a propaganda coup. It worked. The shock in Washington triggered ARPA (founded 1958-02), the National Aeronautics and Space Act (signed 1958-07), and Kennedy's Moon-landing pledge four years later. Sputnik 1 also returned the first orbital ionospheric and atmospheric-density data — engineers used the Doppler shift on the 20 / 40 MHz carriers to map air density at 215 km, work that fed into every subsequent re-entry calculation.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Sputnik 8K71PS lifts off from NIIP-5 Site 1 (later Baikonur, Gagarin's Start) 1957-10-04 19:28:34 UTC.
  2. ORBIT INSERT — Stage-2 cutoff + payload separation; orbit 215 × 939 km, 65.1° inclination, 96.2 min period.
  3. TRANSMITTERS SILENT — Silver-zinc batteries depleted 1957-10-26; the carriers go quiet after ~315 orbits.
  4. RE-ENTRY — Decayed on 1958-01-04 after ~1440 orbits; burned up over the Atlantic.
