Ulysses

Only spacecraft ever to enter a high-inclination heliocentric orbit; only direct measurements of the solar wind at all heliographic latitudes

AGENCY
European Space Agency (lead, spacecraft + 6 instruments) / National Aeronautics and Space Administration (launch + DSN + RTG power + 3 instruments)
DEPARTURE
1990-10-06
ARRIVAL
1992-02-08
TRANSIT
490 days
∆V
~15.4 km/s (Jupiter gravity assist did the heavy lifting)
VEHICLE
STS-41 Discovery + IUS + PAM-S
PAYLOAD
366 kg spin-stabilised, 9 instruments (magnetometer, solar wind, energetic particles, radio + plasma waves, dust, cosmic rays, gamma-ray burst detector, solar-wind composition, X-ray + gamma-ray bursts). Powered by a single GPHS-RTG.

Joint ESA / NASA mission. Launched 1990-10-06 from STS-41 Discovery + IUS + PAM-S upper stages, Ulysses used a Jupiter gravity assist on 1992-02-08 to deflect itself ~80° out of the ecliptic — the only way to reach a near-polar Sun-relative orbit from Earth's near-equatorial orbital plane. Three full polar orbits of the Sun over 18.5 years mapped the solar wind, heliospheric magnetic field, and energetic particles at all heliographic latitudes — direct measurements that revealed how solar-wind structure varies between equatorial slow-wind regions and high-latitude fast-wind streams from coronal holes. Mission terminated 2009-06-30 when thruster hydrazine froze in the lines. Hardware remains in heliocentric polar orbit.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — STS-41 Discovery deploys Ulysses + IUS / PAM-S upper stages.
  2. JUPITER FLYBY — Gravity assist deflects Ulysses ~80° out of the ecliptic into a polar Sun-relative orbit.
  3. FIRST SOUTH POLAR PASS — First-ever direct measurements of solar wind over a Sun pole.
  4. FIRST NORTH POLAR PASS — Completion of the first full solar polar orbit.
  5. MISSION END — Thruster lines freeze; final command issued 2009-06-30.
