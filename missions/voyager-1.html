Voyager 1

Most distant human-made object — currently ~170 AU from the Sun in interstellar space

Voyager 1 is the farthest thing humanity has ever made, and it got there almost by an accident of geometry. A close pass by Saturn’s giant moon Titan — chosen for the science — bent its path up and out of the plane of the planets and aimed it at the edge of the solar system, and it has been climbing ever since. Along the way it found active volcanoes on Io, the first ever seen off Earth, and in 1990 it turned back to take the Pale Blue Dot. In 2012 it became the first machine to cross into interstellar space. It is still transmitting, on fading nuclear power, from more than a hundred times the Earth’s distance from the Sun.

Launched 16 days after Voyager 2, Voyager 1 flew faster and arrived at Jupiter first (1979) and Saturn first (1980). Its Titan flyby bent the trajectory upward out of the ecliptic plane, ending the planetary tour but sending it on the most direct path to the heliopause — which it crossed on 2012-08-25, the first human-made object to enter interstellar space. From a heliocentric distance of ~6 billion km it took the Pale Blue Dot family portrait (1990-02-14), the photograph Carl Sagan wrote about. Both Voyagers carry the Golden Record as humanity’s calling card.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Titan IIIE / Centaur lifts Voyager 1 from Cape Canaveral.
  2. JUPITER CLOSEST APPROACH — Flyby at ~349,000 km; discovered Io volcanism, mapped Europa’s surface.
  3. SATURN + TITAN FLYBYS — Titan flyby bent trajectory out of the ecliptic plane — no further planetary encounter possible.
