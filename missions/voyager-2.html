Voyager 2

Only spacecraft to visit Uranus and Neptune; Grand Tour of four giant planets

Voyager 2 flew the tour that may never be flown again. Launched sixteen days before its twin on a slower, longer arc, it is the only spacecraft ever to visit Uranus and Neptune — two worlds that remain, decades later, known to us almost entirely from the few hours it spent racing past each one. It found a planet tipped on its side, the fastest winds in the solar system, and a frozen moon venting geysers into space, and then it too crossed into interstellar space, in 2018. Every serious plan to return to the ice giants still rests on what this single spacecraft saw on its way out.

The twin that kept going when budgets tightened, Voyager 2 carried the Grand Tour from Jupiter and Saturn out to Uranus and Neptune — still the only close-up images we have of the ice giants. Its Neptune encounter in 1989 revealed the Great Dark Spot and Triton's geysers. Today both Voyagers sail in interstellar space with a golden record as humanity's calling card. Orrery lists Neptune as the catalogue destination; the real path relied on multiple gravity assists.

EVENTS

  1. LAUNCH — Titan IIIE lifts Voyager 2 from Cape Canaveral on a Grand-Tour trajectory.
  2. NEPTUNE CLOSEST APPROACH — Flyby of Neptune and large moon Triton; last planetary encounter of the Grand Tour.
