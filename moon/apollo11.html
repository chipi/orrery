Apollo 11

Mare Tranquillitatis

The first humans on the Moon. Watched live by ~600 million people — roughly one in six people on Earth at the time. The laser retroreflector placed by Armstrong and Aldrin still receives pulses today, measuring the Earth-Moon distance to millimetre precision. Footprints in the airless regolith will survive unchanged for a million years.

First human landing — proved crewed planetary surface operations are possible. The capability ladder for Mars begins here.

Apollo 11 landed in Mare Tranquillitatis at 20:17 UTC on July 20, 1969 — the first crewed landing on another world. Armstrong and Aldrin spent 2h 31m outside the Lunar Module Eagle; Collins orbited in Columbia overhead. The crew returned 21.5 kg of lunar samples and left behind footprints, a retroreflector array (still used by Earth-based lasers), and a plaque: "We came in peace for all mankind".

  1. Hardware — Saturn V + Command Module Columbia + Lunar Module Eagle
  2. Launch — July 16, 1969 — Saturn V from Kennedy Space Center
  3. Surface — Mare Tranquillitatis — landed July 20, 1969
  4. Science — 21.5 kg of basalt samples, retroreflector array, seismometer
