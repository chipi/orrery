Apollo 17

Taurus-Littrow Valley

The last time humans visited the Moon. Gene Cernan was the last person to walk on its surface. Before climbing the ladder, he said: 'We leave as we came, and God willing, we shall return.' Harrison Schmitt, the only trained geologist on the Moon, collected samples that reshaped our understanding of lunar geology.

Descent stage · ALSEP · LRV rover · flag · plaque: 'Here Man completed his first explorations of the Moon. May the spirit of peace in which we came be reflected in the lives of all mankind.'

First trained geologist on a planetary surface — set the precedent that crewed Mars missions should include scientists, not just pilots.

Apollo 17 landed in the Taurus-Littrow Valley on December 11, 1972 — the last crewed lunar mission, and the only one with a professional geologist (Harrison Schmitt) on the surface. Cernan and Schmitt spent 22h outside across three EVAs, drove 35 km, and returned 110 kg of samples including the famous Orange Soil (volcanic glass beads from a 3.5 Ga fire fountain). Cernan's footprint is the most recent human mark on another world.
