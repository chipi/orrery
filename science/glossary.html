Glossary

The technical terms this site uses, each in one sentence. Every page uses the same word for each term.

ABDEFGIOPRST
apogee

The point of an orbit around Earth that is farthest from Earth.

Read more: Apsides — Perihelion, Aphelion, Perigee, Apogee

Used on
  • Special orbits — Molniya, Tundra, GTO, frozen
  • Polaris Dawn
  • Orbit Regimes — LEO, MEO, GEO, HEO
  • Molniya
  • Freedom 7 / MR-3
booster

A rocket that provides thrust at launch: a first stage, or a strap-on rocket at the side of the core.

Read more: Stages — why no rocket has ever flown single-stage to orbit

Used on
  • Keep the Rocket
  • Throttling and gimbaling — how rockets steer and how they hover
  • Starship
  • Space Shuttle
burn

A period of firing a rocket engine to change a spacecraft's velocity.

Read more: Oberth Effect

Used on
  • The Grammar of Delta-V
  • Orbit Insertion — LOI / MOI
  • Trans-X Injection — TLI / TMI / TVI / TJI
  • Hohmann Transfer
delta-v

Change in velocity, written Δv: how much a spacecraft can change its motion, the currency of every mission plan, in km/s.

Read more: ∆v as Mission Budget

Used on
  • The Grammar of Delta-V
  • Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation
  • ∆v Heatmap
  • The Wrong Treasure
descent

The way down to a surface, from orbit or from the atmosphere to touchdown.

Read more: EDL — Entry, Descent, Landing

Used on
  • The Seven Minutes
  • Sky-Crane
  • Terminal Velocity
  • Propulsive Landing
docking

Joining two spacecraft mechanically so that crew or cargo can pass between them.

Read more: Node Module

Used on
  • Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (ASTP)
  • Gemini
  • Gemini 8 / GT-8
  • Crewed-station design — Salyut to ISS to Tiangong
entry

A spacecraft's passage into a planet's atmosphere, where drag and heating slow it down.

Read more: EDL — Entry, Descent, Landing

Used on
  • Deorbit & the Re-entry Corridor
  • Space Shuttle Columbia
  • Soyuz 11
  • Vostok
  • Friendship 7 / MA-6
flyby

A close pass by a planet, moon or other body without going into orbit around it or landing.

Read more: Mission Types — Flyby, Orbiter, Lander, Rover, Sample Return

Used on
  • Giotto
  • BepiColombo
  • Voyager 1
  • Mariner 10
  • New Horizons
gravity assist

Using a close pass of a planet to change a spacecraft's speed and direction without spending propellant.

Read more: Gravity Assist

Used on
  • BepiColombo
  • Europa Clipper
  • JUICE
  • Mariner 10
inclination

The tilt of an orbit's plane against a reference plane, such as Earth's equator or the ecliptic, in degrees.

Read more: Inclination

Used on
  • Sun-synchronous + polar orbits — the Earth-observation backbone
  • Special orbits — Molniya, Tundra, GTO, frozen
  • Molniya
  • Cislunar orbits — DRO, NRHO, halo, lunar frozen
orbit

The closed path a body follows around another under gravity — an ellipse in the simplest case.

Read more: Keplerian Orbit

Used on
  • Cislunar orbits — DRO, NRHO, halo, lunar frozen
  • Special orbits — Molniya, Tundra, GTO, frozen
  • Commercial Stations
  • The Exits We've Always Known
  • Keep the Rocket
orbit insertion

The engine burn that slows a spacecraft enough to be captured into orbit around its destination.

Read more: Orbit Insertion — LOI / MOI

Used on
  • JUICE
  • MESSENGER
  • NEAR Shoemaker
  • Europa Clipper
payload

What a rocket carries for the mission: satellites, a crewed spacecraft, cargo or instruments.

Used on
  • Reading Viability
  • Energia
  • X-37B OTV-1 (USA-212)
  • X-37B OTV-2 (USA-226)
  • Blue Moon MK1
perigee

The point of an orbit around Earth that is closest to Earth.

Read more: Apsides — Perihelion, Aphelion, Perigee, Apogee

Used on
  • Special orbits — Molniya, Tundra, GTO, frozen
  • End-of-life disposal — graveyard orbits, controlled deorbit, the 25-year rule

What a rocket engine uses up to make thrust: usually a fuel and an oxidizer together.

rendezvous

Bringing two spacecraft to the same place at the same speed so that they fly together — usually the step before docking.

Used on
  • Apollo 9
  • Gemini 6A / GT-6A
  • Gemini 7 / GT-7
  • Gemini
  • Gemini
specific impulse

How efficiently an engine uses its propellant, in seconds: the higher it is, the more Δv from the same propellant.

Read more: Specific Impulse (Isp)

Used on
  • The Exits We've Always Known
  • The Honest Arithmetic
  • The Grammar of Delta-V
  • Engine types — chemical, electric, nuclear
  • Fusion Propulsion — the star we cannot yet light
stage

A section of a rocket with its own engines and propellant, dropped when its propellant is used up.

Read more: Stages — why no rocket has ever flown single-stage to orbit

The force a rocket engine produces to push the vehicle forward, measured in newtons.

The path a spacecraft follows through space, open or closed — for example the arc from Earth to Mars.
