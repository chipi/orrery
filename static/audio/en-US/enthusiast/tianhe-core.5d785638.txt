Tianhe — the core module of Tiangong — launched on April 29th, 2021. Long March 5B rocket, Wenchang launch site, Hainan Island. Tianhe carries the station's life support, attitude control, propulsion, three crew sleeping quarters, the main galley, and the forward docking ports. Pressurized volume around fifty cubic meters — about half the volume of Zvezda, the analogous module at the back of the ISS. Same function as Zvezda. Different generation. Different agency. Different rocket. The first crew, Shenzhou 12, arrived in June 2021 — six weeks after Tianhe reached orbit. Three astronauts. Three months. Since then, Tiangong has been continuously occupied. The first experimental lab — Wentian , meaning "quest for the heavens" — joined Tianhe in July 2022. The second lab — Mengtian , "dreaming of the heavens" — joined in October 2022. Total assembly time, first module to fully crewed and fully assembled: seventeen months. Compare to the International Space Station, which took twelve years and forty-two flights, across an entire decade of Space Shuttle and Soyuz launches. Tiangong is smaller — about one sixth the ISS's mass. But it was built to a tighter timeline by a single agency on a single rocket family, optimized end-to-end for this one program. Look at the T-shape on screen. Tianhe is the upright. Wentian and Mengtian are the crossbar. Four solar arrays — two on each lab — provide about a hundred kilowatts. Three Chinese astronauts live and work inside as you listen.
