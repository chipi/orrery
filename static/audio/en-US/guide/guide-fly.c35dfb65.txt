This view is called a mission arc . You picked a mission — say, the transfer from Earth to Mars — and the screen shows you the actual trajectory the spacecraft will fly, in heliocentric coordinates, against the orbits of the planets. Look at the arc. It is not a straight line. It cannot be a straight line. When the spacecraft leaves Earth, it leaves with Earth's velocity around the Sun — about thirty kilometers per second — plus whatever the rocket adds on top. That total velocity puts the spacecraft on a Keplerian ellipse around the Sun. The ellipse's perihelion sits at Earth's distance. Its aphelion reaches out to wherever it's going — Mars in this case. Press play. Watch the spacecraft slide along the arc. Watch Earth and Mars move at the same time. Notice how the spacecraft's arrival has to be timed against Mars's own motion — Mars is moving while you're flying. If you leave a month too late, Mars will have moved on, and your arc — which is set by physics, not by you — will arrive at empty space. This is what the porkchop plot on the Plan screen is computing. For every possible launch date and arrival date, what does the arc cost in energy, and does the geometry line up? Some missions add a cislunar view — switchable from the toolbar. Cislunar is Earth-centered, not Sun-centered. It shows you what an Apollo crew or an Artemis crew sees: Earth in the middle, the Moon orbiting around, the spacecraft riding a trans-lunar injection arc to lunar orbit and back. Heliocentric arcs and cislunar arcs are two different geometries — same spacecraft, different reference frame. Use whichever makes the mission make sense. Below the view, the timeline lists the mission's phases — parking orbit, trans-Mars injection, cruise, mid-course corrections, arrival. Click any phase chip. The animation jumps to that moment in the mission. Pause it. Read the numbers. Restart and watch the next phase begin. Every mission you can fly here actually flew, or is scheduled to fly. The arc is real. The clock is real. Only the speed has been adjusted, so a seven-month transfer fits on your screen in seventy seconds.
