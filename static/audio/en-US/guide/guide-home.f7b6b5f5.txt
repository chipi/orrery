Welcome. An orrery is a mechanical model of the solar system — brass, wood, and clockwork — built in the eighteenth century to show how the planets move around the Sun. This Orrery is the digital version of that idea. Real planetary positions, real orbital math, real missions — drawn on a screen instead of cast in metal. Look at the cards on this page. Each one takes you to a different part of the story. Explore opens the whole solar system — eight planets in their orbits, real velocities, real distances. Earth , Moon , and Mars are the places we know best — each gets its own map with every spacecraft humanity has ever sent there. ISS and Tiangong are the two space stations currently being lived in — pickable, rotatable, module by module. Missions is the catalogue of forty-two flown and planned missions, sortable and filterable. Fleet shows the hardware itself — every rocket, every rover, every spacecraft, lined up by builder and era. Science is the in-app encyclopedia — every number you see anywhere else, with the physics that produced it. Plan and Fly let you design a transfer trajectory — Earth to Mars, for instance — and watch it play out in heliocentric scale. Nothing on this site is made up. Every mission has a launch date, a crew list, a destination, and a citation. Every image comes from a named agency with a named license. Every link to read further points to a primary source, not an aggregator. This isn't a textbook. It's a working model of where humans have been in space — and what we know now because someone went there. Take any card. Take the tour. When you find something that doesn't make sense, the tiny question-mark chips open the Science encyclopedia at the right page. When you find something beautiful, look for the share button — every view in Orrery has a URL you can send to someone. Welcome to the orrery. Pick a card.
