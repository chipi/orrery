Every other screen in this Orrery refers to physics. Hohmann transfers. Lambert solutions. Vis-viva. Free-return geometry. Tsiolkovsky's equation. C three. Delta-V. V-infinity. This screen is where those terms get explained — with diagrams, with worked-out math, and with the missions that use them. The encyclopedia is organized into ten tabs across the top. Orbits. Maneuvers. Propulsion. Re-entry. Solar System. Stars. Astrophysics. Instruments. Life support. Operations. Each tab opens to a list of sections — eighty-five sections in total. Click any section card. The full article opens with worked math rendered by KaTeX, hand-drawn SVG diagrams, and links to read further. Where a section explains a phenomenon you can see in action elsewhere in Orrery, a chip at the top of the article links straight to it — Hohmann transfer to the Fly screen, free-return to the Plan screen, vis-viva to the Explore screen with the orbital-velocity overlay turned on. Notice the small question-mark chips throughout the rest of the site. Each one is a hyperlink directly into a specific Science section. Whenever you see one and don't know the term, click it. The encyclopedia opens at the right page. Try the search. Press Cmd-K — or Ctrl-K on a Windows or Linux keyboard. A search dialog opens. Type anything. The full text of all eighty-five sections is searchable, and results jump you directly to the matching paragraph. Seventy-one diagrams are hand-drawn SVG. Each one was authored from a primary source — a textbook, a paper, a NASA technical memo — and the source files for the SVGs are committed to the same repository as the site itself. Click any diagram for the credit and the source. Every formula has a citation. Every constant has a reference. Every diagram has an author and a license. This isn't a wiki.
