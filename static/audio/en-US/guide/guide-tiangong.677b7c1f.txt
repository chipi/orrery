The T-shape in front of you is Tiangong — in Chinese, 天宫, Heavenly Palace . It is the second continuously-crewed space station ever flown. It is operated by the China Manned Space Engineering Office, and it has been continuously occupied since June 2022. Drag to rotate. You'll see three modules. The core of the T is Tianhe — meaning Harmony of the Heavens — launched in April 2021 on a Long March 5B rocket. Tianhe holds the crew quarters, the life-support systems, the control cabin, and the docking ports. Click Wentian — the arm to one side. Wentian — Quest for the Heavens — is the first experimental laboratory, launched in July 2022. It houses life-science experiments and the airlock used for spacewalks. Click Mengtian — the arm to the other side. Mengtian — Dreaming of the Heavens — is the second laboratory, launched in October 2022. It carries physics experiments and a cold-atom clock for fundamental research. Look at the solar arrays. Four large panels, two at each lab module's far end. Together they produce about one hundred kilowatts of electrical power. Look at the docking ports on the front of Tianhe. Crews arrive on Shenzhou spacecraft — the Chinese crewed vehicle, derived in part from Soyuz but with a domestically developed re-entry capsule. Cargo arrives on Tianzhou ships — the Chinese resupply vehicles. Tiangong was assembled in seventeen months. By comparison, the International Space Station was assembled over twelve years. Tiangong is smaller — about a sixth the mass of the ISS — but it was built on a much tighter timeline by a single agency. Click any module. The right panel opens with its builder, launch vehicle, launch date, and pressurized volume. Three modules. One agency. Continuously crewed for four years. Most people who ask "is there anyone else in space" don't yet know to include the answer: yes, also here.
